# How We Damage the Liver

Hello everyone. Today we will talk about a topic: how we damage our liver. We should know that the harm done to the liver has unthinkable consequences. The liver is one of the most important organs in our body, and it has rendered great service to our health. Yet more and more of our bad habits and the various pressures of modern life do not make us care for the liver in daily life. On the contrary, they may make us do many things that harm it, such as drinking too much, staying up late, sitting for long periods, and an unhealthy diet. How exactly do these bad habits harm the liver?

First let us talk about drinking too much. We see that many people always have various reasons for drinking, such as improving circulation. But from a health point of view, drinking too much is extremely harmful to the liver. Medical experts have found through research that there is a component in the liver called alcohol dehydrogenase. What does it do? It oxidizes ethanol, that is, alcohol, into acetaldehyde. Acetaldehyde is harmful to the body, but under the catalysis of another enzyme, acetaldehyde dehydrogenase, it is quickly converted into harmless acetic acid. What is acetic acid? You can think of it as the component in vinegar. Acetic acid is about the only substance produced after alcohol enters the body that has a little nutrition. What does it do? At least it can provide the body a little heat. But do not forget that the body's speed of metabolizing alcohol is very limited. When you drink too much, more and more acetaldehyde gathers in the body, especially in the liver and brain. Why? Because acetaldehyde dehydrogenase cannot convert acetaldehyde that fast. Its working speed cannot keep up with the speed at which alcohol gathers, and the body easily develops the symptoms of alcohol poisoning. Understanding this, you may find it terrifying. Why? Because drinking consumes and depletes our acetaldehyde dehydrogenase and alcohol dehydrogenase. When these two substances become fewer and fewer, eventually no one is left to do the job of breaking down alcohol. One day the alcohol will directly poison your liver cells. Once it becomes toxic, although your liver is still metabolizing, it is now metabolizing while sick. What is metabolizing while sick? It is working while ill. If a person works while ill, will his health not decline faster? It is the same with the liver. Once it works while ill, it directly triggers liver damage. Some people do not quite understand what liver damage is. Alcoholic liver is liver damage.

Next let us talk about staying up late and sitting for long periods. Nowadays, because of work, more and more people become night owls and the immobile tribe, and this group keeps growing. No matter how much you can stay up, and no matter how good your constitution is, the big data overall tells us that this group's physical condition is much worse than those who go to bed early and get up early and love to exercise. So they easily become the target of disease. Take staying up late, for example. According to the meridian circulation theory of traditional Chinese medicine, what happens between 11 p.m. and 1 a.m. every night? It is the time when the qi and blood of the liver and gallbladder meridians are most vigorous, the best time to nourish liver blood, and also the time the liver begins to detoxify. But for the liver to detoxify, you need to be asleep. So people who often stay up late have a liver that does not detoxify. Not only does it not detoxify, it does not neutralize poison either. It is even more likely to make your secretion of hormones like adrenaline higher than ordinary people's. Think about it: when you stay up late, who stays up for you? It is adrenaline, right? So this further burdens the liver and kidneys, and at least triggers a series of uncomfortable symptoms. Now sitting for long periods. People today generally think that sitting for long periods makes the body gain weight, but they do not know that sitting still also harms the liver. This is because once a person sits still for a long time, the flow of qi and blood through the body easily becomes disordered. Think about your car. If you leave it sitting for half a month, and it is cold, even starting it becomes a problem. Sitting still for a long time is the same principle. Problems appear all over your body, and over time it easily triggers high blood pressure, disordered digestive function, and in women, irregular menstruation. Many people, after sitting for a long time, feel uncomfortable all over. Where is it uncomfortable? There does not seem to be one specific spot. It is just that the whole body feels off. Let me tell you, this is the discomfort of stagnant liver qi. In addition, sitting for a long time plus an irregular life makes the liver gain weight first. What does it mean for the liver to gain weight? Fatty liver arrives.

Now let us talk about emotions. In life we inevitably meet times of bad mood. If these emotions are not released in time, they seriously affect the liver's blood. We know one of the liver's functions is to ensure the free flow of qi. If you have emotional stress, your liver qi is certainly [[stagnant liver qi|肝气郁结, a traditional Chinese medicine concept meaning the liver's qi is blocked and not flowing freely. It is associated with symptoms like irritability, sighing, chest tightness, and digestive upset.|https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/traditional-chinese-medicine-what-you-need-to-know]]. Once the liver qi stagnates, is chronic disease far behind? How does stagnant liver qi show itself? For example, unexplained stomach pain, diarrhea, headache, chest tightness, breast hyperplasia, uterine fibroids, high blood pressure, and so on. Sometimes we wonder how these diseases found us. At the root, they are all related to stagnant liver qi. I have a friend from Henan. He has two children and is in his fifties. A few days ago he finished a major surgery and told us he could finally get out of bed. It was because of long-term emotional depression that he damaged his liver, and the damaged liver triggered the diseases I mentioned. So how do these diseases come about? Are they not related to damaging the liver? In the earlier lessons we said the liver has no nerves. If you hurt it, it will not cry out, and you will not feel it. So only by keeping a happy mood can we avoid damaging the liver.

Next, let us talk about an unhealthy diet. We know that all the food we eat passes through the liver for detoxification and metabolism to supply the body. If we always eat carelessly, or always eat greasy food, we increase the burden on the liver. For example, the many additives in processed food all add to the liver's burden. Many people today like spicy food. Spicy food irritates the stomach and intestinal lining, causing more stomach acid secretion, and it can also cause the intestine to develop a leaky gut. You can look for our other courses to understand the harm of leaky gut to the body. Also, if we always eat greasy food, it leads to excess fat that deposits in the liver and increases its burden, easily leading to fatty liver, and finally liver damage. In addition, overusing the eyes and taking some medicines also increase the burden on the liver. Because the liver opens into the eyes, overusing the eyes eventually damages the liver, or at least lowers liver function, and lowered liver function leads to a series of immune problems.

In fact, all the problems I just discussed ultimately come from the bad habits of our daily life, from not realizing the harm of these habits, which brings lasting harm to the liver and health. If we let these habits continue, the liver may, under our step-by-step abuse, develop a series of serious problems: alcoholic liver, fatty liver, cirrhosis, and even liver cancer. Let us also talk about alcoholic liver. An expert once said that the incidence of alcoholic liver is positively related to the sales of alcohol. The better alcohol sells, the higher the incidence of alcoholic liver. It is no longer an invisible killer. It is a visible killer right beside us. We all know the liver's main functions are metabolism, detoxification, and defense, and alcohol only increases the liver's metabolic burden, causing a disorder in the liver cells' breakdown and metabolism of fatty acids, directly triggering fatty liver. Over time it further affects the liver cells, causing them to die directly. If they do not die, they degenerate. The result of degeneration is that the synthesis of collagen inside the liver increases, and making collagen is not the liver's job. Think about it: if collagen synthesis inside the liver increases, the whole liver becomes fibrotic. Continuous collagen synthesis brings continuous tissue proliferation, and finally the liver dies completely. In the past we joked that fatty liver was a disease of the rich. Today we can no longer say that. It is a disease of the poor in mind, of people without a health concept.

In a normal person, every hundred grams of liver tissue contains four to five grams of lipids, of which phospholipids make up more than fifty percent, triglycerides twenty percent, fatty acids twenty percent, and cholesterol seven percent, with the rest being cholesterol esters. But when the lipids gathered inside the liver cells exceed five percent of the liver's weight, fatty liver has actually already occurred. If you leave it alone, the next step is [[cirrhosis|肝硬化, the late stage of liver scarring. Long-term damage replaces healthy liver tissue with scar tissue that gradually stops the liver from working.|https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/cirrhosis]]. What is cirrhosis? It is the fibrotic change of the liver cells caused by long-term repeated damage to the liver. In the early stage, because the liver still has compensatory function, that is, it can still do some work, the symptoms are mild, such as loss of appetite, fatigue, abdominal bloating, and discomfort in the upper abdomen, and these symptoms are easily ignored. In the late stage there are often symptoms like ascites and upper gastrointestinal bleeding. Cirrhosis not only causes liver ascites, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, and massive gastrointestinal bleeding, but also triggers hepatic encephalopathy, also called hepatic coma, which refers to the disturbance of consciousness, abnormal behavior, and even coma caused by severe liver disease. It can also trigger hepatorenal syndrome, including kidney failure. These diseases basically cannot be cured. They are all complications after damaging the liver, and they are basically the main cause of death in late-stage cirrhosis patients. In the past, someone with cirrhosis could generally live only a few years. Now, with medical progress, most cirrhosis patients still have a good chance of returning to social life, but the best period for maintenance has passed, and the quality of life has declined greatly and cannot be recovered.

So should we start paying attention to liver health from now on, from this very moment? Liver disease and liver cancer are almost killers of life. The liver is the largest solid organ in the human body, and it carries all kinds of important metabolic functions. Once a malignant tumor appears in the liver, it causes life-threatening consequences. Because the liver has a very rich blood flow, and it is closely connected to the body's important structures, such as the inferior vena cava, the portal vein, and the biliary system. So why is treating liver disease extremely difficult? Because it is connected to other parts of the body, and treating the liver interferes with other tissues. Now let us talk about malignant tumors. Malignant tumors of the liver are generally divided into two kinds, primary and secondary. Primary tumors originate in the epithelial tissue of the liver, and they are dangerous malignant tumors with a high incidence in our country. What is a secondary tumor? It is when malignant tumors originating in multiple organs of the whole body finally invade the liver. Generally the malignant tumors of organs like the stomach, biliary tract, pancreas, colon, rectum, ovary, uterus, lung, and breast metastasize to the liver. We know a characteristic of the liver: it keeps working until the very last moment before it stops, and it does not cry out in pain. So by the time liver cancer is found, it is often already late stage. In fact, in our country, liver problems have become a longstanding hard problem. Our lifelong health depends on the organs of the body working together to run normally, and among them the liver is one of the most hardworking and important members. So when should the health and care of the liver begin? It begins now, from this moment. Only by preparing in advance can the liver be well protected, and let it escort our health.
