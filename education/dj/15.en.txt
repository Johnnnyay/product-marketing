# Pairing Nutrients for Gut Health

Hello, friends. I am Ling Min, from Liaoyang. Today I will share with you the pairing of nutrients for gut health management. As living standards rise, our food becomes more and more refined, causing our intake of dietary fiber to become lower and lower. According to the recommended intake of the 2016 edition of the Chinese Dietary Guidelines, a normal adult should reach 25 to 30 grams of [[dietary fiber|膳食纤维, the indigestible parts of plant foods that add bulk to the stool and feed beneficial gut bacteria. It supports digestion, blood sugar, and heart health.|https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002470.htm]] a day, but our actual intake is only 10.8 grams, less than half of the guideline's recommended amount. Today many people treat only fish, meat, eggs, and milk as nutritious food, and do not treat vegetables and fruit as nutritious food. Added to the modern lifestyle of staying up late, stress, and irregular eating, this causes an imbalance of the gut flora, which puts most people's gut health in crisis and leads to the occurrence of many diseases.

In the earlier courses, we also mentioned that an imbalance of the flora causes an imbalance of the chemical barrier. When the chemical barrier is imbalanced, there are fewer probiotics and more harmful bacteria. The harmful bacteria then enter our immune barrier and produce all kinds of inflammatory factors, causing the permeability of the mechanical barrier to increase, which is the leaky gut we mentioned. After the gut leaks, the gut's toxins flow through the whole body, putting our health in crisis. Maintaining the balance of the gut flora is also an important step in gut health management. To achieve gut balance, we must supplement enough [[probiotics|益生菌, live beneficial microorganisms, mainly bacteria, that can support the balance of the gut flora. They are found in fermented foods and supplements.|https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/probiotics-what-you-need-to-know]].

Regarding the gut flora, there are nearly a thousand known species, and the number is about a million billion. This huge flora can be roughly divided into three types: beneficial bacteria, harmful bacteria, and neutral bacteria. Beneficial bacteria are the flora that help the body digest food and raise resistance to outside factors, and they make up 30 percent of the total flora. When your immune function is balanced, they play a leading role, maintaining the integrity of the gut lining, so that garbage and toxins do not easily leak into the blood vessels beneath the gut lining, and the blood in our body stays relatively clean. The second type of flora is harmful bacteria, which make up only 10 percent, but do not underestimate this 10 percent, because they are too easily harmed by outside chemicals. The third type is neutral bacteria, making up 60 percent. They have one trait: they are opportunists, fence-sitters. They lean toward whoever is stronger. Under normal conditions, when our immune function is good, they ally with the beneficial bacteria, and 90 percent of them maintain our gut health. When our beneficial bacteria weaken, they ally with the harmful bacteria, and the two together use 70 percent to fight our 30 percent of beneficial bacteria. When the harmful bacteria dominate, our gut crisis appears, and the gut leaks. So we must supplement enough beneficial bacteria to maintain gut health.

Another very important nutrient is our dietary fiber. Fiber can be divided into soluble dietary fiber and insoluble dietary fiber. Dietary fiber has the following effects on the body: first, it promotes bowel movement and maintains gut health; second, it slows the rise of blood sugar; third, it inhibits the absorption of cholesterol; fourth, it increases satiety and controls weight; fifth, it lowers the risk of diseases like bowel cancer, heart disease, diabetes, and fatty liver. According to a British journal, intake of dietary fiber can enhance the gut's absorption of key elements like iron, zinc, and calcium. Analysis found that for every 7-gram increase in dietary fiber intake, the risk of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease drops by 9 percent, the risk of stroke drops by 7 percent, the risk of colon cancer by 8 percent, the risk of rectal cancer by 9 percent, and the risk of diabetes by 6 percent. For people with a daily intake of 25 to 29 grams of dietary fiber, the all-cause mortality of the study participants dropped by 15 percent, and coronary heart disease mortality dropped by 31 percent. People with chronic diseases like high blood pressure, diabetes, and high blood lipids, or those troubled by constipation, should eat as much dietary fiber as possible, and it is fine to eat a bit more than the recommended amount.

By increasing the intake of probiotics and dietary fiber, we can help the gut expel toxins. Next, the gut enters the repair stage. We know that the basic substance for gut repair is protein. To repair faster, we add collagen peptides, so as to quickly repair the elasticity of the whole gut, the gut lining, and the gut's smooth muscle, and prevent leaky gut. Of course, for these proteins to be absorbed and take effect, the participation of the B vitamins is needed. In addition, the health of the lining is also related to vitamin A. Vitamin A maintains the health of the gut lining. There is also a very important substance in the cell membrane called phospholipid, so we must also supplement lecithin, to make our gut cells each very healthy and strong.
