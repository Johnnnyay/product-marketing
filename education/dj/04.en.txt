# How the Liver Drives Heart and Brain Disease

Hello everyone. Today we will talk about the relationship between cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease and the liver. Heart and brain disease is also caused by a decline in liver function. To talk about cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease, we first have to talk about fat. Fat is something some people love and some people hate, and most people love it first and hate it later. Food without fat does not taste good, and once fat grows on your own body, you hate it because you cannot shake it off. In fact, fat metabolism is an important and complex biochemical reaction in the body. With the help of various enzymes, fat is processed into the substances the body needs to keep our life activities running normally. The synthesis and release of fat, the breakdown of fatty acids, the production or oxidation of ketone bodies, the synthesis of cholesterol and phospholipids, and the synthesis and transport of lipoproteins, that is a long list of functions, and they all mainly take place in the liver. When the liver is healthy, it can carry out fat metabolism well. It breaks down, synthesizes, and transports the various energy supplies the whole body needs, and keeps the body in balance. But if the liver is seriously damaged, its function in fat metabolism drops sharply, and the body develops a series of problems, such as abnormally high blood pressure and various cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases.

So how does blood pressure rise? Most likely it happens like this. Under normal conditions, blood pressure fluctuates within a normal range as the body's internal environment, including the outside environment, changes. But if a person's systolic and diastolic pressure go beyond the normal range, it begins to harm the body. Looking at the symptoms, you can check your blood lipids. Those with high blood lipids also tend to have high blood pressure, and everyone can understand that. But some people clearly do not have high blood lipids, so why is their blood pressure still high? Where do we look for the cause? This situation may be connected to fat metabolism going wrong because of liver damage. We know our body has a strong immune system, and under normal conditions it regulates and protects the body. The human liver is a place that produces cholesterol. Under normal conditions this cholesterol does not trigger an immune response, because the immune system recognizes it as one of its own. Otherwise, if it were treated as a foreigner, the immune system would clear it out. So cholesterol alone is not scary. But when cholesterol reacts with free radicals in the body, it forms [[lipid peroxides|过氧化脂质, oxidized fats formed when free radicals attack fats in the body. They can damage cells and blood vessels and are tied to inflammation.|https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002404.htm]]. The immune system then becomes unhappy, thinking, you used to be one of us, so why have you changed and now hang around with free radicals all day? So it sets to work clearing them out and engulfing them. The moment this action starts, the walls of our blood vessels develop an inflammatory reaction and begin to swell. The vessel walls get narrower and narrower, and when the same blood flow passes through the narrowed vessels, blood pressure begins to rise. This is easy to understand: when a river channel narrows, the current speeds up, right? In addition, these lipid peroxides damage all kinds of cells, and especially make the blood more viscous. When the blood is more viscous, the resistance to the movement of the vascular smooth muscle increases, and blood pressure keeps rising. Healthy liver cells can automatically regulate the levels of cholesterol and [[triglycerides|甘油三酯, a type of fat in the blood. High levels are linked to a greater risk of heart disease and stroke.|https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002404.htm]] in the blood, keeping the body's metabolism in balance. But when the liver has a problem, this regulating function inevitably declines, which makes cholesterol more easily oxidized and damaged, and high blood pressure more likely.

The liver is like the commander-in-chief of the body's health, regulating the balance of all kinds of fat metabolism. When this balance is broken, blood pressure rises abnormally. If it stays high for a long time, the body's functions and organs are damaged, and it becomes easy to form [[atherosclerosis|动脉粥样硬化, the buildup of plaque made of fat, cholesterol and other substances on artery walls, which hardens and narrows the arteries over time.|https://www.health.harvard.edu/cholesterol-and-lipids/atherosclerosis-symptoms-and-treatments]], and even to trigger more cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, such as cerebral hemorrhage and coronary heart disease, which greatly harm our health. So what is atherosclerosis? Normal blood vessels in the body are elastic. If your blood pressure keeps rising, what do many people do? Many people go to the hospital to seek outside force to dilate the vessels, such as injections or medication. Remember, this is all called dilation by outside force. With outside-force dilation, the blood pressure does come down, and is that not what many people want? But your blood lipids have not come down. If you keep relying on outside-force dilation, do you not think the blood vessels will age and decline faster? The fat and various lipids inside the vessels gather together to form plaque, which looks like a yellow porridge. As the porridge-like plaque accumulates, the originally soft and elastic vessel walls gradually become thicker and harder, and the channel becomes smaller and smaller. This forms atherosclerosis. When the hardening happens in the coronary arteries, it easily causes angina, arrhythmia, and myocardial infarction. When it happens in the arteries of the brain, kidneys, or lower limbs, it causes serious organ damage and can even threaten life. How does this come about? It is because the vessels get thinner and thinner, and not only thinner but also weaker. Let us use an analogy. After atherosclerosis develops, the blood vessels in the brain are a lot like an old bicycle inner tube. When you pump air into it, the moment the pressure gets a little high, one spot bulges out abnormally. What is that bulging part called? It is called a microaneurysm. The cerebral vessels with atherosclerosis carry a great many of these microaneurysms. Under the same blood pressure, these vessel walls are more likely to rupture. Also, think about the arteries of the brain and their surrounding branches. They basically form right angles, and places with right angles bear a greater impact from blood flow, right? This also makes cerebral hemorrhage more likely.

Next let us talk about coronary heart disease. The occurrence of coronary heart disease is directly related to the body's blood lipid level. The coronary arteries are spread like a spider web over the surface of the heart muscle, and they are the important arteries that supply blood to the heart. When blood lipids rise, the walls of the coronary arteries narrow or become blocked, which causes the heart muscle to lack blood and oxygen or to die, causing chest pain, chest tightness, and even myocardial infarction. This has become a ticking time bomb in many people's bodies, an invisible enemy that endangers human health.

In fact, all of the cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases above are importantly related to our liver metabolism. Very often, when various diseases occur, we habitually treat the symptoms but neglect to look further for the root cause. To truly treat the disease, we have to find the source. This kind of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease caused by high blood pressure is essentially tied to long-term liver damage and abnormal fat metabolism. The liver is like a precise scale, constantly regulating the balance of every level in the body, and it plays an irreplaceable role in health. More than half of the diseases in the body are related to a decline in liver function. So only by truly caring for the liver in daily life and doing good liver health management can we let it function normally and better promote the health of the body. That is all for today.
