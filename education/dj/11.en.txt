# Most Disease Begins in the Gut

Hello, friends. I am Ling Min, from Liaoyang. I was a teacher by training and retired from the telecommunications industry, and I am a nationally registered level-three nutritionist. Today I want to talk with you about a topic: all disease begins in the gut. Some friends may ask, is it really that exaggerated? Is that not alarmist? After hearing my sharing below, you may not think so.

Have you noticed that when your stomach hurts, your whole body can feel unwell? Eating less and moving more is a weight-loss method many people firmly believe in, but controlling the mouth and moving the legs has not made many people slim. What other secrets lie behind obesity? There are many causes of insomnia, but did you know that the gut flora can also cause insomnia? Did you know that the gut, called the second brain of the human body, is an important digestive organ, the body's largest immune organ, and also the place richest in flora? The number of microorganisms in the human body exceeds a million billion, and the gut accounts for 78 percent of them. Every day the food we eat is broken down, absorbed, and finally excreted through the gut.

Research shows that many human diseases may be related to an imbalance of the gut's microecology. Gut microorganisms play a very important role in metabolizing food, resisting infection, and responding to medicine. They are not only related to digestion, absorption, metabolism, and immunity, but also take part in the occurrence of diseases like tumors, diarrhea, obesity, and hyperlipidemia. The Greek physician and father of modern medicine, Hippocrates, first proposed in the third century BC that [[all disease begins in the gut|万病始于肠, a principle attributed to Hippocrates and long echoed in traditional Chinese medicine. Modern research on the gut microbiome supports the idea that an imbalance of gut bacteria can drive inflammation linked to allergy, inflammatory bowel disease, obesity, diabetes and other conditions.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4425030/]]. For a long time, civilized society had no evidence or mature theory to explain this idea. In the nineteenth century, the Russian biologist and Nobel laureate [[Metchnikoff|梅契尼科夫, Élie (Ilya) Metchnikoff, the Russian biologist who shared the 1908 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his work on immunity. He is also remembered for proposing that the bacteria of the colon influence aging and longevity.]] discovered the most direct link between human longevity and a healthy balance of bacteria in the body, and confirmed that death begins in the colon. Metchnikoff's discovery made science begin to believe that up to 90 percent of all known human diseases can be traced back to an unhealthy gut. We can say with confidence that if disease begins in the gut, so do health and vitality. Metchnikoff also said that beneficial bacteria must outnumber harmful bacteria. Unfortunately, today most people carry too many harmful pathogenic bacteria, and their bodies lack a rich and diverse microbial world.

Research shows that the following symptoms are all related to our unhealthy gut: intestinal diseases including celiac disease, asthma, autism, insomnia, allergy and food allergy, painful joint inflammation and arthritis, chronic fatigue, high blood pressure, atherosclerosis, diabetes, skin problems like acne and eczema, overweight and obesity, mood disorders including depression and anxiety, bad breath, gum disease and tooth problems, memory problems and poor concentration, chronic constipation or diarrhea, extreme menstrual and menopausal symptoms, frequent colds or infections, and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder in children. In fact, paying attention to your gut health helps relieve all degenerative or inflammatory diseases. It is precisely the three big evil forces, environment, food, and stress, that throw our gut flora out of balance and bring on these diseases.
