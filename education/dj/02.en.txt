# How the Liver Does Its Work

Hello everyone. Today is the second lesson, and we will talk about the remarkable liver and how it works. The liver is an important immune organ in the human body, and there is a saying that it is called the temple of life, because the body's various metabolic, detoxifying, and immune functions all depend on the liver. So once the liver becomes ill, the body faces a serious crisis. How can we avoid these crises as much as possible in daily life? We first need to understand the characteristics of the liver. The liver actually has three main characteristics. First, the substance of the liver itself has no pain-sensing nerves. Second, the liver has a critical threshold. Third, the liver has a powerful ability to repair itself. If we do not understand these characteristics, we will misread the signals the liver sends us in daily life, and in many cases we will simply overlook these danger signals, which brings hidden risks to our health.

Let us start with the first characteristic. Inside the liver itself there are no pain-sensing nerves. Only the capsule that wraps the liver has sensory nerves. So liver diseases, such as tumors, produce no symptoms or sensations in the early stages. Only when a tumor grows large enough in the later stages to cause enlargement inside the liver and tension in the liver capsule does a dull pain or discomfort appear in the area of the liver. It is precisely because of this characteristic that many people overlook the early danger signals of a liver problem, because even when the liver is already damaged, a blood test of your [[transaminase|转氨酶, the liver enzymes ALT and AST, measured in blood tests as markers of liver cell damage. They stay normal until enough liver cells are injured.|https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/ast-test/]] levels may still come back normal. This is because transaminase only shows up in the blood once a certain number of liver cells have been damaged and ruptured. But long before the liver damage becomes obvious, a chronic liver disease has often already been developing in the body for many years. That is why diseases like [[cirrhosis|肝硬化, the late stage of liver scarring. Long-term damage replaces healthy liver tissue with scar tissue that gradually stops the liver from working.|https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/cirrhosis]] and liver cancer are so often already in the middle or late stages by the time they are found, while the early stage, the easiest time to treat, has been missed because of neglect.

Now the second characteristic: the liver has a critical threshold. What does this mean? The liver is closely tied to our health, and it directly controls the growth and functioning of every cell in the body. The critical threshold means this: even if the liver loses sixty percent of its capacity, it can still appear to be working normally, and blood test results will still look normal. At that point you have no sense that part of your liver function has been lost. In daily life you still feel perfectly normal, with nothing out of the ordinary. Notice the contrast here. When the liver has already lost sixty percent of its capacity, you still do not feel that anything is wrong. But by the time you clearly feel unwell, or a checkup shows severely abnormal liver function, the liver has often already lost eighty percent. And remember, when more than eighty-five percent of the liver has died, all its biochemical reactions may stop, which means the liver disease has reached a serious stage requiring a liver transplant or further treatment. So paying attention to the signals from the liver in time, and guarding against the liver problem worsening past the critical threshold, is something we must take very seriously in health management.

The third characteristic is that the liver has a powerful ability to repair itself. Although liver problems are easy to overlook, the liver also has another major feature: a strong ability to repair itself. When part of a person's liver is removed, the remaining liver cells actually keep dividing and regenerating, and within a period of time the liver can grow back to its original size. When liver cells die, as long as the mesh framework of the [[liver lobules|肝小叶, the basic structural units of the liver, roughly hexagonal arrangements of liver cells around a central vein. The liver can regrow as long as this framework is intact.|https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/cirrhosis]] remains intact, the regenerating cells around the lobules extend along that framework and restore the normal structure. It is a bit like a morning glory: as long as there is a trellis, it can climb. The liver cells can replicate and regenerate in much the same way. It is also a little like an earthworm: if you cut it in two, the other half can grow back. So the liver's ability to repair itself is extremely strong.

Next, how do we recognize the disease signals the liver sends? Although the liver is so devoted and resilient, and never gives up until the last possible moment, how should we detect early liver problems in daily life? These methods can help us make a reasonable judgment.

The first is loss of appetite. The liver is the body's largest digestive organ, so liver problems often show up in the digestive system. In addition, the liver is closely tied to the gallbladder's secretion of bile, and bile is mainly used to digest meat. So a liver problem can also show up as a dislike of food, especially an aversion to greasy foods.

The second thing to watch for is unstable mood. Of course this varies from person to person. A person with a liver problem may become irritable and easily angered, or may become sullen and prone to sighing. We know the liver governs the smooth flow of qi, and this is closely tied to a person's emotions. So when the flow is either too strong or too weak, it can both cause emotional abnormalities.

The third thing to observe is an increase in facial acne. In the body, the corpus luteum hormone promotes the secretion of sebum, and the liver is able to break down this hormone to regulate the overall hormonal balance. So when liver function declines, its ability to break down the corpus luteum hormone weakens, your hormonal balance is disrupted, and sebum secretion increases, leading to acne. Of course, adolescent acne may be different. Adolescent acne is mainly related to genetics, higher androgen levels, and overactive sebaceous glands, and it is also related to abnormal keratinization at the hair follicle opening, as well as diet and sleep. It belongs to the surge of hormones in a growing child during puberty, and it should not be blamed on the liver. But I want to say that children at this age especially need to care for their liver, for one simple reason: they need the liver to balance the body's hormones.

The fourth sign is a reddening of the tip of the nose. The so-called red nose, also called rosacea, is the expansion of the tiny blood vessels at the tip of the nose. A red nose does not necessarily mean liver damage, but if you observe many women, when their liver function declines and their hormones are disordered, they too are prone to a red nose. Why does rosacea happen? Heavy drinking harms the liver, so people who drink a lot tend to get rosacea. And the fact that drinking harms the liver tells us something: a bad liver shows up in a red nose.

The fifth thing to observe is a darkening of the complexion. We know the liver plays an important role in iron metabolism. Normally the liver stores iron, but when liver cells are damaged, the iron inside them flows into the blood, and the increase of iron in the blood causes the face to darken. So when a person's complexion darkens suddenly for no apparent reason, you must be alert to whether the liver is damaged, and care for it promptly.

Finally, let us say this. Apart from the brain, there is almost no other organ in the human body as complex as the liver, and no other organ has as many important functions. Precisely because it has so many important functions, think about it in reverse: are not the great majority of diseases also closely tied to the liver? So understanding and protecting the liver creates the precondition for the body to heal itself. If management is the first step of health management, then the liver is the core of our health management. I wish you all the best in your health management. See you tomorrow, and thank you.
