# Caring for the Liver Is Where Health Starts

Hello everyone. Today we will talk about the eighth lesson, which is also the last, caring for the liver is where health truly starts. Under today's national policy of comprehensive health, more and more people are paying attention to health, to nutrition science and health management. Nutrition science is only the first step, and health management is an upgrade after you gradually master nutrition. In the earlier lessons we mentioned that if gut management is the first gateway of health management, then the liver is the core of our health management, the most important link in every person's health. Only when it receives our full attention can our health be protected. So before we officially begin liver health management, there is some common knowledge about protecting the liver in daily life that we must know.

To nourish the liver, we first start with lifestyle. The natural environment we live in is complex and changeable. Climate and environment affect the body's changes every moment. So only by following the laws of nature can we protect our health, for example by sleeping regularly, eating regularly, and resting and working regularly, and turning these into good long-term habits. Only then can the organs of the body work in an orderly way, and can the good recovery of liver cells be promoted. Combining work and rest, not staying up late, and preventing over-fatigue, these words all sound like clichés. But in fact, physiology has long held that fatigue is a conscious physiological phenomenon. Whether the fatigue comes from physical or mental work, it is a protective reaction of the cerebral cortex, signaling that you need rest. If this state is not recovered for a long time, it easily becomes over-fatigue. Once fatigued, we know that resistance drops. With lowered resistance, the body easily suffers from bacteria, and health is threatened. At the same time, enough sleep also effectively protects the liver. A good sleeping posture and quality sleep not only remove fatigue but also help liver function recover. How to sleep well? You can listen to the part of our foundational health management course about what the sleep cycle is and how to calculate and adjust the sleep cycle, which explains it in detail. Why do we emphasize sleep so much? Because it is during sleep that the body secretes large amounts of [[growth hormone|生长激素, a hormone released mainly during deep sleep that drives growth, tissue repair, and the synthesis of protein in the body.|https://www.health.harvard.edu/sleep/what-is-sleep-architecture]]. It is simple: children grow taller only while sleeping. What do these hormones do? Only with them can the body grow and synthesize the protein and other nutrients it needs. In addition, with a good sleep state, your immunity strengthens, which also helps liver cells recover. Some small details also matter. For example, do not move right after a meal. Close your eyes and rest for ten or twenty minutes. And every morning or afternoon, you can often tap and massage the liver and gallbladder meridians, which also helps protect and nourish the liver.

Now let us talk about diet. Our country is a great nation of food, and in traditional health culture, dietary regulation has always held an especially important place. What is the guiding principle of diet? It is four characters, yin-yang balance. If the nature and flavor of foods are matched well, they help keep the body's nutritional balance and benefit health. If matched poorly, they break the body's balance and harm health. Whose diet was yin-yang balanced? Ancient people generally had the best nutritional balance, while the yin-yang balance of our modern diet has long been broken. What do we do? Choosing food according to the modern body's condition is also a basic principle of dietary regulation. So dietary regulation is an important part of liver protection, and it is also one of the important measures to promote health. How can nourishing the liver keep nutritional balance? There are a few principles and guidelines I can share.

First, control calorie intake. Eating high-calorie food increases the burden on the liver and gallbladder, and over time increases digestive system disorders and affects the recovery of liver function. Too little calorie intake increases protein loss and is not good for the repair and regeneration of liver cells. So under normal conditions, the daily calorie supply should be about 1,500 to 2,000 kilocalories, which is enough. I think this number sounds like nothing to everyone. What is 1,500 to 2,000 kilocalories? In fact, modern people do not lack calories at all. So here is a piece of advice: eat fewer carbohydrates. As long as your hands and feet are not cold in winter and your heart does not race in summer, you basically do not lack calories. Too many calories are extremely bad for liver repair.

Second, take in high-quality protein. We know that protein is the essential substance for liver cell repair and must be taken in every day. But for people with liver disease, protein cannot be eaten carelessly. You have to choose. It is not that you pounce on any protein. Because the protein in traditional food, especially animal protein, is broken down by bacteria in the gut and produces ammonia and other harmful substances, which can trigger and worsen liver disease. So protein cannot be supplemented carelessly. You have to learn to choose. How to choose? You can listen to the foundational course on family health management, and pay special attention to the role of plant protein.

Third, take in enough of a variety of vitamins. We all know that vitamins can activate many liver functions. For example, [[vitamin A|维生素A, a fat-soluble vitamin that supports vision, immune function, and the health of the skin and mucous membranes, including the lining of the digestive tract.|https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/vitamin-a/]] protects the liver and prevents liver disease. The B vitamins drive the body's metabolism, turning sugars, fats, and protein into energy, and they are essential vitamins. Vitamin C raises the activity of liver enzymes and strengthens the functions of breaking down and excreting. Vitamin E has an antioxidant effect, preventing lipid peroxides from harming the liver and speeding up the metabolism and breakdown of fat. You can also find this part of the course in the family health management course.

Fourth, take in more dietary fiber. This avoids constipation and obesity, and most importantly, it reduces the burden on the liver. Because a large amount of dietary fiber helps the liver detoxify and neutralize poison, sharing the liver's burden. The above only broadly covers dietary balance. But in fact, everyone knows that the modern diet basically only fills the stomach, not health. Because if the body is healthy, it is not just that the stomach is full, but that your cells are full. This is why the concept of [[hidden hunger|隐性饥饿, a deficiency of vitamins and minerals even when calorie intake is adequate. The World Health Organization describes it as a lack of essential micronutrients that can impair health without causing an obvious feeling of hunger.|https://www.who.int/health-topics/micronutrients]] exists today. If you want to hear this part, you can also find the relevant chapters in our family health management course. In addition, regular meals can strengthen the liver's absorption of various nutrients and reduce the burden on the liver. Through a regular and balanced diet, the liver gains some repair, or reduces a large amount of extra burden, letting it function better.

Next, let us talk about exercise. As we said earlier, the liver is an important and the largest organ in the body, and most of the body's toxins are metabolized out through the liver. So exercise is an effective way to protect the liver, because exercise not only strengthens muscles and bones, but also enhances liver function, promotes metabolism, and strengthens the ability to resist toxins. It lets the liver produce a stronger inhibitory effect on viruses and reduces viral replication. What exercise is suitable? Slow jogging, walking, tai chi, and yoga are all exercises that benefit liver care. Among them, walking is the most suitable. Why? Because everyone can do it. Unlike the other three, its learning cost is small. Also, walking does not increase the body's metabolism and the liver's burden, and it can regulate our mood and relieve mental pressure, which helps the body repair. Slow jogging is also a comprehensive exercise. It clearly strengthens heart and lung function, exercises the muscles of the whole body, and regulates the nervous system. Insisting on slow jogging over the years also promotes blood circulation in the liver and improves the nutrition absorbed by liver cells, greatly helping the recovery of liver function. [[Tai chi|太极拳, a Chinese martial art that combines slow, flowing movement with breathing and mental focus. It draws on classical philosophy and traditional Chinese medicine.|https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/tai-chi-what-you-need-to-know]], as a kind of Chinese martial art, absorbs classical philosophy and traditional Chinese medical theory, and forms a soft, slow, and light form of boxing that trains both inside and outside. It is also very good for liver care. Traditional Chinese medicine has always held that when practicing tai chi, the mind is clear and far-reaching, the mood is calm, and the inside and outside are relaxed, which lets the liver qi flow freely, so that the liver is nourished and liver blood grows. Yoga is also very good. It is an aerobic exercise combining movement and stillness, and it can optimize the body's internal environment and improve the body's ability to heal itself. As an auxiliary method, it is effective for liver disease, high blood pressure, heart disease, obesity, diabetes, insomnia, and headache. Besides these exercise methods, there are many others, such as the Eight Brocades, the Five Animal Frolics, qigong, and swimming. These are all exercises beneficial to liver repair. As long as we choose what suits us and keep at it, they can well assist in improving the liver.

Now let us talk about mood. Nourishing the liver is also related to mood. In fact, the human body produces more than three thousand cancer cells every day. But do not worry, the body naturally produces another kind of cell to fight them, specially used to kill cancer cells. But if a person is in a low mood all day, the power to fight and kill cancer cells drops by more than twenty percent. That is to say, negative emotions like depression, anxiety, and anger all affect the cellular immune response. They can lower the activity of thymus-dependent lymphocytes and reduce resistance to viral and fungal infections and the ability to monitor tumor cells. The obvious signs are unexplained fever, sluggish feeling, fatigue, poor digestion, and poor concentration. So once a person's mind is out of balance, the body follows with a series of changes. Over time, disorders of the nervous system and endocrine system appear, and various diseases slip in. Precisely for this reason, learning to manage your emotions in daily life is especially important. Here is a suggestion that may help us manage our emotions and stop them from escalating: use the power of six seconds.

We know that emotional changes happen in an extremely short time. How short? Six seconds. In just six seconds, our calm mood may well be stirred into waves, a ready-to-erupt anger may be replaced by a calm smile, and taut nerves may already find a solution. These brief six seconds may decide whether the system in the brain that produces emotion can successfully link with the rational-thinking part, the cerebral cortex, to make the best decision to govern our behavior. So using the power of six seconds can stop emotions from escalating. That is, when something makes you want to explode in anger, give yourself six seconds to calm down at once, and often a new world opens up. We all know that our emotions change eighty thousand times faster than our thinking in response to changes in the outside environment. Facing emotions, we are often passive, without enough time to react, and often the emotion is ignited within a quarter of a second. If we find the emotion has already been ignited, then the best way to ride the emotion is to give ourselves six seconds to calm down, letting the two important systems of the brain, emotion and thought, connect, so as to help you make the best choice. Of course, accepting and recognizing the emotion is also an important step. When we can manage our emotions in this way, perhaps many of the emotions in life will no longer, through neglect, cause stagnant liver qi and harm our health.
