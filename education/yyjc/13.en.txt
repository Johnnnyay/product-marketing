# Does Bone Broth Really Build Calcium? Calcium, Part 2

How should children and pregnant women supplement calcium? Can bone broth supplement calcium? Is it true that more sun means more calcium? In this session of the nutrition basics course, we continue the story of calcium.

Today let us talk about calcium. I am careful to supplement calcium myself, but many people say, look, I have been supplementing for a while, what is the result? I do not know if you have heard this kind of question: I supplement calcium, and after ten days what is the result? You need to understand, how do you test the result of calcium supplementation?

Let me give an example. I once knew a woman in her sixties. She came back and told me her face had been scraped. I looked and said, what happened, did you fall? She said, I was probably too happy getting home, carrying my bag, and on the last step I missed it, and I rolled all the way down the stairs. A woman in her sixties rolling down the stairs, that sounds alarming, right? But after the fall she got up, brushed off the dust, and went home. At home she saw in the mirror her face was scraped, applied a little medicine herself, and went out to work the next day. That is when she ran into me, and I asked what had happened. So what is the result of calcium supplementation? If you never break a bone, you will never feel the result.

You may think you should always have strong bones, that even in your sixties or seventies, rolling down the stairs should not break anything. But I tell you, that is exactly the best proof of long-term, consistent calcium supplementation. And you need to know how painful a fracture is once it happens. I once met someone, nothing complicated, also on the stairs. He tripped, reached out to brace himself, and in that single brace, the ring finger of his right hand was sprained. It was not a fracture, just a ligament slightly displaced. At the hospital, after the checkup, there was not much to do, just a splint to fix it in place, with the right hand held in one position, wrapped in bandage with a metal splint inside, kept that way for two months while it healed. How much do you think that affected his life?

So here I want to make a point about health mindset. Through this I want to tell you: however good a health product is, it is not as important as a healthy mindset. What really brings a person health is not the health product, but the healthy mindset. Even if a person uses health products comprehensively, or even deliberately picks the most expensive ones, and has the means to afford them, without a healthy mindset they will still struggle to gain health.

I know calcium is important, and I understand why we need enough calcium daily. So how much is "enough"? Let us look at the calcium table for each age group. Taking 800 to 1000 milligrams a day for an adult as the baseline, does menopause add some? Menopause adds 200 milligrams. Then for pregnant and nursing women, does it add more?

Here I want to raise another important idea about health. In China, nutrient supplementation for nursing mothers often ends with the "sitting the month" period. The peak of supplementation is that postpartum month, and after the month is over, the amount drops. That is wrong. You need to know that a nursing mother, especially one exclusively breastfeeding, should keep raising her nutrient intake after giving birth, all the way until the child is weaned. Especially calcium, especially into the middle and late stages of breastfeeding, her calcium need is actually higher. So please keep this in mind.

One more point concerns calcium for children. On the first row, children's calcium, there is a common misconception: how much calcium to give a child. Most people decide by body size, thinking, I take 800 milligrams a day, and my child reaches my waist, so about half, so give the child half. Is that right? No. Do not convert the calcium amount by body size. You can see that from age four to ten, from kindergarten through early primary school, a child's calcium need is actually the same as an adult's. From one to three years old, it is already three-quarters of an adult's, which is not small. So if we give a one-to-three-year-old a quarter or a third of an adult's amount, that is too little. By eleven to fourteen, the calcium need is already similar to menopause, and adolescents need extra calcium.

I have always regretted that I probably did not get enough calcium during my own adolescence, and this is something that cannot be made up later. Yesterday our director told me he hoped I would wear slimmer trousers on stage, but in the end I insisted on a looser pair. Why? Because I have bow legs. Bow legs mean that standing with both heels together, the gap between the knees is more than half a centimeter. I did not know I had bow legs before. Later in military training we had to stand at attention and squeeze the legs together, and the instructor put a playing card between each person's legs that you had to hold in place. With me, the card just dropped, again and again. He said, use strength, and I said, I am using strength, I just have a bit of bow legs. That is calcium and iron deficiency in early childhood, uncorrected for a long time, so that even when the bones rebuild in adolescence, the original damage remains. So do not overlook it: even in modern society, rickets is still an important nutritional health problem for children.

Next, food sources of calcium. Food sources of calcium come in two kinds: foods high in calcium, and foods good for calcium. What is high in calcium? Small fish eaten whole with the bones. Why is eating small fish good? Because you do not spit out the bones, and you eat the calcium in the fish bones too, so the calcium content is high. But what food is good for calcium? The most outstanding is milk. Whether you dare to drink milk today is another question, but milk is indeed a good food source of calcium, because lactose promotes calcium absorption. There is also one everyone knows, bone broth for calcium, especially for women after childbirth, who should cook bone broth, fish soup, and rib soup.

So can bone broth really supplement calcium? Bone is a poorly soluble calcium phosphate compound. You want to soak such hard-to-dissolve bone in water, heat it, and boil the calcium out into the water, which is actually very difficult. There was an experiment using different clay pots, adding different amounts of bone and different amounts of water, some thick and some thin, boiled for different times, and compared. In the end, the calcium measured in even the thickest, longest-boiled bone broth was no different from tap water. There is even a comparison saying that if you wanted to boil the broth long enough to dissolve out a little more calcium, to get one day's calcium from the broth you would have to drink about a bathtub's worth, enough to fill a single-person tub.

So once you know this about food, you do not need bone broth for calcium. Does bone broth have nutrition? Yes. The nutrition of bone broth is that it supplies some protein, broken down by long simmering into amino acids, and some fat dissolved in the broth. When we make fish soup, we all aim for a milky white color, right? What is the milky white? It is fat. You need fat, in a state blended with water, to produce that milky white. So fish soup made from unfried fish does not easily turn white; you must fry the fish first, so it carries enough fat, then boil the soup to form that water-and-fat milky white. So the soup supplies fat and amino acids, which is good, but not good for calcium.

Now the next question: I pay attention to enough daily calcium, which can curb bone loss, and the bone pain from osteoporosis is held back. But then comes another question: I already have bone spurs. How do I supplement calcium to curb the trouble from bone spurs? I have to tell you, at this point there is no good way. You may have an idea in mind, say there is a spur in the knee joint, and maybe there is a surgery that opens the knee and scrapes the spur off with a small knife or spoon. But I tell you, there is no such surgery. And the spur actually grows out of the cartilage, not something you can scrape away with a small spoon. What can you do? Once a bone spur grows in the body, all our calcium work is aimed at keeping it from growing fast.

So here I want to ask you, do you now understand how important calcium is? Because if you do not pay attention to calcium, when bone spurs actually develop, there is no final cure except replacing the joint with an artificial one. As far as I know, an artificial joint, and they may ask whether you want a domestic or imported one, costs about sixty thousand yuan to replace one leg, and it does not solve the squatting problem. You will need both legs done, in two operations, two artificial joints, plus postoperative recovery. And it is not that one replacement lasts you a lifetime; it has a service life and a limited number of years.

I want to tell you, avoiding bone spurs is a core point of calcium supplementation, because once bone spurs form, they are hard to reverse. And after they form, when you hear all this you may easily feel pessimistic. Should I still supplement calcium? Yes. Why? Because the spur is still there and will keep growing, and new ones will form. In the end you see old people with deformed joints. Why? Because the spurs inside have pushed the joints out of shape, the fingers bend, the knees deform, for this reason. And tell me, does exercise still help at this point? Once bone spurs form, it is hard to keep exercising.

At the same time, I especially remind you, when a person exercises less, their sun exposure shortens, because they do not leave the room as much. With less sun, the vitamin D the body makes naturally is insufficient. Without enough vitamin D, the body's absorption of calcium drops, and the kidneys' reabsorption of calcium also drops. Why is vitamin D so important to calcium metabolism? Why have I always said to get as much sun as possible? Especially in the north, in a whole year you can probably wear short sleeves for only three or four months; the rest of the time we wear long sleeves. Dressed like this, which skin can make vitamin D when we go out? The hands and face. A bit colder, and we add a mask; colder still, maybe a hat, some even sunglasses. Human skin makes vitamin D when exposed to ultraviolet light. Can ultraviolet light pass through clothing to reach the skin?

Let me tell you, everyone has had this experience: in summer, wearing a sports vest with a wide strap, you see someone who has tanned all summer, and the place covered by the strap is still white, while the exposed skin is dark. That tells you a thin sports vest can block ultraviolet light, so do you think it can pass through a sweater or a down jacket? Impossible. So how do you make enough vitamin D? I notice that in summer, many women on the street still wear a little white cape. Finally it is summer, the time to get natural UV and make enough vitamin D, and they cover their skin with a cape. Of course, you do it for sun protection and looks, but you can block vitamin D, so then you must supplement through other channels. Have you paid attention to supplementing it? This is an important point in calcium supplementation, worth calling out separately.
