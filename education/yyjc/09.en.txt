# Deficiency vs. Chronic Disease: Multivitamins and Minerals

What is the difference between a deficiency disease and a chronic disease? What did the comet experiment prove? What important role do phytonutrients play? In this session of the nutrition basics course, we open up the mystery of multivitamins and minerals.

Today let us talk about multivitamin and mineral supplements. One important experiment concerns their protective effect on human DNA. It sounds deep, called the [[comet assay|彗星实验, a laboratory test that measures DNA damage by how far broken DNA fragments migrate in an electric field, drawn out into a comet-like tail.|https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17406208/]], but once I explain the idea, it is easy to follow.

You probably know that DNA is a double helix. Compared to a daily-life object, I think it is like a fried dough twist. If the twist sits too long and takes a lot of oxidative attack, it crumbles easily. Now take two twists in different states, one fresh and one old and already crumbled, and place them here. Then apply electricity on the other side, which pulls the twists and their crumbs forward. If a twist has been attacked harder and has more crumbs, what does its trail look like? It should spread wider and longer, right? If a twist is very fresh, with almost no crumbs, then with electricity applied, there is no trail of crumbs on the other side. That is what we see here, the difference between undamaged and damaged DNA. In the image, if it looks like a star with a long tail, that is the more damaged one; the two above with no obvious tail are less damaged. So which DNA is more damaged, top or bottom? The one with the tail below.

I want to tell you, we have actually run this experiment. Multivitamins and minerals can protect DNA from damage. That is, we can spare DNA a lot of oxidative attack and keep it from crumbling into bits. So this experiment is very persuasive for multivitamins and minerals, showing they protect the safety of human DNA.

What good does it do a person if their DNA takes less attack from the outside? The benefits are many, because everything that tends to go wrong in a person, traced to the root, comes down to DNA, the problems of aging, death, and tumors, all of them. So do not overlook this point.

There are some points to note when using multivitamins and minerals, and I will go through them one by one. First, the amounts of nutrients in a multivitamin and mineral supplement are clearly higher than a single vitamin such as niacin or pantothenic acid. The amounts in a multivitamin and mineral supplement are greater than in a B-complex alone.

If your concept of supplementation is still "you should take a bit, or you will be deficient, and low B vitamins means angular cheilitis, beriberi, and pellagra," then your understanding of multivitamins and minerals is too shallow. Why take so many vitamins? Because supplementing vitamins must rise from the concept of "deficiency disease" to the concept of "preventing chronic disease." At today's nutrition level, deficiency disease is already rare, especially in the developed big cities of the east. So why still take them? Because they can prevent heart and blood-vessel disease, stroke, and cardiac events. The risk of these diseases can be lowered by supplementing basic nutrients. So please raise your understanding of nutrients to this level.

Let me give an example. There is a technical term, [[homocysteine|同型半胱氨酸, an amino acid produced in the body whose high blood levels are linked to a higher risk of heart disease and stroke.|https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/homocysteine-test/]], a substance produced in the body's metabolism. Some large hospitals now test for homocysteine in checkups. If a person's blood test shows a high homocysteine level, what does it mean? It means the person is prone to disease events. If the person already has coronary heart disease, a high homocysteine raises their chance of angina or heart attack. If the person already has poor blood supply to the brain or hardened brain arteries, a high homocysteine makes cerebral hemorrhage or embolism more likely. That is what this marker means.

Modern nutrition research has found that supplementing B vitamins can effectively lower homocysteine levels. Have you heard that the United States once fortified all its flour with folic acid? What is folic acid for? It is a preventive. A pregnant woman lacking folic acid may carry a fetus with a neural tube defect, such as anencephaly or spina bifida. An anencephalic baby has no skull and cannot be delivered normally, so it is very frightening at delivery. This brainless fetus is the result of the mother lacking folic acid in early pregnancy. Now that we supplement folic acid, this mainly occurs only in remote rural areas. But why did the United States fortify flour nationwide with folic acid? Was it to avoid anencephaly or reduce spina bifida? No. It was because modern nutrition research found that folic acid can effectively lower the incidence of heart disease. It had reached that level. They spent money adding folic acid to flour, but it reduced everyone's disease costs, an effective approach that clearly paid off.

So here I especially remind you to grasp this nutritional concept: the point is not to fill a deficiency, but to make you less likely to develop other diseases, chronic disease, and events.

One more point: because each tablet of a multivitamin and mineral supplement is rich in nutrients, I advise taking it after a meal. Early on I experimented myself, before, during, and after meals, and I found that taking it on an empty stomach made me uncomfortable. So to spare you the same problem, we uniformly recommend taking it after a meal, with some food in the stomach, so the nutrients do not cause such a strong reaction.

Another common question: after taking a multivitamin and mineral supplement, urine turns yellow. Most people see this, and some even say the urine is not just yellow but slightly green. Let me tell you why. It is the vitamin B2 and its metabolites, a yellow substance with a faint green fluorescence. Once in the blood and filtered by the kidneys, the unabsorbed part that comes out is yellow, and if more concentrated, faintly green. Do not worry, it is the metabolite of vitamin B2, so use it with confidence.

One more point: a multivitamin and mineral supplement is relatively rich in water-soluble nutrients but less so in fat-soluble ones. If you want simplicity, taking it twice a day is fine. If you want to supplement calcium, for a normal adult who also drinks some milk, it is roughly enough for a healthy person. But for a pregnant or nursing woman, the amount may not be enough and they should add more; a menopausal woman may also need extra calcium. For the water-soluble nutrients like B vitamins and vitamin C, the amounts are relatively adequate, but fat-soluble nutrients like carotenoids, vitamin E, and iron are not. If a user especially wants to supplement vitamin E, we recommend taking extra vitamin E. If a person is iron-deficient and wants both iron and comprehensive nutrients, we recommend adding more, because a day's multivitamin and mineral supplement provides only a few milligrams of iron.

Compared to other nutrients, a multivitamin and mineral supplement has an important advantage: it contains selenium. You may know that our country did a study of long-lived populations and found that most longevity villages lie along belts where the soil is rich in selenium, and people there have a higher chance of long life.

Then there is chromium. Chromium is less commonly heard of than selenium. What is chromium for? It relates to blood-sugar metabolism. You all know the common diabetes is called type 2 diabetes. What do people usually understand about the cause of diabetes? That a person whose insulin secretion is insufficient is prone to diabetes. So some diabetes drugs work by stimulating the pancreas to secrete more insulin, thereby lowering blood sugar. But I want to tell you, today there is another common form of diabetes: some diabetic patients are actually quite overweight, yet when you test their insulin level, it is not low. Since their insulin secretion is nearly normal, why do they clearly show diabetes?

Here I want to tell you, the mechanism of diabetes in these people is quite different from the ordinary reduced insulin secretion. The amount of insulin their body secretes is not low; the problem is that their cells respond poorly to insulin. It is like insulin is a little stick, and I poke a person with the stick, and they should react somehow. If the stick is missing, of course there is no reaction. But in some overweight people, the stick is not missing; it is the person being poked who has become slow to react. There is a technical term here, [[insulin resistance|胰岛素抵抗, a condition where the body's cells respond poorly to insulin, a key factor in type 2 diabetes.|https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/what-is-diabetes/prediabetes-insulin-resistance]], and it means the body's cells barely react to insulin. This is where chromium plays its role. The value of chromium is raising the sensitivity of cells to insulin, so your body does not resist insulin. This is actually even more meaningful for many people's diabetes today, especially early-stage diabetes.

So how important are multivitamins and minerals to us? They are far more than a bit of nutrient supplementation. They contain a lot of protection for DNA, and the various nutrients work together to lower the risk of other chronic diseases and disease events in the body.
