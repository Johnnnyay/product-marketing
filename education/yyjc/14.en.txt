# Can Vitamins Reduce Heart Disease? The B Vitamins

Which vitamin should you especially remember to supplement before pregnancy? Can vitamins also reduce heart and blood-vessel events? In this session of the nutrition basics course, we unpack the natural B vitamins.

In this lesson we study the B vitamins. When it comes to the B vitamins, everyone here is familiar with them. They are important nutrients we come across often in daily life.

Let us look at the traits of the B vitamins. First, they are all coenzymes in the body's energy metabolism, so they are closely tied to the health of every one of us, and everyone needs them. Second, they are all water-soluble, so they are very prone to deficiency, and we should all replenish them promptly every day.

Here is a daily-life detail: when cooking porridge, to make it thicker, people add a little baking soda. In fact many B vitamins fear alkali, and adding soda wipes out the entire pot's B vitamins. Also, B vitamins are abundant in many organ meats, such as liver and kidney, but we do not often prepare liver and kidney, and some people simply do not like organ meats, so their B-vitamin intake is relatively low.

We split the B vitamins into two groups to make them easier to remember. One group is the B vitamins tied to energy-metabolism coenzymes, mainly B1, B2, niacin, and pantothenic acid. The other group is tied to a very important health topic, [[homocysteine|同型半胱氨酸, an amino acid whose high levels are linked to heart disease and stroke risk.|https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/homocysteine-test/]], a substance harmful to the body, and its metabolism, including B6, B12, and folic acid.

First, B1, B2, niacin, and pantothenic acid. A lack of vitamin B1 has a classic disease called beriberi. A lack of B2 has a matching classic disease, angular cheilitis. Niacin corresponds to a disease called pellagra. Pellagra is rare today, but it once occurred on a large scale in our country's Xinjiang region. Pantothenic acid deficiency has very broad signs, and lacking it also leads to insufficient energy metabolism.

You may wonder: if I lack B1, just supplement B1; if I lack B2, supplement B2; if I lack niacin, supplement niacin, is that not enough? Why are the B vitamins, not just ours but most B vitamins, made as a B complex? Here I want to tell you, the body's energy metabolism cycle has a series of steps, and pantothenic acid, niacin, B1, and B2 all work at the same time. Though it is drawn very simply here, this is a very important cycle that produces the body's energy. All the energy-producing substances a person eats ultimately enter this cycle to make energy. And in this cycle, step by step from pantothenic acid to niacin to B1 and B2 and back to niacin, they work together. So when we supplement B vitamins, it is usually a B complex.

Also, B-vitamin deficiency is closely tied to the skin, mucous membranes, and nervous system. A nerve cell in the body has a cell body in front and a long fiber trailing behind. The nerve fibers in the body are really the tails of nerve cells, and nerve signals travel along these fibers. These fibers run through the body without shorting out. Why does a signal from the ear not jump to the arm? Why does one from the eye to the leg not jump somewhere else? I tell you, the reason nerve fibers do not short-circuit their signals comes down to two important nutrients. The first is the B vitamins, because B vitamins protect the insulating sheath around the nerve fiber. It is like turning on a light, with wires connected, and even if the wires run through one conduit, each is separated by insulation so the signals do not cross. And what affects the insulation on the body's wires? First, the B vitamins, and second, lecithin. So a lack of B vitamins leads to aging insulation and shorting out.

Let me give a classic sign of B-vitamin deficiency: a nerve behind the ear, at the back of the head, throbs with a stabbing pain. This pain, I tell you, is actually peripheral neuritis caused by B-vitamin deficiency. The insulation around the peripheral nerve has aged and degenerated, so the nerve shorts out a little. This is what we call occipital neuritis, or postauricular neuritis, a peripheral nerve inflammation people often get. So do not overlook it; in this case, attend to B-vitamin supplementation.

B-vitamin deficiency has another classic sign, twitching during sleep. Halfway through sleep, the hand suddenly jerks, the foot suddenly jerks. Some people, just as they fall asleep, dream of falling from a high staircase, tense up, and wake. When do people have such dreams? Mostly in adolescence, in middle school. Parents see it and say, do not be afraid, you are just growing. But if you know that B-vitamin deficiency causes twitching during sleep, you will understand that this is a clear B-vitamin gap in the body, and that is why the sign appears.

Also, the B vitamins act on the mucous membranes. When a person vomits, and you consult a doctor, the doctor often prescribes some B vitamins. Why? Because the B vitamins stabilize the stomach lining and make the stomach more comfortable, less swollen and nauseous. That is another important role of the B vitamins.

Now the remaining three B vitamins, B6, B12, and folic acid, all affect the metabolism of several amino acids, and through that the development of cells throughout the body. The simplest example is red blood cell development. Lacking B12 and folic acid, red blood cells do not mature, and stay at a very immature, very large stage. This disease is called megaloblastic anemia, different from iron-deficiency anemia, another kind of anemia.

Also, a lack of B12 and folic acid affects the development of the fetal neural tube, especially folic acid. Everyone has heard of common birth defects, anencephaly and spina bifida. Why do they happen? Because the mother lacks folic acid, the brain vesicles do not develop, and the fetus does not grow a brain. Spina bifida you may not have seen; severe spina bifida does not survive long. There is a mild form, where a person has a tuft of hair on the back along the spine, and that spot of hair often indicates a hidden spina bifida. Lacking folic acid, the neural tube on the embryo's back does not close, and that produces anencephaly and spina bifida. So to spare a pregnant woman from carrying a fetus with a neural tube defect, remember to supplement folic acid before pregnancy.

The substance most studied in modern nutrition as harmful to health is homocysteine. B6, B12, and folic acid affect homocysteine's metabolism. What harm does homocysteine do? Mainly it damages blood vessels. If homocysteine in the blood is high, a person is more prone to atherosclerosis, and the cholesterol and lipids in the blood are more easily oxidized into atherosclerosis. Today in checkups, some large hospitals have added homocysteine testing, especially for middle-aged and older people who already have heart or blood-vessel disease. If homocysteine is high, it signals a higher chance of a heart or blood-vessel event. How do we lower homocysteine? B6, B12, and folic acid can convert homocysteine into other substances and metabolize it away.

Here I especially remind you, are we supplementing B vitamins today only to prevent beriberi and angular cheilitis? No. We do it to prevent high homocysteine, to nourish the nerves, and to prevent developmental disorders of cells.

So when does a person need B vitamins? The first row lists health issues, such as preventing heart and blood-vessel disease, because you want to lower homocysteine. I also want to share that the United States, a country with a very high rate of cardiovascular disease, fortifies its flour nationwide with folic acid. What does our country fortify? To prevent endemic goiter, our country fortifies salt with iodine. Since we fortified salt with iodine, you rarely see goiter, the "big neck" disease, anymore. There are also women preparing for pregnancy and pregnant women.

In daily life, since the B vitamins relate to energy expenditure, people who drink alcohol or exercise often also need to watch their B-vitamin intake. The B vitamins relate to the skin and mucous membranes, so with stomach discomfort or mouth inflammation, attend to B vitamins. Also, people who dislike organ meats, people controlling their diet and cutting staple foods, and situations of travel or heavy summer sweating, where B vitamins are lost, all need reminding to supplement B vitamins.

Alright, that is our lesson on the B vitamins. Thank you.
