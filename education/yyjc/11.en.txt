# Fighting Free Radicals: The Antioxidant Trio

Who is the chief culprit behind disease and accelerated aging? With so many antioxidant vitamins, is one enough? Is there any difference between natural and synthetic vitamins? In this session of the nutrition basics course, the antioxidant trio takes the stage together.

In this lesson we look at three nutrients closely tied to antioxidation: vitamin C, vitamin E, and carotenoids. We often link them together and call them the antioxidant trio.

Mention antioxidation and a bad thing immediately comes to mind, [[free radicals|自由基, unstable molecules with unpaired electrons that can damage cells, proteins, and DNA and are linked to aging and many diseases.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3614697/]]. Free radicals affect the body in every aspect of life, in dementia, tumors, stroke, diabetes, high blood pressure, and when the body is inflamed, organs also produce many free radicals. Free radicals can be said to be the chief culprit harming human health today in every way.

Moreover, even in a person without disease, aging is itself the process of being oxidized by oxygen in the air, and free radicals are produced constantly during oxidation. Some people also smoke and drink, producing more free radicals than ordinary people. So how exactly do free radicals harm the body?

As a person eats food and comes into contact with air, they encounter many free radicals with oxidative attack. These highly oxidative free radicals attack the body. Where? They attack the body's proteins, hair, skin, and eyes, and also the enzymes in the body, including the DNA in cells. If DNA is attacked too much, it mutates or breaks into fragments. Free radicals also attack fatty acids, causing fat oxidation, which in the blood vessels becomes a core cause of blood and blood-vessel problems. After the attack, the body shows the series of health problems just mentioned.

Where do free radicals come from in daily life? Smoking, drinking, strong ultraviolet light, and environmental pollution. In winter everyone shares a concern, haze. Haze contains many factors that harm the body by inducing the production of free radicals. There are also fried and grilled foods, illegal food additives, and leftover food. What I want to share is something people easily overlook, the problem of excessive food additives.

Many people no longer steam buns at home and buy them outside. Buns from a stall are very white, so white that if you hold a sheet of white paper against them, they match the paper. Flour should not be that white; normal flour has a faint yellow color. We also no longer roll noodles at home. Have you noticed that noodles from some places are very white? They may have added a whitening agent. And pickled vegetables, dried meat, and dried tofu in small packages, why do they not rot for a long time? Because preservatives were added. Even after you open the package, they are hard to spoil and last a long time. Why? Because quite a lot of preservative was added.

Here I want to tell you, many excessive additives in our food actually cause oxidative effects on the body. Does this mean from now on I must steam my own buns and roll my own noodles? No. What I am saying is that in daily life today, you cannot avoid all kinds of oxidative attack. What we can do is make ourselves and our families more antioxidant than others.

Among the important antioxidant nutrients, today I introduce vitamin C, vitamin E, and carotenoids.

First, vitamin C. Vitamin C's other name is ascorbic acid, and it corresponds to a major deficiency disease called scurvy. In the early age of sailing, many sailors on long voyages developed bleeding gums and other signs of collagen damage, and over a whole voyage many sailors died of scurvy. Later, a country's long-distance fleet brought pineapple juice for the sailors to drink before setting out, and found that pineapple juice prevented scurvy, because it is rich in vitamin C, which resists scurvy.

Vitamin C's main roles are, first, antioxidation. It also has an important role in promoting collagen synthesis and blocking the formation of carcinogens. Because vitamin C is water-soluble, it works as an antioxidant in the body's water-based environment. For example, it helps prevent hardening of the arteries, helps remove some heavy metals from the body, and promotes the absorption of some minerals such as iron. Consider children with high lead. When a child is iron-deficient, at first it is iron deficiency, and gradually lead levels rise. To remove lead from a child's body, on one hand supplement iron adequately, and on the other supplement vitamin C, because vitamin C both promotes iron absorption and promotes lead excretion. Also, vitamin C promotes the formation of antibodies, and with abundant vitamin C, the body's resistance is stronger.

Vitamin C has another trait: it can reduce melanin in the skin. There is a protein in our skin, and if oxidized it becomes melanin. If the skin has enough vitamin C, the body does not produce melanin.

Also, collagen is very important to the body, in ligaments, joints, skin, and bone. When we think of collagen, the important nutrient that comes to mind is protein, but do not forget that vitamin C is the important substance that links these proteins together to form collagen. So vitamin C is crucial to collagen formation. A person rich in vitamin C keeps skin youthful, and bones, ligaments, and joints also stay younger.

One more: if food sits too long, especially green leafy vegetables, the nitrites inside turn into nitrosamines. Nitrosamines are an important factor that stimulates digestive-system tumors and other tumors, and vitamin C can reduce nitrosamines.

How much vitamin C per day is appropriate? The recommendation is 100 milligrams a day, which is the basic amount to prevent scurvy; below that a person may develop scurvy. But today our need for vitamin C is already far higher than preventing scurvy. What we are preventing is chronic disease, so vitamin C should be at least 200 milligrams a day.

Many people ask, if I want vitamin C, why not choose synthetic vitamin C? Synthetic is simpler and cheaper, so why choose natural? Let me tell you, through spectral analysis we can see that the natural vitamin C in acerola cherry extract contains, beyond vitamin C itself, many other substances, such as citrus bioflavonoids. Pure synthetic vitamin C is very simple, just vitamin C. Do not overlook that in natural vitamin C, these citrus bioflavonoids and other nutrients help the body absorb and use the vitamin better, so the nutritional value is different.

Next, vitamin E. Vitamin E is also called tocopherol, and it is a fat-soluble antioxidant, while vitamin C is a water-soluble antioxidant. Vitamin E's main site of antioxidation is the cell membrane, because the cell membrane is a fat-soluble environment, while inside and outside the cell are water-based. So vitamin C works mainly in the water-based environment inside and outside the cell, while on the cell membrane, who does the antioxidation? Vitamin E. It is vitamin E that protects the cell membrane, sparing the cell from free-radical attack and maintaining its basic function.

If I supplement vitamin E, why choose natural over synthetic? Because natural vitamin E comes from natural plant oils. Vitamin E from wheat germ oil, for example, can supply the body essential fatty acids, and natural vitamin E works as an antioxidant in the body for longer. When do I need vitamin E? Vitamin E is an antioxidant and delays aging, so when I want to slow aging, protect skin, or even beautify, I may want more vitamin E to protect my skin cells. Also, if my blood vessels need antioxidation against attack from within, I need more vitamin E.

Here I should mention a term, high blood viscosity. In a checkup there is a blood-lipid test and also a blood-viscosity test. Does high blood lipids mean high viscosity? No, blood lipids and blood viscosity are two different dimensions. One indicator in blood viscosity is that the cells in the blood stick together more easily. When do blood cells stick together more easily? When the cell membranes have been oxidized, their viscosity rises. So if we want to prevent blood cells from clumping and clotting, we should keep the cell membranes antioxidant, and what works on the cell membrane? Vitamin E. So remember, supplementing vitamin E helps protect blood-vessel health.

Also, when a person smokes or drinks, both the B vitamins and antioxidant vitamins need extra supplementation, because those are all harms to the body. And when preparing for pregnancy, we should also supply more antioxidant vitamins.

Now carotenoids. Besides antioxidation, carotenoids have another standout role, protecting vision and converting to vitamin A. So carotenoids have two roles: as carotenoids they are antioxidants themselves, and in the body they can convert to vitamin A to do vitamin A's work.

Carotenoids are very abundant in life; there are about three hundred kinds in nature, and they share a trait, mostly yellow, orange, or red. The carotenoids we encounter daily are mainly alpha-carotene and beta-carotene. The main roles of carotenoids are, first, antioxidation and delaying aging; second, as an extension of antioxidation, resisting mutation, and like vitamin C and vitamin E, killing free radicals. Carotenoids are mostly yellow, so they help the body, especially the eyes, resist the damage of blue light.

Blue light damages the eyes a lot. What do we see with? The macula, a spot in the eye we use to discern fine detail. Blue light mainly harms the macula. With long exposure to strong light, in old age a disease called [[macular degeneration|黄斑变性, age-related macular degeneration, a leading cause of vision loss in older adults that affects the central macula of the retina.|https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/age-related-macular-degeneration]] tends to develop. It is usually an old-age condition, but today it cannot be reversed, only delayed. Once it develops it cannot be reversed. What does macular degeneration look like? The more you want to see something clearly, the less clearly you see it. The more you want to see what is in a picture, the more blurred the field of view, which is very distressing. So remember to protect your eyes and macula from a young age.

One more important role of carotenoids: beta-carotene can convert to vitamin A in the body. What does vitamin A do? Everyone knows that a lack of vitamin A causes night blindness; once it gets dark, you cannot see well. Vitamin A deficiency also causes follicular papules; if you feel the skin, say the back of the arm, and it feels a bit prickly, that is an accumulation of epithelial cells. Vitamin A benefits the growth of epithelial cells and mucous membranes, and the growth of hair and skin epithelium. But why are people reluctant to supplement vitamin A? Because vitamin A is fat-soluble, and too much can accumulate in the body and cause harm. So people hesitate to supplement vitamin A long-term.

The safest way is to supplement vitamin A through carotenoids. Because if the body lacks vitamin A, carotenoids convert to vitamin A; once enough is converted, and the body no longer lacks vitamin A, the carotenoids stop converting and stay in the body to continue their antioxidant work. So it is very safe, with no risk of excess. By supplementing carotenoids, you get both vitamin A's role and the carotenoids' own antioxidant role, without worrying about excess.

Our carotenoids come from pure natural sources, containing alpha-carotene, beta-carotene, zeaxanthin, and lutein. Lutein is especially good for the macula. The only issue here is that after taking a lot of carotenoids, the skin may turn yellow, but the white of the eye, the sclera, will never turn yellow. If jaundice comes from a liver or gallbladder problem, the sclera turns yellow. When carotenoids turn the skin yellow, it does the body no harm at all. How do you clear the yellow? You can simply drop to two tablets, or stop for a week or two until the yellow fades, then restart at one or two tablets. That is all fine, no need to worry.

Now someone may ask, of this antioxidant trio, vitamin C works in the water-based environment, and carotenoids and vitamin E are fat-soluble. Since vitamin E works on the cell membrane, where do carotenoids work? Let me tell you. The division of labor among the three is this: in the water-based environment, vitamin C mainly works; in the fat-soluble environment, vitamin E and carotenoids work. The difference between the two is that vitamin E works in oxygen-rich tissues, while carotenoids work in oxygen-poor tissues. So do not think they can replace each other. They cannot.

Vitamin C, vitamin E, and carotenoids together are a one-plus-one-plus-one greater than three relationship, each with its own role, working in concert to help the body resist oxidation. That is our antioxidant trio, and let us fight free radicals together.
