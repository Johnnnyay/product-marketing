# The Two Key Nutrients for Growing Children

To make a child stronger. Why are calcium and iron so important for children? How do you properly correct a child's picky eating and poor appetite? In this session of the nutrition basics course, we get children's nutrition right.

In this lesson we study children's nutrition together. Speaking of children, they span many age stages. Throughout growth and development, a child's need for every nutrient is large, because the child must meet not only daily metabolism and energy use, but the whole process of growth. To put it plainly, growing from a tiny individual into a large one depends on the continuous accumulation of basic nutrients like protein and minerals.

Today we mainly share four topics: first, childhood obesity and malnutrition; second, children's bone development and health; third, iron deficiency in children; and fourth, the very common picky eating and poor appetite in children.

First, children's nutritional status, with two typical extremes, either overnutrition and obesity, or malnutrition. What causes children to develop into these two extremes? In the first case, the child takes in too much energy, too much fat and carbohydrate, meaning an unreasonable intake of energy-producing nutrients. In the second case, also from an unreasonable diet, the intake of energy-producing nutrients is relatively low, producing the thin, dry type.

Correcting childhood obesity and malnutrition must be done by adjusting the diet, not by simply cutting energy intake. How do you adjust a child's diet? It comes down to the three energy-producing nutrients, protein, fat, and carbohydrate. To lower a child's body fat, reduce fat and carbohydrate intake, and give the chubby child more protein. For the skinny "bean sprout," first, they must not be too picky, and second, they need more protein to support growth. So protein is very important to children's nutrition. The one difference is that an adult eating protein can ignore taste, but a child will not, so you must pay attention to a child's taste.

The second nutrient closely tied to children's growth is bone development and calcium. Surveys show that in our country, primary and middle school students' daily calcium intake reaches only half the recommended amount for their age. If a child's calcium is short, it affects either height or the body. Here we also list the calcium standards for children of different ages: before school age, from toddler to school age, calcium intake stays at 600 to 800 milligrams, while an adult's daily calcium is about 800 to 1000 milligrams. You can see that an ordinary child, from kindergarten through the end of primary school, needs nearly as much calcium as an adult. In adolescence, the need is even greater than an adult's, basically the same level as a pregnant woman's.

On the question of childhood rickets, today few of you have likely seen a child clearly diagnosed with rickets. If a child is not given vitamin D, the first signs are night crying and night terrors; then, because of heavy sweating, the pillow gets wet and uncomfortable, the child keeps shaking the head and rubbing the pillow, gradually rubbing off the hair on the back of the head, producing a classic early sign of rickets called occipital baldness.

Near one year old, a child starts trying to stand and walk. When body weight presses on the legs, because the leg bones are still soft, pressing outward makes bow legs, and pressing inward makes knock knees. Especially for a little girl, because both legs squeeze the pelvis, it can deform the pelvis in adulthood, which clearly affects her future pregnancy and delivery.

The classic leftover sign of rickets is pigeon chest, along with funnel chest. Funnel chest is when the pit of the chest is shaped like a funnel, a hollow that can hold water. So do not overlook the impact of rickets on overall health. Rickets can be partly corrected in children by supplementing vitamin D and calcium, but if it is too late, lifelong problems remain, so stay alert. That is why, when correcting a child's calcium status, we generally supplement calcium and vitamin D together.

Now children's iron deficiency and anemia. Iron deficiency in children is listed as a key disease to prevent and treat in our country's child health care. How do you tell if a child is iron-deficient? There are basic signs, such as the complexion. A child short of iron may have a waxy or sallow complexion, or faint pale patches. Also, iron-deficient children often show a distinctive sign called [[pica|异食癖, an eating disorder in which a person, often iron-deficient, craves and eats non-food items like dirt, paper, or ice.|https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001538.htm]], craving and eating things like wall plaster, paper, or nails. Today pica is rarely seen, but you may have seen children who love to bite their nails, which may also have an early sign of iron deficiency.

If a child is iron-deficient, how do their behavior and temperament show? Mainly fatigue and listlessness, signs of low oxygen in the body, drowsiness, or an inability to concentrate in class. Another is lower resistance, the child falling ill often.

Having covered the signs of iron-deficiency anemia, as the deficiency deepens, lead levels in the body gradually rise. There is a mechanism here I have explained before, and I will review it with you.

Suppose the line in front of me has the intestine outside, and where I stand is the blood. The food we eat, including iron and various nutrients, is in the intestine. Now to absorb iron from food into the blood, note that iron does not enter directly; there is a transport system. This system is like a little cart, with carts dedicated to transporting nutrients. If a nutrient is square, there is a square cart, the nutrient sits on it, and with a push the cart rolls in and the square nutrient is absorbed. Suppose iron is round. To transport iron in, you need a round cart, put the iron on it, push it in, and the iron is absorbed.

Why does long-standing iron deficiency in a child lead to lead overload? Because lead happens to be round too. When your food lacks enough iron, the cart sits empty. Once it is empty, who gets to ride? Lead, because its shape is round too, so it gets on the cart, and with a push, the lead comes in. Over time, the child absorbs more and more lead.

We just said an iron-deficient child shows fatigue and listlessness, but as lead overload develops, the child becomes irritable and hyperactive. Based on this transport mechanism of iron and lead, if you want to reduce a child's lead absorption, what should you do with the diet? Supplement iron. Why more iron? Because you want more iron to occupy the transport carts and block lead. And what about the lead already absorbed? Here there is an important nutrient, vitamin C. Vitamin C promotes the excretion of lead from the body. Vitamin C has two roles here: first, directly promoting lead excretion, and second, promoting iron absorption. So "iron plus C" is an important combination in children's nutrition.

Back to the question of how to supplement iron and for how long. Let me explain the mechanism of iron storage and use in the body. Suppose I have a counter selling iPads, with some iPads on the counter, and a warehouse behind with more in stock. As one is sold from the counter, I take one from the warehouse and put it up. This is like the body's iron: the counter is the iron used in daily consumption, and the warehouse is the body's large iron store.

Now a question: if the warehouse still has stock, the counter can certainly stay full, meaning that as long as there is a little stored iron, daily needs are met. If a test shows the counter cannot be kept full, that is, you are diagnosed as anemic, is the warehouse low or empty? Because even with one left, you could put it up. Since it cannot be filled, the warehouse has nothing left. The body's iron is the same. If anemia is diagnosed and the child shows signs, the body's iron stores are exhausted, and now it is time to restock. After restocking, first fill the counter, and once full, business is not affected. But should the restocking stop? No. Because you have only filled the counter, and the stock is still zero, so you must keep restocking until the warehouse is full. The same with iron: after supplementing an iron-deficient child, in about three weeks the complexion turns rosy and the fatigue fades. Should you stop then? No, keep supplementing. To fill the child's iron use and iron stores both, you need a full daily amount for over six months. Many parents stop supplementing regularly once the child improves, which is a real pity.

Let me add one point here, intestinal parasite infection in children. Children's hygiene is not always good, and you will see dirt under their nails. Many parasite eggs spread this way, so deworm children regularly.

Next is a common children's problem tied to diet and nutrition, poor appetite, picky eating, and food refusal. One important nutrient here is zinc. Zinc-deficient children tend to have a distorted appetite, because zinc is closely related to a substance in the body called gustin, which supports the sense of taste.

In daily life, how do you avoid and correct a child's poor appetite and picky eating? First, a picky mother tends to raise a picky child. Because the mother eats different foods, the flavor of her milk shifts subtly, and if the infant gets used to these shifts, the child adapts to more foods later. Research even confirms that such children are not only less picky later, but have a lower rate of chronic disease in adulthood.

Also, at mealtimes, do not distract the child with things that feel novel. If the child is eating and you put fresh toys beside them, they may lose interest in the meal.

Next, we adults may not be so strict about a food's color, aroma, and taste, but a child is different. A child insists on taste, refusing flavors they dislike and colors they reject. So you can shape food into cartoon animals to make the child happy to eat.

One more point, try not to criticize the child at meals. A child's sense of discouragement is strong and suppresses appetite, so encourage more. If the child refuses a food, do not blame or force, because forcing makes them resist more. If the child will not eat, just take the food away, and try another form next time. For a child trying a new food, ten to fifteen attempts is not too many.

For a child who already has poor appetite or picky eating, how do we correct these bad habits? There is a basic principle: accept that everyone has some food preference, and hardly anyone is completely unpicky. As long as the pickiness is not excessive, it is acceptable. But excessive pickiness should be corrected, because it not only causes malnutrition but also harms the child's personality and psychology, and the child may grow into someone with a rather absolute temperament.

Also, parents should trust a child's own regulation of food. Research found that comparing meal to meal, a child's intake can differ by over forty percent, eating much more at one meal and much less at the next. But measured by the day, the difference is under ten percent. So do not worry that missing one meal will starve the child, and do not use snacks to fill the gap of a missed meal.

Also, as parents, we should broaden our own diet and try more foods, giving the child a chance to try different foods too.

One last point, please understand that sometimes it is the child's own body. Some meats are genuinely hard for the child's teeth to chew, and unchewed meat in the mouth is very uncomfortable, so over time they may dislike meat. Have you tried mincing the meat very fine so it is easy to swallow? Also, a child's teeth are not good at cutting fibrous vegetables. If you keep pushing coarse, older cabbage or whole leafy greens, it is hard for the child, and over time they refuse. Have we tried chopping the vegetables fine and adding them to some food so they are easy to chew and swallow? This is something parents should also attend to.

Children's nutrition is multifaceted, and in most cases it depends on the parents' nutritional and health mindset. Here we take this course as a chance to call on parents to learn more about nutrition, to bring health not only to themselves but to their whole family. Thank you.
