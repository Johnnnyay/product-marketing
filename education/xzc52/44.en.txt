# The Gut Microbiome: Our Bacteria and Us

The dietary fiber and probiotics we studied earlier lay a very good foundation for managing the gut from here on. From those lessons we know that the bacteria in our bodies live in symbiosis with us. All the flora in the body together weigh [[about 1.5 kilograms|A 2016 recalculation in PLoS Biology put the total mass of the body's bacteria closer to 0.2 kilograms wet weight, and the bacteria-to-human-cell ratio at roughly 1.3 to 1 rather than the 10 to 1 figure the speaker cites later in this lesson. Both older numbers are still widely repeated.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4991899/]]. We also know that as a fetus we are sterile; our flora has not been established. At birth, the mother gives us our first flora. The first channel through which we receive it is the mother's birth canal. Shortly before delivery, her birth canal becomes covered with beneficial bacteria, and during delivery the baby's skin is coated with the flora passed down from the mother. So flora is inherited from the previous generation, and if the child is not born vaginally, that inheritance is lost.

The second channel is breastfeeding. The flora in breast milk is also passed to the next generation. If neither of these happens, you have no flora to give the next generation, and their flora is easily unbalanced. That is when extra probiotics are needed. So we know flora is passed to the next generation, and if the mother's flora is poor, the child's flora will not be good either. That is why the first group who should take extra probiotics seriously is mothers.

Second, we should know that as we age, harmful bacteria in the body increase and beneficial bacteria decrease. How do we know? I wonder how many of you have noticed that older people at home sometimes carry an "old person smell." It is a strange odor. Even after a bath it does not come off, and it is especially noticeable on the towels they use. Younger family members are sometimes puzzled by it. That smell is evidence that the beneficial bacteria are dwindling.

When beneficial bacteria decrease, the body's internal environment changes, and it shows up in that strange smell we commonly call old person smell. So without extra supplementation, an older person's flora is naturally unbalanced. We sometimes say a baby should not sleep in the same bed as a grandparent. That is because older people carry more harmful bacteria, which can affect the baby's health.

A fair amount comes off through the skin and the airways, unless you give the older person enough probiotics. So these two groups, mothers and the elderly, are the ones we pay special attention to. Probiotics also play an important role in our immune system. For years the word "immunity" has seemed rather abstract. You do not know how to judge whether your immunity is good; you cannot feel it. Let me give you a rough way to judge. Normally the body's flora is stable, and that stability lies in a balance between the flora and the immune system. When the flora is complete in number and variety, the immune system is well protected.

In the gut there are [[roughly 100 trillion bacteria|The 100 trillion figure is the traditional estimate for the gut microbiota. A review of gut bacteria and human health uses it and describes how these bacteria affect digestion, immunity, and even brain function.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4425030/]], harmful and beneficial alike, and normally the large intestine does not become inflamed. As we know, [[70 percent of the body's white blood cells are in the large intestine|The immunology literature describes gut-associated lymphoid tissue as almost 70 percent of the entire immune system. The speaker phrases it as white blood cells; the underlying idea is the same.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2515351/]], maintaining its health and keeping it from inflaming. Most of our immune cells are in the large intestine, so in general the large intestine is healthy and does not inflame.

But turn that around. If your gut is frequently inflamed, if you often have loose bowels, what does that tell you? That your immunity is not enough. It is the beginning and the sign of low immunity. Why does a child keep getting upset stomachs? His immunity is not enough. What is he short of? There is no medicine called "immunity." What he actually needs is to start with probiotics.

From this angle, understand the gut as a barometer of the body's immunity. If your gut is not doing well, it definitely means your immunity cannot keep up, because 70 percent of immunity is in the gut. If that much immune strength is stationed in the gut and still cannot keep it healthy, if your gut is always inflamed, then your immunity is quite low. Probiotics are one of the fastest ways to change immunity. When we take probiotics, they prompt the cells around the intestinal wall to produce more immune cells, and your immunity starts to rise.

So the fastest way to raise immunity is probiotics. It is very direct. The next group is children. Children often have allergies, and some often have eczema. Most of these symptoms come from a flora imbalance. Where is the root? The mother did not pass on enough flora. So children need to be given flora promptly and in sufficient amounts.

With these ideas in place, let us turn to today's topic: the gut microecology, and the relationship between gut flora and us. Microecology research is relatively new in nutrition science. It concerns microorganisms, and especially the microecology of the gut. We know the gut's microecology matters greatly to human health; we simply did not pay enough attention to it before.

In 2006 the [[United Nations health organization|The speaker likely means the World Health Organization. He gives no specific document, and I was not able to identify a particular 2006 initiative, so treat the date as the speaker's recollection.|]] began calling on people to study microecology, and you can see that in the years since, results in this field have multiplied, including the research on the various gut flora we discussed earlier. Why study the human microecology at all?

Our earlier research on the body's bacteria was relatively narrow. We assumed that since it is a bacterium, you have to destroy it. Later research found that bacteria are almost impossible to eliminate. Even if all of humanity disappeared, bacteria would not. So where we once studied flora from the angle of fighting it and treating disease, now we study it from the angle of symbiosis, of a shared win.

For example, many city parents, when teaching their children hygiene, used to insist on a sterile environment, terrified the child would touch a speck of dirt, sterilizing every bowl and pair of chopsticks. Country children, by contrast, crawl around in the soil and the adults are not so particular about cleanliness. When they grow up, research finds that children raised in the countryside have fewer allergies than children raised in cities. So the current view is that bacteria can do a certain amount of damage to the body, but they also do it good.

Another obvious example is what we call "not acclimatized" stomach trouble: when we travel from one place to another and eat different food, we easily get diarrhea. What is the most important reason? It is not the food. It is the flora. People in every region have different gut flora. Someone living by the sea and someone living in the mountains have different gut flora. Someone who eats seafood and meat and someone who has long eaten vegetables and fruit have different gut flora.

So when you go somewhere else, because the flora is different, a change in diet easily produces symptoms. This is why so many people now have what is called food intolerance. Why? Because the food you ate as a child and the food you eat as an adult may have changed a great deal in character. Does the tomato salad you ate as a child taste the same as the one you eat today?

Our digestive system adapted to the food of our childhood. If as adults we do not choose our food accordingly, food intolerance easily develops. The body may not feel anything obvious for a while, but over the long term all sorts of reactions appear, and this is an important reason so many chronic diseases emerge.

So it is best when flora and body can live together in symbiosis. We tend to see bacteria as lowly life forms, but they are extremely powerful. Living bacteria have been found in volcanic rock, at the poles, in deep-sea glaciers. They tolerate extreme cold and extreme heat, and high pressure as well.

The number of bacteria in our bodies is more than ten times the number of our cells, and what matters most is that every one of them carries genetic material. Physiologists have even proposed that they are the body's real [[metagenome|宏基因组: the combined genetic material of all the microbes living in and on a person. The bacterial genes outnumber human genes many times over, which is why some researchers describe the microbiome as a second genome.|]]. A person's habits, emotions and thinking, the diseases that appear, the dietary pattern, every aspect is influenced by these bacteria.

How do we understand that? From another angle, you could say these microbes treat our bodies as one big petri dish. They are simply cultivating their own species inside us, and in doing so they influence our personality, our physical condition, our habits, our emotions, our diet, and so on.

Some people are hot-tempered and some are steady, and that is related to the different strains they carry. Some people love meat and some prefer vegetables. We assume it is personal taste, but research finds it is not really that we like these foods; it is that our gut bacteria like what we like. It is not even decided by our brain; it is decided by the flora.

Say there is one food you especially love. I especially love durian. Is that decided by my brain? No. The brain does not decide what we like to eat. It seems as if the things we like are inborn, but they are not inborn either. Your flora decides.

So human research here is only just beginning, and it has taken some wrong turns. Consider the fuss a few years ago over so-called genetically modified foods. When you eat GM food, is the experience good or bad? To this day there is no verdict. It may have little effect on our own coding genes, but it deeply affects our gut bacteria, and through them it can have significant effects on the body. For now we cannot control that effect in a positive direction, so for now the view is: if you cannot control it, better not to eat GM foods yet.

So we do not know whether GM food is good or bad. But GM technology and future genetic research will certainly continue, just as research into nuclear fusion and fission produced atomic and hydrogen bombs. That does not mean fusion and fission are wrong; they may be used in power stations or in other energy sources that advance humanity. GM technology is the same. The technology itself is neither good nor bad.

Back to microecology. There are large populations of flora in our mouth, on our skin, in the digestive tract, the airways, and the female reproductive tract, and above all in the microecology of the gut, where the microbes number as many as a hundred trillion. More than 99 percent are bacteria. They live in numbers above 100 trillion, in 500 to 1,000 different species.

These enormous numbers of bacteria fall into three broad categories: beneficial, harmful, and neutral. We mentioned all three earlier. The beneficial bacteria, also called probiotics, are mainly the various [[Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus|双歧杆菌 and 乳酸杆菌: the two bacterial groups most used in probiotic products. Bifidobacteria are among the first microbes to colonize the infant gut and were first isolated from breast-fed infants in 1899.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4908950/]] species. They are indispensable to health. With them present, various vitamins can be synthesized; they take part in digesting food, promote gut motility, suppress the growth of pathogens, and break down harmful and toxic substances. Once their numbers fall short, all kinds of disease and toxic, harmful, even carcinogenic substances go unchecked.

So their presence deeply affects our immune system, which we have discussed before. What are the neutral bacteria? They are bacteria with a double role, such as Escherichia coli and the enterococci. Under normal conditions they are beneficial to health.

But once they move from the gut to other parts of the body, they can cause many other problems.
