# Aging, Children, and Taking Back Responsibility

Hello, everyone. This is our sixth lesson. Yesterday we said that the nutritional situation of today's children and teenagers is genuinely worrying. Where exactly does the problem come from? From the wrong health mindset. You often see a mother in the morning wondering what breakfast to give her child: rice porridge, steamed buns, stuffed buns, an egg, and then nothing else. If that goes on for years, that child's health will certainly develop all sorts of problems. Behind all this data about young people, what is really being reflected is a set of problems: the mother does not understand, and does not know what to do, especially about the immunity of many of these children.

Faced with a cold or a fever, many mothers do not know what to do. All they can do is treat the head when the head hurts and treat the foot when the foot hurts. Whatever comes up, they haul the child to the hospital and leave everything else for the doctor to handle. Are you satisfied with that? Of course not. So the central problem now is that today's parents do not have modern knowledge about raising children. You are about to become a mother, and is there any training for being a mother? None of us who became parents in the past was ever trained. What training could parenting possibly need? But the environment has changed, and it is very different from before. If you do not get trained, do not learn some health knowledge, do not understand nutrition, it really will not do.

There is another problem, the aging of the population. In 2020, that is last year, China entered the severe stage of population aging. The group over sixty-five reached [[270 million people|The speaker's figure is close to China's seventh national census, taken in 2020, which counted 260 million people aged 60 and over, 18.7 percent of the population, and 190 million aged 65 and over, 13.5 percent.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11681201/]] in 2020. Two hundred and seventy million. That is severe aging.

So our situation now is that we have grown old before we grew rich. Our aging is happening very fast. Compare us with other countries: when developed countries entered the aging stage, their GDP per capita was generally between five thousand and eight thousand US dollars. But China became an aging country back in 2003, when our GDP per capita was only a thousand dollars. Look at how long the transition into aging took around the world: France took 115 years, Switzerland 85, Britain 80, the United States 60. China took only 18 years to reach the aging stage.

So 2020 was the beginning of our severe aging stage. In other words, we are already old, but we have not prepared for being old, and we have not prepared for it mentally. When problems come up we do not know how to solve them, so what can we rely on? Entirely on the hospital and the doctor. But can a hospital solve the problem of slowing down your aging? That is impossible.

And now many families that had a single child are allowed a second one, but with the financial pressure on the household, many people do not dare to have one. When one elderly person falls ill, doesn't the whole family have to look after them? You will find that when four grandparents take turns being ill, two young people have to take turns caring for them. And if the generation above them is still alive, because people live longer now, then it is not four elderly people, it is eight. Two young people taking turns to care for all of them. What do we do? Have many of you thought about what we will do when we face that future? Many people say, well, we will take medicine. In their minds there is nothing but medicine. Say someone has high blood pressure, and you suggest taking some nutrients and working on bringing the pressure down. He says, I will just take my medicine. Why?

Because medicine costs him nothing. He can get it through medical insurance. So which do you actually want: to take medicine, or to get your high blood pressure under proper control? These are all mindset problems, and they reveal that many of us have not prepared for growing old. Can medicine solve every problem? Clearly not. So why is medicine the only thing we know when our health goes wrong? You suggest taking some nutrients and many people find it expensive. What they do not realize is that when you are admitted to the hospital, having your family member at your bedside every day is the most expensive thing of all, because while they are sitting with you they cannot be creating any economic value. So everyone should work this arithmetic out clearly and understand where the real value of health lies.

Looking at all of this, we can see that we ought to prepare for our own old age, or rather prepare for our own health. It is not that there are no good things around us, and not that there are no good nutritional products that benefit our health, and not that we have no money. The key is whether we are willing to change our mindset. And where does change come from? It comes from receiving education in health management. What is more, we are not only to be educated and to receive education. One day we must turn around and influence more people, so that they change too.

In fact, facing the aging trend ahead, everyone in our country, from the young to the old and from top to bottom, needs to learn and be educated in family health management. And in the process of receiving that education, we can also do a very good job of influencing the next generation.

As parents, we all want our children to be outstanding and to do well at school. But you find that some children are simply mischievous. From an education standpoint that is one thing, but from a nutrition standpoint, you need to know that when a child's nutrition is inadequate, his brain leaks current. It is not that he does not want to concentrate in class. It is that around the developing nerves of the brain there is a [[myelin sheath|髓鞘. Myelin is the fatty layer that wraps nerve fibers and insulates them so signals travel fast. Vitamin B12 is needed to build and repair it, and deficiency causes demyelination with numbness and loss of position sense.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6930825/]], which is our brain's insulation. If our nutrition is inadequate, the insulation leaks, and the child becomes very jumpy and cannot sit still and listen to the teacher.

So sometimes we have to look at a child from two angles. Not only from the angle of discipline and education, but also thinking harder about which nutrients we have failed to give him. Are we able to see whether a child's temperament is settled or unsettled? We cannot simply go by appearances and keep nagging the child to concentrate in class and finish his homework. We really do need to understand some of the nutritional knowledge about nerves. The nerves need nutrients, not drugs, so you have to know what the nutrients are and what the relationship is between a lack of certain nutrients and a child's behavior. That is essential. It is something a competent mother has to have.

Here I want to tell you about my son. He is seven and a half this year. He has an outgoing personality and a very active mind, and he loves Lego and making things by hand.

Early last year, when our whole family went to Dubai on holiday, I bought him a Lego set there, the Burj Khalifa, the tallest building in the city. We brought it home. Before long my son came over and said, Dad, look, I have finished building it, do you want to come and see? I went over, and it really was well done. But then I turned my head and saw that the instruction sheet was still sealed in its plastic bag, unopened. So I asked him, how did you build it? The instructions are still in the bag. Did you build this without looking at the instructions? My son said, Dad, I do not need the instructions. As soon as I opened the box I knew how to build it. So I opened the bag a little and checked the instructions against what he had built, and it really was exactly right.

Here is another thing. Once I brought home a small table from IKEA. I opened it up and got ready to assemble it myself with the instructions in front of me. My son came running over and squatted on the floor to look at the instructions with me. We looked at them for a while, and then I started building, following the diagram step by step, tightening a screw, attaching a leg, reading one step and doing one step.

After he had looked at it, my son said, Dad, it is not that complicated, let me put it together for you. And he did the whole thing in one go, almost finishing the little table.

The important part is that through the whole process he did not look at the instructions a second time. He was not reading one step and building one step. He looked through the whole diagram once, put it aside, and then assembled the table.

I was quite astonished at the time, so I asked him, when you build this table, don't you read a step and do a step? That is what I do. He said, Dad, I do not need to. I read the whole sheet through once, put it down, and then I am done. After that, I thought my son was pretty clever. But afterwards I wondered, can it all be put down to being clever or not clever? I do not think so, because behind cleverness what is really needed is the core support of nutrients.

As parents, should we not learn which vitamins nourish the nerves? How expensive can it be to spend a little money on the best brand? At the least we should know the relationship between the B vitamins and a child's nerve development. Vitamin B12, for example, is indispensable to the nervous system, because it takes part in forming a kind of lipoprotein in nerve tissue. We need to have this knowledge. We also need to know about the calming effect of calcium and magnesium on the nerves. If you want your child to settle down, if you want him not to lose his temper, you have to give him enough raw material. And we need to know that if the breakfast we give our children has no adequate protein, only carbohydrates, only vegetables, or only so-called high protein that is not high-quality plant protein, then once the morning passes 10:30 that child has no energy left to support concentration in class, because the oxygen-carrying capacity of his blood has fallen.

The air in a school classroom is limited. Whichever child has plenty of hemoglobin in his blood and strong oxygen-carrying capacity will take the lion's share of that limited oxygen. The other children are not unwilling to concentrate. Their nutrients cannot support them in concentrating. And there is another situation in China now. More and more people say, when I am old I will move into a nursing home, and then someone will take care of me, won't they? Let us ask whether a nursing home really takes care of everything. A friend of mine had a mother in a nursing home. If she had high blood pressure, or any other condition, the home would call him immediately and say that he had to take his mother to the hospital for a checkup himself, because the home would not handle it.

So old-age care, and health, are in the end matters of being responsible for yourself. You cannot push all of your health onto the hospital or onto a care institution. Our country can no longer carry that load.

Another thing is that in recent years we have all seen news of celebrities dying of cancer. We are not here to rake over their affairs. We use news like that only to ask ourselves more questions. When our own bodies go wrong, especially with cancer, will the hospital we find be better than the hospital those celebrities found?

No. And most of us do not earn more than those celebrities either. They were not cured, so what grounds do we have for believing that the hospital we find will cure us? That is not realistic.

Take the television host Li Yong, whom we all know. The hospital where he spent his final time in America is one of the world's top hospitals for cancer treatment, the last word in cancer care. It is the most authoritative cancer hospital in the world, with the most authoritative cancer specialists and scholars. When heads of state, celebrities and business leaders anywhere in the world have a cancer or a tumor and go to that hospital, that is the end of the line.

And what was the result? After seventeen months, he was gone all the same. In saying this I am not telling you not to go to hospital. I am saying that when we do go, shouldn't we also take some responsibility ourselves? Hospitals do not much like people who take no responsibility for their own health either. If we all took a little more responsibility for ourselves, wouldn't relations between patients and doctors be much better too?

Another well-known figure is [[Kai-Fu Lee|李开复, the technology executive and writer. He was diagnosed with stage four lymphoma in 2013, changed his diet, sleep and work habits during treatment, and wrote a memoir, published in Chinese as 向死而生, "Living Toward Death".|]], who works in education. He also developed cancer, but besides going to hospital himself, what else did he do? He changed his own living habits and worked on his body. He began eating a vegetarian diet, set his work aside for a time, let his mind relax, and began to regulate his sleep. In the end he recovered, and he wrote a book about it.

So it is not that we should not go to hospital. It is that we are telling ourselves that a hospital alone cannot solve the problem, because [[no case of cancer has ever been cured by a hospital alone|This is the speaker's view, stated strongly. WHO's position is that many cancers can be cured when found early and treated well, and that some, including testicular seminoma and several childhood leukemias and lymphomas, have high cure rates even when advanced.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer]]. Remember that, not one case. Everyone who has recovered has in the end done it by regulating their own immune system. I think you all understand this. Even if a hospital wipes out every one of your cancer cells, that does not mean you are well. Take the singer Yao Beina. In 2011 she was found to have breast cancer. She had a mastectomy, she had reconstruction, she had radiotherapy and chemotherapy, and her body recovered its health. The hospital formally declared her restored to health. Then at the end of 2014 she suddenly worsened. She was admitted on December 26, and by January 8 she was gone. We have to ask why it went so fast from relapse to death, and why a relapse left nothing more to be done.

What it means is that when a person has cancer, their immunity is already extremely low. Any medical procedure that so-called cures them has only cured them by the definition used in medicine. The person's immunity has not been raised, and that is why it comes back, and comes back on a large scale. So who must the raising of immunity depend on? On yourself. We have to understand this ourselves. When we say we must raise our own immunity, we are not saying we should rely on the hospital. We are saying we have to carry our own share of responsibility. We cannot depend on the hospital alone, and some people even depend on insurance. Before Li Yong died, insurance companies had him buy policies, and in the end he bought quite a few, with a payout of more than a hundred million yuan. Was it any use? No use at all. What does insurance insure? Insurance is tied to medical institutions. It is not tied to what you eat every day, and it is not tied to your immunity.

If our three meals a day do not produce nutrition and do not produce health, does insurance cover that? Can insurance be tied to breakfast? Insurance takes no interest. Insurance only starts paying you once you walk out of your front door and head for the hospital. But if our thinking and our [[health quotient|健商, "health quotient", a Chinese coinage modeled on IQ and EQ, meaning a person's working knowledge of health and their ability to act on it.|]] have made no progress, then sometimes the more money insurance pays out, the faster we die.

You often see those online fundraisers, the crowdfunding appeals where someone in the family is ill but there is no money for treatment, so they start a campaign online, collect the money, and send the family member to hospital. Sometimes, with no professional knowledge, the more money there is, the faster it is spent, the faster it is used up, and the faster the person even dies. I heard of a case like this. A man's mother-in-law had lung cancer, so they rushed her to hospital for treatment, and within a week she had died. Afterwards an expert who understood nutrition asked him, you must have used the best drugs in the world. The man said, of course, money is no problem for me, my business is quite large, so naturally I had to give her the best drugs, the best in the world if I could.

How could she be gone in a week? The man said she had seemed quite all right on the first and second days of the medication, and then on the third day she failed. The expert told him that if he had not used such very good drugs, she might have lived longer. Precisely because he used the so-called better drugs, she went faster. Why?

Everyone knows that the so-called better drugs must have very strong killing power. Cancer cells or the body's own antibody-producing cells, they wipe out everything.

On the first and second day, perhaps hardly any cancer cells were left alive and the patient rallied a little. Then on the third day the immune system collapsed completely. So what did this person really die of?

The collapse of the immune system. So it is not that having money to buy the best drugs solves the problem. Think of the Thousand-Hand Guanyin dance at the 2005 Spring Festival Gala, with twenty-one deaf performers. Not all twenty-one were born deaf. Nineteen of them lost their hearing because of adverse drug reactions. So of China's ten million deaf people, [[seven million were made deaf by medication|A widely quoted Chinese figure. What is well documented is that aminoglycoside antibiotics, once heavily used in China, can cause permanent hearing loss, and that people carrying certain mitochondrial gene variants are far more susceptible. We could not verify the seven million figure itself.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7263198/]]. In the past, when we used these drugs, we may not have known what side effects they had. But if we still do not know now, problems will certainly follow. I think everyone understands. We all have online encyclopedias now, so you can look up any drug and see what it does and what side effects it has. We should all know something about this ourselves.

Because when we go to hospital now, clinical medicine only runs you through the standard procedure. A patient arrives with certain symptoms, and the hospital runs the standard procedure: the tests that should be done are done, the drugs that should be used are used, and whether you live or die in the end is your own responsibility.

In most cases the hospital only runs the standard procedure. Whether it is an inflammation or a cancer, it runs the standard procedure, because the hospital does not need to take on any more responsibility or risk than that.

Some family members of patients will go to the doctor privately and say, I am begging you, this illness is too painful, this treatment is unbearable.

Could you change the method, could you change the treatment? The doctor says no, this is the standard procedure, I follow the standard procedure. And what is the standard procedure? It is what guarantees the highest rate of success overall, but it does not guarantee any single individual. So in the end it comes back to us: we ourselves need to understand more about our own health, and to learn this discipline of family health management well, so that we do not hand our health over to the hospital entirely. When everyone learns about health, only then can everyone reach a comfortable life. That is all for today. Tomorrow we will begin talking about what health management is. Thank you, everyone.
