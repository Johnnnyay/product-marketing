# Bifidobacterium: The Gut's Essential Resident

Today we discuss Bifidobacterium, the centerpiece of the probiotics section. There will be more academic terms than usual today, and some of it may sound dry. That is all right. If you do not follow every term, it will not stop you from understanding today's content, and I will keep it as plain as I can. Bifidobacterium is the name of a bacterial genus, the genus Bifidobacterium.

Let us start with the encyclopedia entry to get a general picture. Bifidobacterium is a [[Gram-positive, non-motile|Gram-positive means the bacteria hold a violet stain in the standard laboratory test, which reflects a thick cell wall. Non-motile means they do not swim. "Strictly anaerobic" means they cannot grow in the presence of oxygen, which is why they live deep in the large intestine.|]] bacterium, rod-shaped, sometimes forked at one end, and strictly anaerobic. Those two sentences are just for your ears; no need to dig into them. The genus is found widely in the digestive tract, the vagina, and the mouth of humans and animals. Bacteria of this genus are an important part of the gut flora of humans and animals, and two especially important species are Bifidobacterium lactis and Bifidobacterium infantis.

As early as [[1899, a pediatrician at the Pasteur Institute in France|Henry Tissier isolated bifidobacteria from the feces of breast-fed infants in 1899, as the scientific literature on the genus records. Bifidobacteria are among the first microbes to colonize the infant gut and dominate the gut of healthy breast-fed babies.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4908950/]] isolated an anaerobic, Gram-positive strain from the stool of healthy breast-fed infants and named it Bifidobacterium. Later it was found that this strain could treat intestinal infections, and after that more members of the genus were discovered. Later still, its physiological functions were investigated in depth.

After more than a century of development, and especially with the recent growth in research on the gut microbiome, which is the gut microecology we discussed earlier, people have increasingly recognized how important Bifidobacterium is. It is now used widely in medicine and in food. Bifidobacterium is a physiological beneficial bacterium. What does that mean?

It means the body's physiological activities cannot do without it. If all our physiological activity is to be healthy, Bifidobacterium must be there doing its part. For human health it provides a biological barrier and a nutritional function, as well as anti-tumor, immune-strengthening, gut-improving, and anti-aging functions, among other important physiological roles. All of these require the Bifidobacterium strains.

A great many microorganisms reside in the human gut, and they are intimately related to health. From what we have learned, gut microbes affect health in three categories: beneficial, harmless, and harmful.

Under normal conditions the gut microbes form a relatively balanced state. But how does that balance get broken? For example, you take antibiotics, you undergo radiotherapy or chemotherapy, you are emotionally depressed, your body is weakened, your immunity falls. All of these throw the gut flora out of balance. Some gut microbes then overgrow in the gut and produce harmful substances.

Bifidobacterium and the lactic acid bacteria we discussed earlier can suppress the growth of these harmful bacteria, resist infection by pathogens, process and synthesize the vitamins the body needs, promote absorption of the various minerals, and produce acetic acid, propionic acid, butyric acid, lactic acid, and other organic acids. None of that happens without these beneficial bacteria.

We know that these acids are what stimulate the gut, and a stimulated gut moves. Once it moves, bowel movements become normal. So these acids prevent constipation, suppress putrefaction in the gut, clean up the gut environment, break down carcinogens, stimulate the immune system, and raise resistance to disease. They play an important part in all of this.

Specifically, how does Bifidobacterium work? It shows up in ten areas. First, it suppresses the growth of putrefying bacteria. When there is a high concentration of Bifidobacterium in the gut, invading pathogens such as Escherichia coli, Shigella, Salmonella typhi, Proteus, Staphylococcus aureus, and Candida albicans, along with some of the body's own putrefying organisms, are held in check.

Bifidobacterium can rapidly colonize the intestinal mucosa. Plainly, what is colonizing? It is settling down and making a home on your gut lining. It forms a barrier of beneficial bacteria, spread densely across the mucosa as a bacterial film, so the pathogens have no room to colonize. A comparison makes it easier: if your gut is fully occupied by good people, the bad people have nowhere to live and nowhere to settle. That is the first area.

Second, the acetic acid and lactic acid that Bifidobacterium produces lower the pH of the gut. Once the pH drops, harmful bacteria have a hard time surviving, because putrefying bacteria prefer places where the pH is high.

Third, Bifidobacterium produces proteins similar to bacteriocins, which have a bactericidal effect. How to understand that? Roughly, it produces protein-like substances that kill bacteria. We will not go further; a general awareness is enough, because if I expanded on it we would never finish this lesson.

Fourth, Bifidobacterium breaks [[conjugated bile acids down into free bile acids|偶联型胆汁酸 and 游离型胆汁酸. Bile acids are made in the liver and released into the gut attached to an amino acid (conjugated). Some gut bacteria remove that attachment (deconjugation), and the resulting free bile acids are more hostile to many other bacteria.|]]. Those two terms are also technical; just listen. What matters is to remember that this kind of acid strongly suppresses putrefying bacteria, so Bifidobacterium is able to suppress their growth in our large intestine. We know that when the large intestine has many putrefying bacteria, it has many toxins. What does "many toxins" look like? Your gas smells especially foul. When your waste smells very bad, it means the harmful bacteria in your gut are numerous. When the smell is not strong, the beneficial bacteria are numerous. That is a very simple way to judge.

Fifth is its nutritional role. Bifidobacterium can synthesize a certain amount of vitamin B1, B2, and B6 in the gut, as well as vitamin K, and it can also synthesize alanine, aspartic acid, and threonine there, amino acids the body requires. So it processes the nutrients we take in, and this has real significance for the nutrition of pregnant and nursing women, children, and the elderly.

Bifidobacterium also secretes a variety of digestive enzymes that promote the breakdown and absorption of food. Without these enzymes, the various foods we eat cannot be digested and absorbed.

It also maintains a mildly acidic environment in the gut. Why does that matter? Because only in a mildly acidic environment are calcium, iron, and vitamin D absorbed well, which in turn helps prevent iron-deficiency anemia and rickets.

Bifidobacterium also ferments lactose to produce galactose. We know galactose is a component of the brain's nervous system, and in particular, after birth, when the brain is growing rapidly, it especially needs galactose.

When the same function operates in an adult, it is no longer about brain development. It is about improving how well people with lactose intolerance digest and absorb dairy products.

That is why people eat yogurt in daily life. Many yogurts contain some flora, so yogurt is generally considered easier to absorb than milk.

The sixth function is anti-cancer. A large body of animal experiments shows that taking in Bifidobacterium, live or even dead, raises the body's antibody levels and activates the phagocytic activity of macrophages. What do macrophages do? They engulf infectious material from outside. They are part of our immunity. So Bifidobacterium plays a major role in raising the body's resistance to infection, and in preventing, suppressing, and even killing tumor cells.

We know the human gut contains a great many harmful bacteria, and to guard against their bad effects, where is [[70 percent of the body's immune material|Gut-associated lymphoid tissue is described in the immunology literature as almost 70 percent of the entire immune system.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2515351/]]? In the gut. So when Bifidobacterium increases, it stimulates these immune cells and raises antibody levels, which is why its effect on immunity is so noticeable. This is why we keep saying that if you want to raise immunity quickly with functional foods, the first choice is always probiotics; the speed is remarkable. That includes cases I have handled personally and things I have seen: many children, after an infection, raise their immunity quickly by taking probiotics, only the amount must be sufficient. It cannot be small. We know that the putrefying bacteria in the gut produce many carcinogens during their metabolism, and some convert pre-carcinogens directly into carcinogens. How to understand that?

Some substances are bad but not directly carcinogenic. These putrefying and harmful bacteria can turn them into villains, into carcinogens. Bifidobacterium does precisely the opposite. It suppresses those putrefying bacteria that turn neutral substances into carcinogens, and so it has a certain cancer-preventing role. In that opposite process it also directly absorbs and eliminates the toxic effects of these carcinogens, and in particular it suppresses mutation, which is a wonderful thing, because it effectively reduces the potential for cancer to develop.

The peptidoglycan on the cell walls of Bifidobacterium, along with its phospholipids, teichoic acid, and polysaccharides, all have anti-tumor effects. That is the sixth point.

Seventh, it protects the liver. We know the putrefying bacteria produce many metabolic products, and these toxins all travel through the portal vein to the liver, which uses acid to neutralize them. They are then excreted in the urine as salts. What if the liver cannot detoxify in time? It leads to disordered liver function and disruption of the circulatory system. What does that look like in daily life? Your sleep quality suffers badly. So many people's poor sleep is not necessarily a calcium shortage. It is that the liver cannot detoxify in time, which disrupts its own function, which then affects the nervous system, which causes the sleep disorder.

For older people in particular, and stressed middle-aged people, what do you do about poor sleep? Add calcium on one side and add probiotics on the other. Please note this. What does Bifidobacterium do here? It suppresses the growth of putrefying bacteria and corrects the imbalance in the gut flora. Once the flora is balanced, your ability to absorb nutrients comes up, you notice your gas is no longer so foul, which means fewer toxins, and the burden on your liver lightens.

So indirectly it improves liver function. As we said, it lowers the gut's pH, and once the pH drops, ammonia shifts into a form that is hard to absorb. Ammonia in quantity damages the liver. This has been shown clinically: in patients with cirrhosis and chronic hepatitis, long-term use of Bifidobacterium lowered the blood levels of harmful substances such as phenols and indican. No need to memorize those terms; a general awareness is enough.

Researchers also found that in particularly thin, frail people, when Bifidobacterium in the stool increased, appetite gradually improved, weight slowly increased, and protein metabolism returned to the normal range. Why is a person thin and frail? A problem in protein metabolism. Why is a person obese? A problem in fat metabolism.

Eighth, Bifidobacterium regulates gastrointestinal function. It secretes large amounts of [[short-chain fatty acids|短链脂肪酸: acetic, propionic, and butyric acid, produced when gut bacteria ferment fiber. They lower the pH of the colon, feed the cells of the gut lining, and stimulate motility.|]], which make the gut mildly acidic. What does that achieve? It controls the abnormal fermentation caused by harmful bacteria. In a mildly acidic environment the gut's osmotic pressure rises, which stimulates motility and promotes bowel movements. This has been tested clinically in people with severe flora imbalance, who get bacterial diarrhea at the slightest provocation.

After about two weeks of Bifidobacterium, the shape and frequency of the patients' stools returned to normal and the diarrhea disappeared. What was the overall effectiveness rate? Ninth, it lowers cholesterol. Bifidobacterium's cholesterol-lowering effect has been supported by both in vivo and in vitro experiments. The one pity is that the mechanism is still not settled. We see the phenomenon, that it lowers cholesterol, but the principle has not been finally determined.

There are three main views. Let me lay them out and you can see whether they make sense. The first is the absorption theory, the second the precipitation theory, and the third the theory that it inhibits the cholesterol synthesis pathway. Treat these three as general knowledge; no need to dig in. The tenth function of Bifidobacterium is anti-aging. We know the body contains large numbers of free radicals. What is a free radical? An atom missing one electron. Let me put it in a picture so it is easier to grasp. Why are free radicals harmful? Because a free radical is a bachelor who, having no ambition and no goal in life, cannot find a wife. But the atoms in every cell of our body have their positive and negative charges paired off, husband and wife, each a complete family.

When a free radical enters the body, being a bachelor short of one electron, it goes around stealing wives. It starts wrecking the atoms in our healthy cells, breaking up couples, which makes the whole society, that is, our body, extremely unstable.

So free radicals have long been recognized as harmful substances produced during metabolism, and they accelerate the aging and death of cells. If the body has enough Bifidobacterium, it noticeably increases the activity and quantity of [[superoxide dismutase|超氧化物歧化酶, usually abbreviated SOD. An enzyme the body makes that neutralizes a common type of free radical (superoxide). It is one of the body's main antioxidant defenses.|]] in the blood. When the activity and quantity of this enzyme increase, the oxidative reactions involving free radicals decrease, meaning the free radicals are no longer allowed to steal wives everywhere, and the aging of the body slows noticeably. So Bifidobacterium plays a very clear role in the body's anti-aging process.

Bifidobacterium also directly suppresses the growth of putrefying bacteria in the gut, reduces their metabolism and the toxic substances they produce, prevents them from harming the body, and adjusts and improves the composition of the gut flora, which fundamentally lowers the likelihood of intestinal tumors, inflammation, constipation, and cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease.
