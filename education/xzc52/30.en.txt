Now let us go on with vitamin C. Many important biosynthetic processes in the body require vitamin C, and cannot proceed without it. Most mammals make their own vitamin C in the liver, so they have no deficiency problem. Humans are different. We are primates, and primates cannot make vitamin C for themselves, so it has to come from food. In the body, vitamin C is a highly efficient antioxidant, and its first role is antioxidant protection.

When antioxidants come up, I imagine a bad character immediately springs to mind: [[free radicals|自由基. Highly reactive molecules produced during normal metabolism and by smoke, pollution and radiation. When they outnumber the body's antioxidant defenses, the resulting oxidative stress damages proteins, fats and DNA, a process linked to aging and to chronic disease.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5551541/]]. Free radicals affect the body in every area of our lives. Take a tumor, high blood pressure, dementia, a stroke, diabetes, or inflammation in the body. What is the root cause? Large numbers of free radicals produced in the organs. You could say free radicals are the chief culprit affecting our health in every direction.

Even when we have no disease at all, the process of human aging is oxidation by the oxygen in the air, and in that process the body constantly generates free radicals. If you also smoke and drink, you will produce more free radicals than a normal person. So exactly how do free radicals harm the body?

As a person eats food and takes in air, they come into contact with many free radicals that attack by oxidation. These highly oxidizing free radicals attack the body. What do they attack?

They attack the proteins in the body. Our hair, our skin, our eyes and our tissues and organs are all protein. They also attack the enzymes in our bodies and the DNA in our cells. And if DNA is attacked by free radicals often enough, what happens? The DNA mutates. What does mutation mean? It means being cut into fragments by free radicals. Free radicals also attack fatty acids, causing fats to oxidize. And what is the harm in oxidized fat? It causes serious problems on the surface of our blood vessels and in the blood itself. It is the prime culprit behind heart and cerebrovascular disease.

After this attack, a whole series of health problems appears. In daily life, what conditions produce free radicals easily? The smoking and drinking I just mentioned, and also strong ultraviolet light and air pollution. In the north of China, when winter comes, what does everyone talk about? Smog. Fog and haze are not the same thing. Fog is a large quantity of tiny water droplets or ice crystals suspended in the air. It mainly creates a visual obstacle, blurring your vision and cutting visibility. But haze is more harmful than fog.

[[Haze|WHO describes fine particulate matter, PM2.5, as the standard measure of air pollution, made up of sulfates, nitrates, ammonia, sodium chloride, black carbon, mineral dust and water. The particles penetrate deep into the lungs and the bloodstream, and WHO links them to heart disease, stroke, chronic lung disease, respiratory infections and cancer.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ambient-(outdoor)-air-quality-and-health]] also contains dust and particles of sulfuric acid, nitric acid and other compounds. It does not merely block your vision. Its harm is to oxidize your body directly. Every tissue exposed to the air, including the eyes, the skin and the mucous membranes, is attacked directly and has no defense. So smog carries many factors that harm our health, and it drives the body to produce more free radicals.

Then there are fried and grilled foods, and the additives in the foods we buy at the supermarket. All of these are sources of free radicals. So you see, our bodies are under attack from free radicals twenty four hours a day. The one people most easily overlook is the problem of excessive additives in food. Many people no longer steam their own buns at home and buy them outside instead. Sometimes you see buns at a small stall that are steamed to a brilliant white. Hold a sheet of white A4 paper against them and they are whiter than the paper. Should flour normally be that white? It should not, should it?

Normal dough has a pale yellowish color. In the same way, few of us roll our own noodles at home anymore. If you see noodles for sale that are extremely white, a whitening agent may have been added. And be even more careful with the pickles, dried meat and dried bean curd sold in small packets at the supermarket. Why do those packaged foods not spoil for such a long time? There must be preservatives inside. With preservatives, the food can sit for a long time without going bad. We have all gotten used to this and no longer find it strange. But think carefully: are these foods normal?

It is precisely because modern foods contain these excessive additives that our bodies are so easily oxidized.

If you ask what the root cause of many chronic diseases, tumors and cancers is, it is hard to point to a single specific reason. In fact, they are the result of your body being oxidized day after day over a long life. Hearing me say this, you may think you now have to steam your own buns and roll your own noodles at home. That is not what I mean, and I doubt you could manage it even if you wanted to.

But today's reality is that we are subject to all kinds of oxidative attack. What we can do is take in antioxidant foods to offset the process, to let the body be oxidized a little more slowly, so that you stay further away from chronic and metabolic disease. We are not competing over lifespan. We are competing over whose body oxidizes more slowly. So we have to know that in the human body vitamin C is a highly efficient antioxidant. Vitamin C does many things, but its single most important role is antioxidant protection, helping us reduce the damage free radicals do.

We all know that every breath in and out is a process of oxidation, and that oxidation is a process of producing more and more free radicals. When free radicals increase, what do they turn into? Inflammation. Inflammation that does not resolve, even repeated infection, means repeated free radical attack. And one day, when those free radicals damage your DNA and destroy the information your cells use to divide and copy themselves correctly, they act like a hacker who steals and then destroys the core information for cell replication in your body. It is as if your core technology had been taken and the website of your body had been hacked. The information for cell division becomes a set of wrong orders. Your body's headquarters sent out a correct instruction, but a spy, the free radical, intercepted it and rewrote it into nonsense, and that is what reaches the troops at the front. Can you still win that battle?

So the division and replication of your tissue cells goes wrong. If an army operated like that, never mind whether it could fight, the whole system would be in chaos, wouldn't it? And when the body falls into that kind of chaos, inflammation becomes cancer. That is why cancer is so hard to treat. The information in your body has been altered beyond recognition. The cells want to work, but they cannot get the correct instructions. So what do many cancer patients die of? Not the cancer itself, but the collapse of the immune system. We will go into that when we reach the chapter on immunity.

That brings us to the second function of vitamin C given in the textbooks, which is that it raises the body's immunity.

Take a cold or fever caused by bacteria. Why is it, especially with children, that you do not have to reach immediately for an antibiotic? Because the best way to fight bacteria is to let your white blood cells engulf them. Whatever course of treatment you use in between, the effect you are ultimately after is for white blood cells to swallow the bacteria, and for their strength and numbers to increase. That is the fundamental solution.

We all know vitamin C raises resistance. That is common knowledge by now. But what does raising resistance actually mean? Inside your body, vitamin C strengthens the ability of white blood cells to engulf invaders. Think of a war. Vitamin C does not join the fighting itself. So what is its role? The white blood cells are the ones fighting the bacteria. What does vitamin C do? It is the medical team, the ambulance crew and the cheering squad. It keeps delivering shells and bullets into the hands of the white blood cells and keeps them supplied with food and fodder. Doesn't that strengthen the white cells' ability to keep fighting? The battle goes beautifully. That is vitamin C's important role in raising immunity.

Vitamin C promotes the formation of antibodies in the body. The more vitamin C a person has, the stronger their resistance. Vitamin C has another quality that women love: it can reverse the melanin in our skin. Look at how many high-end cosmetics and whitening products contain vitamin C. Why? Because of this property. In our skin there is a substance called [[tyrosine|酪氨酸. The amino acid that the enzyme tyrosinase converts, through several oxidation steps, into melanin, the pigment that darkens skin. Third, vitamin C takes part in the synthesis of collagen. We all know how important collagen is to the body. Our ligaments, our joints and our skin are all made of collagen, and there is a great deal of collagen in our bones. When collagen comes up, the first important nutrient everyone thinks of is protein. But do not forget that vitamin C is the key substance that links these proteins together to form collagen. Without vitamin C, the synthesis of collagen simply cannot happen. So it protects the health of the bones, the teeth and the gums.

Why do wounds heal faster when you have vitamin C? Because collagen is being made faster. So vitamin C plays a crucial part in the formation of collagen.

Someone with plenty of vitamin C keeps their skin younger, because they have more collagen than other people. Why is a child's skin so tender and so elastic? Because a child has more collagen than an adult. And doesn't that make it easier for our bones, ligaments and joints to stay elastic and young as well? So whenever bones come up, everyone thinks first of calcium and protein, and easily overlooks the part vitamin C plays.

Fourth, vitamin C also improves the absorption of iron. It helps iron bind with protein, so it has a definite role in treating iron-deficiency anemia. Vitamin C also takes part in a whole series of reactions in the body called hydroxylation reactions, and you can look those up for yourself.

Put simply, hydroxylation reactions are important steps in building up or breaking down many important substances in the body, and vitamin C is essential to them. The synthesis of collagen I just described is a hydroxylation reaction, and it cannot happen without vitamin C. Vitamin C also promotes the synthesis of neurotransmitters, which is connected with adrenaline. Beyond that, it supports the processing of steroids, so people with high cholesterol in particular should be taking enough vitamin C. The fifth function of vitamin C is that it assists the detoxification of organic compounds, including the toxins in the medicines we take. That process requires vitamin C.

Here is an everyday example. If we leave food sitting for a long time, especially leafy greens, what happens to the nitrites in those greens? They become [[nitrosamines|亚硝胺. Nitrites in the acid of the stomach form compounds that react with amines to make N-nitroso compounds. Research links exposure to these compounds to a higher risk of several cancers, including stomach and esophageal cancer, and shows that vitamin C blocks their formation by reacting with the nitrite first.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6539140/]], and nitrosamines are an important factor in provoking tumors of the digestive system. Vitamin C can reduce nitrosamines, turning them back into nitrites. And because vitamin C is a water-soluble nutrient, it helps the body eliminate certain heavy metals. We all know that heavy metals enter the body through our food and water. How do we get them out, and how do we do it without hindering the absorption of iron and calcium?

A heavy metal such as [[lead|WHO states that lead permanently affects children's brain development, lowering IQ and causing shortened attention span and behavioral difficulties, and that it also causes anemia, high blood pressure, kidney damage and harm to immunity and the reproductive organs. There is no known safe blood lead level, and young children absorb four to five times as much lead as adults.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lead-poisoning-and-health]] does great harm to the body. Take lead poisoning in children. The most immediate outward signs are that the child does not grow, is a picky eater, has a bad temper, and has constipation and bloating. If you notice a child has trouble falling asleep, has insomnia, and also gets headaches and dizziness, you should ask whether lead poisoning is possible. Those are still the mild symptoms. Lead poisoning in children also affects vision, so if a child's eyesight suddenly declines, parents should consider whether it might be lead.

Children with lead poisoning all share one common problem: they are frail and often ill, with colds and fevers that keep coming back. They also become emotionally unstable, unable to concentrate, and slow to react.

What are the early symptoms when lead is too high in a child? Early on, the signs are always the signs of iron deficiency. This is a test of a parent's basic knowledge. Most children with high lead levels start out iron deficient, because if the body lacks vitamin C, iron is poorly absorbed, and by the same token the harmful heavy metal lead cannot be cleared from the body because vitamin C is lacking. So we do not talk about lead poisoning.

We say that the early stage of high lead in a child is the picture of iron deficiency. It begins with a lack of iron, and gradually the lead builds past the safe level. So how do you clear lead from a child? On one side, give enough iron. On the other, give enough vitamin C. Vitamin C both improves iron absorption and helps eliminate lead. By the same logic, vitamin C helps us absorb calcium. We talk all the time about taking calcium, and when calcium comes up everyone thinks about how many milligrams they need. But without vitamin C involved, the calcium does not get in.
