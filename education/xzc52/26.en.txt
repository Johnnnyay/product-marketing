# Vitamin B2, Ulcers and Cancer Risk, and How Much to Take

Hello, everyone. Last time we said that if a woman is severely short of vitamin B2, it may lead to cervical erosion, and cervical erosion makes her vulnerable to infection by which virus? HPV, the human papillomavirus, the one that makes people turn pale at its name. Cervical erosion together with HPV makes cervical cancer very likely. Routine testing treats the detection of HPV in your body as a warning sign of cervical cancer, and if the cells are found to have become squamous, you will basically be told you have cervical cancer. Some people say, I can have my uterus removed, or have it burned off with a laser. After it is burned off, is the HPV still there? It certainly is.

Removing the uterus does not clear the HPV. If you go on lacking B2, will the erosion come back? It certainly will. And if you are short of vitamin A as well, the chance of cancer becomes very high. Some people, seeing many celebrities getting the HPV vaccine, say, isn't getting the vaccine enough? But there are bivalent, quadrivalent and nine-valent vaccines. The nine-valent is the most widely used now, and it prevents only nine types of HPV. There are fourteen high-risk types, and [[more than a hundred known types of HPV altogether|The WHO counts about 200 known HPV types. Around 90 percent of infections clear on their own; cervical cancer is caused by persistent infection with high-risk types, and types 16 and 18 alone cause about 76 percent of cases. The WHO recommends vaccinating girls aged 9 to 14, before they become sexually active, since the vaccine prevents infection rather than treating it, and regular cervical screening from age 30.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cervical-cancer]]. Getting the vaccine does not mean your cervical erosion will heal. You have only protected yourself against those nine. When you get it, a responsible clinic will always tell you that the vaccine's antibody level lasts at least four to five years. Think about that sentence. What does "at least four to five years" really mean?

Also, many women with gynecological problems are not suitable candidates for the vaccine. It is especially not recommended for women who have not yet had children, because [[it has a large effect on the heart of a future baby|This is the speaker's claim and it is not supported by evidence. The WHO's Global Advisory Committee on Vaccine Safety, after reviewing more than 270 million doses, considers HPV vaccines extremely safe. Studies of more than 540,000 pregnancies in Denmark and 92,000 in the US found no harm to mother or baby, including no birth defects, and no link to reduced fertility.|https://www.who.int/groups/global-advisory-committee-on-vaccine-safety/topics/human-papillomavirus-vaccines/safety]].

And before you get it, they will tell you that if you are over 26, the vaccine's effectiveness has not been proven. If your immunity stays low, can you be infected again? So getting the vaccine does not mean you will not get cervical cancer. You can still get cervical erosion after the vaccine, and long-term inflammation from cervical erosion can still become cervical cancer. So whatever you decide, we are not against your getting it, but be careful, because vaccines have side effects too. Every vaccine has side effects, and none of them is a hundred percent.

So when the body has an ulcer that does not heal for a long time, it is easily infected by bacteria, again and again, and inflammation follows. Whether you use Chinese or Western medicine, what can it do for the inflammation? It reduces the inflammation and kills the bacteria. The drugs only kill the bacteria. But will the ulcer heal? The drug reduces the inflammation, but it does not make the ulcer better, because healing an ulcer depends on what? On protein and vitamin B2. So if you only use drugs, the inflammation improves for a while, but the congestion and swelling persist for a long time and bulge out from the surface of the tissue, forming all kinds of [[polyps|A polyp is a small growth on the lining of an organ. Most are benign. Some kinds of colon polyps can turn into cancer over years, which is why doctors remove them during colonoscopy. The link the speaker draws between polyps and B2 shortage is his own view.|]], such as stomach polyps, colon polyps and gallbladder polyps. Whenever there are polyps, what does that mean?

It means you have been severely short of B2 for a long time. So sometimes people have a gastroscopy or colonoscopy and have the polyps snipped off. Will they grow back after being cut off? They will. And if polyps grow back again and again, they slowly turn into a tumor. In the end the tumor's surface breaks down, it is infected and festers over and over, and the cells inside multiply wildly. That is cancer.

So what is cancer? [[It is the sign that the body has been severely short of B2 for a long time|This is the speaker's view and not the mainstream one. The WHO attributes nearly a quarter of cancer deaths to tobacco, alcohol, high body weight, low fruit and vegetable intake and physical inactivity, and about 10 percent of cancers to infections such as HPV, hepatitis B and H. pylori. Riboflavin shortage is not recognized as a root cause of cancer.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer]], that its tissues, organs and functions are close to failing, a warning of total collapse. That explains why, even after the cancerous part has been completely cut out, the cancer can still spread and return. It is not only that cancer cells spread. It is that one tissue and organ after another falls into collapse.

Of course, getting from ulcer to cancer may take another five, ten, twenty or even thirty years, and along the way there are all kinds of causes. A single lapse in attention can easily let cancer develop. We are not saying that a lack of B2 will certainly turn into cancer. But a lack of B2 will certainly produce ulcers, and if the ulcers do not heal, cancer becomes likely later on. At what age it appears depends on your immune system at the time, on your habits, and on many other things. But the most important cause is the one we just described. We often hear that cancer cells spread from here to there. Why? Because there are already many ulcerated surfaces all over the body. The whole body has become a breeding ground for cancer cells, and wherever they go, they can find a foothold.

That is why we say a lack of B2 is the root cause of cancer risk. Put that way, it makes sense, doesn't it? But can you rely on the hospital to deal with this? You cannot, can you? Because it is not the doctor's job. Whether your cells are well fed is not the doctor's job. What does the doctor deal with? You have an ulcer, you have inflammation, come in and I will reduce the inflammation. When the inflammation reaches a certain stage and becomes a polyp, come in and I will remove it. If you have a tumor I will cut it out. If you have cancer, I will cut it out and then give you chemotherapy. These are the doctor's scientific, standard procedures. As for why you got the disease and where it came from, that is not his responsibility.

So we say a lack of B2 is an important cause of cancer risk. Should we supplement vitamin B2? Can we skip it? At the pace of modern life, if you do not supplement, the risk of cancer in your body has not been removed.

Many people today have inflammation of one size or another, especially hidden, chronic inflammation, and you do not know how much. All of it carries a risk of cancer. We also know that almost everyone has cancer cells in the body. When they meet an ulcerated surface, a breeding ground, won't they flare up easily? So on the one hand we must raise immunity, and on the other we must reduce the ulcers and the inflammation. Vitamin B2 is so good, and we need so much of it, so how much should we take? The first thing is that we must learn to read the figures for ourselves. We have to find the data and look at it ourselves.

Second, we cannot just look at the data as it stands. We must also learn to calculate, taking our own situation into account. So solving the question of how much to take means two things. First, you have to know how to read the figures, and second, you have to go back and calculate, combining them with your own situation. The recommended intake of B2, the RNI, differs at each age. From birth to six months it is 0.4 milligrams a day. From six months to one year, 0.5 milligrams. From one year, 0.6. From four years, 0.7. From seven years, 1.0. From eleven years, 1.2. From fourteen, boys need 1.5 and girls 1.0. [[From eighteen, men need 1.4 and women 1.2|These are China's recommended intakes. The US RDA for riboflavin is 1.3 mg a day for men and 1.1 mg for women, 1.4 mg in pregnancy and 1.6 mg while breastfeeding. The US sets no upper limit, because no toxic level has been seen from food or from long-term high-dose supplements.|https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/riboflavin-vitamin-b2/]]. Breastfeeding mothers need more than 1.8 a day, and pregnant women 1.7. Having seen these amounts, do you think we can simply eat according to them? What these amounts tell you is that if you are not deficient, taking in this much each day is fine.

But what we mostly find is that we already have deficiency symptoms, and then these amounts will not do. Each person has to decide how much to take based on the pressures of work and life, and on the deficiency symptoms in his own body. So these figures are a reference, but they vary from person to person.

But wherever I go, someone always raises the same question: why should I take these nutritional supplements? Can't I get all my B2 from food? So take the time to understand the concept of nutritional supplements correctly. In essence they are food. They are a food product. It is just that we can no longer get the nutrients we need from the food we eat every day, so our food has to be supplemented and upgraded, and that is what we call a nutritional supplement.
