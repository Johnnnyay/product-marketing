So let us first understand what a functional food is and how functional foods are classified. Everything here comes from that university textbook. Functional foods are what people commonly call [[health foods or supplements|保健品 or 保健食品. In China this is a regulated legal category: foods that claim a specific health function or supply vitamins and minerals, are meant for particular groups of people, regulate the body's functions, and are not intended to treat disease. The category is roughly what an American reader would call dietary supplements.|]]. In our country they are divided into three generations.

The first generation is called fortified food. Its functions were inferred from the nutrient components in the food. Listen to that word carefully: inferred. The claimed functions were never confirmed by any trial. Think of the malted milk powder we drank as children, or royal jelly. Those belong to this first generation. Today the countries of Europe and North America regulate this whole class as ordinary food. In China in the 1980s and 1990s, this kind of so-called health product was everywhere, and all of it was first generation. But from June 1996, when China's Health Food Administration Measures came into force, products of this type were no longer permitted to be sold as health foods. Once you hear this, you understand that first-generation products were never rigorous nutritional foods at all. They were simply foods with something added.

The second generation is called, in our country, primary functional food. These are required to go through human and animal trials to confirm that the claimed function is scientifically real. Most of the health foods China produces itself today fall into this generation. Many brands are involved, and I will not go into them here.

The third generation is the one we are mainly concerned with. It is called advanced functional food. A third-generation product must not only prove through human and animal trials that it has a particular physiological function. It must also identify the active ingredient responsible for that function, the structure of that ingredient, its dose-response relationship, its mechanism of action, and its compatibility and stability within the food. You can see that the bar is far higher.

In the developed markets of Europe, the United States and Japan, most health products on the shelf are third-generation functional foods. Products of this class are already on the market in China, but most are imported from abroad, from companies such as Amway, DuPont and Procter & Gamble. Those three names are written in black and white in the university textbook.

Although our country still lacks systematic research on third-generation functional foods, the textbook says that as the concept takes hold it is bound to become the direction of the health food industry. When you hear the word direction, understand that it signals the state's recognition, and that this is part of what our national health industry embraces. Because the state recognizes the third-generation functional food, or health food, it also recognizes the range of functions those products may serve. In other words, Nutrilite falls within the scope that the state recognizes. What does that scope of functions include? The list is fairly long, so let me read it.

Functional foods, meaning health foods, and especially the third-generation products our country recognizes, have the capacity to regulate physiological activity. That includes supporting the body's defenses, regulating physiological rhythms, preventing disease and promoting health.

According to the 2012 adjustment to the scope of health food functions, the original 27 functions were cancelled, merged and adjusted down to 18. I want to read all 18, because they are worth remembering.

These 18 functions are the legally defined directions in which a health food in our country may work: helping to strengthen immunity, helping to lower blood lipids, helping to lower blood sugar, helping to improve sleep, antioxidant action, helping to relieve exercise fatigue, helping to reduce body fat, helping to increase bone density, helping to improve iron-deficiency anemia, helping to improve memory, helping to improve tolerance of low oxygen, helping to reduce alcoholic liver damage, helping to eliminate lead, helping with lactation, helping to relieve eye fatigue, helping to improve gastrointestinal function, and helping to promote healthy facial skin.
