# The B Vitamins in Review: Teamwork and Homocysteine

Over the past six lessons we have spent our time on the vitamin B family, which tells you how rich a subject it is. After protein, the B vitamins are the second major nutrient we need to understand. We have given them this much time because I want you to know how important they are. They are an excellent partner for protein, and without the B vitamins, protein may not deliver its full effect in the body. Today we will wrap up the B vitamin chapter. We will pin down the key points, and I will also explain how the nutrients within the B family work together.

Let us begin with the summary and review what characterizes the B vitamins. First, they support the body's energy metabolism. They act as coenzymes, the helpers in energy metabolism, so whatever you eat each day, it has to be digested and metabolized, and that process cannot do without coenzymes. The health of every one of us, our metabolism minute by minute every day, depends on the B vitamins. Everybody needs them. Second, all the B vitamins are water-soluble. They generally leave the body in sweat and urine, which makes deficiency very common. That is why each of us has to pay attention and replenish them promptly every day.

When older people in the past cooked rice porridge, they would add a pinch of alkali, baking soda, to make it more fragrant and thicker. In fact, that is the most destructive thing you can do to the B vitamins. B vitamins cannot tolerate alkali, and the moment you add it, the B vitamins in the whole pot are lost.

Another key point is that the B vitamins are found in many animal organ meats. But we do not recommend eating processed liver and kidney, because the toxicity of these foods outweighs their nutritional value.

Many people used to eat grilled kidneys, following the old belief that "you nourish what you eat," so grilled kidney would nourish your own kidneys. In fact, an animal suffers a great deal as it dies, and at the moment of death it releases a large amount of adrenaline. That adrenaline was meant for the animal's stress response, but after death it cannot all be broken down. It stays in the kidney, and when you eat it, it is a toxin. So in daily life our sources of B vitamins are very limited.

Next let us sort the B family into groups. Some friends say the family tree is too big: B1, B2, all the way to B12, so many nutrients, how am I supposed to remember them? Here is a good method. We can divide the B vitamins into two groups to help you remember. The first group consists of the coenzymes mainly involved in energy metabolism. Which B vitamins are those? Mainly B1, B2, niacin and pantothenic acid.

The second group relates to an important health topic I will cover in the second half of today's lesson: how to hold down the [[homocysteine|同型半胱氨酸. An amino acid the body normally breaks down quickly using vitamins B6, B12 and folic acid. MedlinePlus notes that high levels may damage arteries and raise clot risk, but also that in most cases lowering homocysteine has not been shown to reduce the risk of heart attack or stroke.|https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/homocysteine-test/]] in your blood, because this substance does great harm to the body. Which B vitamins are needed to clear it? Remember these three: B6, B12 and folic acid.

Let us start with B1, B2, niacin and pantothenic acid. A deficiency of vitamin B1 has a classic symptom. Do you remember what it is? Beriberi. A deficiency of B2 also has a matching symptom. What is it? Angular cheilitis, the cracking at the corners of the mouth. And the symptom that matches a niacin deficiency? Pellagra. Pellagra seems rare today, but there was once a widespread outbreak of it in Xinjiang. Then there is pantothenic acid. What deficiency matches a lack of pantothenic acid? It leads to a shortfall in the body's energy metabolism. You can go back and listen to the earlier lessons to help you understand and remember.

The key here is whether you can link each of these nutrients to its main deficiency disease. Once you can match them up, remembering them becomes very easy.

The next big point in the B family is a question you may be asking. If I am short of B1, give me B1. If I am short of B2, give me B2. If I am short of niacin, give me niacin. Isn't that enough? Why can't we supplement them one at a time, and why must we take them as a whole? What is special about the B vitamins is that they form one large class, the B complex, and together they produce a combined effect. You can think of them as a combination of punches in your body. Leave out one move, and the whole combination loses much of its power. That brings us to the synergy within the B family.

The body's energy metabolism is a very complex cycle involving millions, even tens of millions, of steps. Throughout that process, B1, B2, niacin and pantothenic acid work in concert, and none of them can be missing. We cannot break down those millions of steps one by one, but taken together there are at least nine major stages. From the moment you eat something to the moment it leaves your body, there are at least nine major stages in the body, and the cycle of energy metabolism is extremely complex.

Everything a person eats must ultimately become energy-producing material and enter this cycle to produce energy. You can see why the quality of the food you eat matters so much. From the food going in to the waste coming out, step by step through the energy cycle, from pantothenic acid to niacin, then to B1, then to B2, and finally back to niacin, these few work together.

That is why, when we supplement the B vitamins, we usually take a B complex, which means taking them all at the same time.

Another point is that a lack of B vitamins is closely linked to the skin, the mucous membranes and the nervous system. A nerve cell in our body looks roughly like this: at the front is the cell body, and behind it runs a long fiber. That long fiber is really the tail of the nerve cell, and nerve signals travel along these tails. So how do these nerve fibers run through the body without crossing their wires?

Think of renovating a large house. When you redo the basic plumbing and wiring, how do you lay out all the electrical cables, pipes and network lines so that nothing crosses? Why does a nerve in the ear not short-circuit into the arm? Why does a nerve running to the eye not cross into the thigh? What keeps the electrical signals in the nerve fibers from crossing comes down to two important nutrients. The first and most important is the B vitamins. The B vitamins protect the [[insulation around the nerve fiber|The protective coating on many nerve fibers is called the myelin sheath, a fatty layer that lets electrical signals travel quickly and keeps them on track. Vitamin B12 in particular is needed to maintain it, and long-term B12 deficiency can cause nerve damage.|]]. It is like the electrical signals in your home. The bedroom switch controls the bedroom light, the living room switch controls the living room light, and each runs on its own line.

The wires may run through the same conduit, but each is separated from the others by insulation, which is why the wiring in your home does not short-circuit. And what forms the insulation on the signal wires in our body? First, the B vitamins, and second, lecithin. So if the body is short of B vitamins, the nerves' insulation tends to age and the signals cross, and the nerve signals in your body are bound to go wrong. Your mood, your state of mind, your overall condition and even your sense of pain are all affected.

Let me give you an example. What is a classic sign of a B vitamin deficiency? A nerve at the back of the head and behind the ear that throbs, or even gives a jab of pain again and again. Has that ever happened to you? This pain tells us there is peripheral neuritis caused by a lack of B vitamins. The insulation on a peripheral nerve has aged and worn, so the nerve is leaking a little current into the nerve beside it.

If you go to the hospital, what do they tell you? They call it [[greater occipital neuritis|枕大神经炎, known in English medicine as occipital neuralgia: sharp, jabbing or electric-shock pain at the back of the head and neck caused by irritation of the occipital nerves. Mainstream sources list causes such as muscle tension, injury and arthritis of the neck; the B vitamin link is the speaker's own view.|]], or neuritis behind the ear. It is a very typical peripheral nerve inflammation that people today get often and often ignore. It is not that serious; you may simply feel off. But what it is really telling you is that your body is short of B vitamins. It is called peripheral neuritis, and if we lack the knowledge and ignore the signal, the symptoms of B deficiency will grow more and more severe.

Another typical sign of a B vitamin deficiency is twitching in sleep. Partway through the night, a hand or a foot suddenly jerks. Other people dream the moment they fall asleep. They dream they are standing on a tall building, miss a step and fall, and the feeling of weightlessness and fear jolts them awake. When are these dreams most common? In adolescence, especially in the middle school years. Teenagers have this dream all the time and wake up with a start. Parents usually say, "It's fine, don't be scared, it means you're growing." But if parents knew that this [[twitching in sleep|Sudden jerks while falling asleep are called hypnic jerks or sleep starts, and are very common in healthy people. The speaker attributes them to a lack of B vitamins; this is his interpretation rather than an established medical finding.|]] comes from a lack of B vitamins, they would not say that.

These are fairly obvious signs of B vitamin deficiency in the body. The first step in family health management is to understand the hidden worry behind these symptoms: they represent a nutrient deficiency in the body. You can dismiss them as minor, but when this child grows up, a lack of B vitamins during puberty may lead to metabolic problems later. Why did blood sugar climb after middle age? Why have even the liver and kidneys developed problems? All of this is connected with nutrient deficiencies while the body was still growing.

Another role of the B vitamins is in the mucous membranes. When someone is vomiting and goes to the hospital, the doctor often prescribes some B vitamins. That kind of B vitamin is a drug and cannot be taken long term, but it works in the moment, because the B vitamins help stabilize the stomach lining. They make the stomach feel a little more settled after vomiting so you are not so nauseous. That is another important role of the B vitamins.

Now let me introduce the other three B vitamins: B6, B12 and folic acid. We put these three in one group because all three affect the metabolism of many amino acids in the body. In plain language, they affect the development of the body's cells.

The simplest example is the development of red blood cells. Without enough B12 and folic acid, red blood cells cannot mature. They stay stuck at a very immature, oversized stage. What is this disease called? [[Megaloblastic anemia|巨幼红细胞贫血. MedlinePlus describes it as anemia in which the body lacks enough folate (or vitamin B12) to make healthy red blood cells, so the cells are abnormally large. Causes include too little folic acid in the diet, overcooked food, certain medications, alcohol use, pregnancy and intestinal disorders.|https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000551.htm]]. It is not the same as iron-deficiency anemia. It is a different kind of anemia.

There is another point I want you to remember especially. A lack of B12 and folic acid can severely affect the nervous development of the fetus, folic acid above all. We have all heard of the common birth defects called anencephaly and spina bifida. I will not go into detail here, and you can look them up yourself. Why does anencephaly happen? Because the mother lacked folic acid during pregnancy, the brain cells did not develop, and the baby did not grow a brain.

Then there is spina bifida. You may never have seen it, especially the severe form, and a person with severe spina bifida does not live very long. In [[mild spina bifida|Spina bifida occulta, the hidden form, in which the bones of the spine do not close fully but the spinal cord stays in place under the skin. According to KidsHealth, a dimple, a patch of hair or a red mark at the base of the spine can be a sign, and most people with it never know they have it.|https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/spina-bifida-occulta.html]], some people have a tuft of hair growing at one point along the spine on their back, and the place where the hair grows often indicates a hidden spina bifida.

Without enough folic acid, the brain vesicles at the back of the embryo do not develop and the neural tube does not close, and that results in anencephaly and spina bifida. So pregnancy is hard work. A mother gives a great deal of her body to it, but she must also arm herself with some knowledge and take good care of the baby inside her, so that she does not suffer in vain. Above all, remember to take enough folic acid, especially in early pregnancy.

Finally, let us come back to what I mentioned at the start: the relationship between the B vitamins and homocysteine. This is an area modern nutrition has studied a great deal. Homocysteine is a substance that is very harmful to the body, and B6, B12 and folic acid play a crucial role in clearing it. They speed up the breakdown of homocysteine. So what exactly does homocysteine do to our bodies? Listen carefully. Mainly, it damages the blood vessels. If a person has a high level of homocysteine in the blood, that person is very prone to atherosclerosis, and the cholesterol and blood lipids in their blood are very easily oxidized.

When we go for a physical today, some large hospitals have already added a test for homocysteine levels, especially for middle-aged and older people who already have heart or cerebrovascular disease. If the level is high, it signals that the chance of a cardiovascular or cerebrovascular event has gone up. This is openly available and widely accepted knowledge. So how can we lower the level of homocysteine in the body? The greatest role of B6, B12 and folic acid is to convert homocysteine into other substances and clear it away.

So when I talk today about supplementing B vitamins, the point is not merely to prevent beriberi or cracked lips. Those are not the main concern. The real aim is to keep down homocysteine, which is far more closely tied to our health. An elevated level, which we call high homocysteine for short, sharply raises the risk of coronary heart disease, peripheral vascular disease and cerebrovascular disease.

You can look it up yourself. Blood homocysteine is an important health indicator. So let me stress it again: what plays the decisive role in clearing this harmful substance are B6, B12 and folic acid from the B family. I hope you will hold on to this point, because it really does help you prevent heart and cerebrovascular disease in your family.

Did you know that the United States is a country with a very high rate of heart and cerebrovascular disease? So the standard practice there is to [[add folic acid to all the flour in the country|Since 1998 the United States has required enriched cereal grain products to be fortified with 140 micrograms of folic acid per 100 grams. The stated goal was to prevent neural tube defects, and the CDC estimates it prevents about 1,300 affected births a year. It was not introduced as a heart disease measure.|https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm6401a2.htm]]. You see, it has become national policy, and that should make every one of us take it seriously.

If we can strengthen B vitamin intake in the daily life of the whole family, it helps a great deal in preventing and even reversing metabolic disease. So why are the B vitamins so important? Because they are easy to run short of, not easy to replenish, and most of all, they are so often overlooked. That completes our lessons on the B family.
