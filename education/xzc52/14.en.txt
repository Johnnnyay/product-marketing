# Exercise in the Right Amount

Hello everyone, this is lesson fourteen. Yesterday we talked about sleep, and about sleep cycles in particular. Have you all learned how to calculate and use them? Today I want to talk with you about exercise. Let me first pick up the question I left you with yesterday, and look at how we should understand exercise. The third controllable factor in our health is exercise, and what we mean by that is exercise in the right amount. When exercise comes up, many people misunderstand it, especially those who are good at training hard. Have you heard people say this? Some say, I do not care about anything else, as long as I exercise I will be healthy, because life is movement.

All right then. Go and look at professional athletes. What is the average lifespan of professional athletes worldwide? [[Fifty|The speaker's figure. Large studies point the other way: a cohort of 15,174 Olympic medallists lived on average about 2.8 years longer than matched controls, and a study of French Olympians found mortality about half that of the general population. The heavy wear on joints in certain sports, which is the speaker's concern, is a separate question from lifespan.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5039136/]]. Fifty. So for us, exercise must be in the right amount. Do not turn it into professional sport, because anything professional is bound to harm the body. So what kind of exercise should we do? Health-nurturing exercise. The first point about exercise is to match it to your stage of life. Before thirty, the joints repair themselves well, so you can play competitive sports: badminton, table tennis, basketball, volleyball, football, all fine.

After thirty, the joints repair themselves less well, so between thirty and fifty you can do muscle work. Building muscle and anaerobic training are no problem. And after fifty? As far as possible, do health-nurturing exercise. After fifty you should do more of [[tai chi, baduanjin, and wuqinxi|太极拳、八段锦、五禽戏, three traditional Chinese systems of slow, gentle movement. Trials of baduanjin qigong report improvements in quality of life, sleep quality, balance, grip strength, flexibility, blood pressure and resting heart rate, generally with at least two thirty-minute sessions a week for eight weeks or more.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5359459/]]. In other words, unless you are a professional, after fifty you should do as little as possible of things like marathons and other demanding competitive sports. They may make you feel energized, but your body is running on credit.

There is something else to watch carefully. The moment you start exercising, your body burns through a great many nutrients. If exercise is not combined with a sensible diet and nutritional supplementation, then the more you exercise, the more you are simply consuming yourself, because when we exercise, does the body need more nutrients? Does it use up more nutrients? It does. Which means that the dietary structure exercise requires and the dietary structure a sedentary life requires are completely different in nutritional terms.

So once you start exercising, if you do not adjust your nutrition, then the more you exercise the higher your risk. Our organs divide into vital organs and non-vital organs. A vital organ is one you cannot live without. The human body has five vital organs: the brain, the heart, the lungs, the liver and the kidneys. Everything else is non-vital. And what is the largest non-vital organ? The four limbs. Because staying alive comes first, the body will do anything to keep the vital organs running properly.

So when the body runs short of nutrients, it takes nutrients from the non-vital organs and gives them to the vital ones. That means it pulls nutrients out of the limbs. And where do they go? To the center. So when you exercise, you are strengthening the limbs, which means you are forcing nutrients toward the limbs. Is that not working against the body's own direction? You are making your non-vital organs compete with your vital organs for nutrients. Past a certain age, is that not [[dangerous|The speaker's model resembles the "triage theory" proposed by the biochemist Bruce Ames: when a vitamin or mineral is in short supply, the body allocates it first to the functions needed for immediate survival, at the expense of the repair processes that protect long-term health.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1693790/]]?

On top of that, because of the lack of nutrition, certain key parts of your limbs age faster, so injuries come easily. As the limbs age, you cannot execute the technical movements properly, your form breaks down, and you cannot do what you mean to do, so it is very easy to get hurt. Look at players on a football pitch. Few are injured in the first half. Injuries usually come in the second half, because by then their nutrition can no longer keep up, they are tired, and injuries follow. So exercise is only safe and beneficial when nutrition is sufficient.

So choose exercise sensibly, according to our age and the condition of our body. The simplest exercise is brisk walking and light jogging. Swimming also calls for care, because your conditions usually are not good enough. Many swimming pools have no dry sauna beside them, and [[the Chinese tend to carry a lot of dampness|Cold and dampness, 寒气 and 湿气, are traditional Chinese medicine concepts describing states of the body rather than diagnoses in Western medicine. They sit within the nine body constitutions used in Chinese medicine today, one of which is the phlegm-dampness constitution.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5671717/]], so if you swim and do not use a dry sauna afterward, there is often no way to drive the cold back out.

Generally speaking, among the nine body constitutions, those who carry a lot of dampness and who swim for a long time without using a dry sauna will take in a great deal of cold. In particular, sometimes you swim for twenty or thirty minutes and come out to rest. If your body has dried off completely and you want to go back in, what should you do? You must warm up again. If you do not, problems can arise in the body when you get back in the water. So be careful with swimming. A good pool will have a dry sauna beside it, and the dry sauna is excellent. It drives the cold out and helps you sweat a great deal, and swimming in that manner is quite good. In other words, we have to understand that professional sport and health-nurturing exercise are completely different things.

Look at all the divers who retire at thirty. Most come away aching all over, with a body full of problems. So what kind of exercise should we do? Exercise in the right amount. And what does exercise do for the body? First, it speeds up blood circulation and gives the heart muscle a certain amount of training. Once the blood is circulating, your nutrients travel through the whole body and your toxins are more easily expelled. So we need exercise, but it must be sensible exercise, and we must not turn ourselves into full-time professional athletes.

Some people say, surely there is nothing wrong with practicing tai chi or yoga. Let me say this: practicing yoga after forty is not the same as practicing it when you were young. After forty, you need to understand that you do not have to hold every posture to the exact standard, because the other person started yoga at a young age and you are starting at forty. You are not the same. It is the same principle I explained earlier. So is it true that as long as you do this kind of exercise you will be healthy?

There is a well-known yoga teacher named Guo Jian who, after she developed breast cancer and had her breast removed, posted the photographs of her chest, because she wanted people to have a correct understanding of health and exercise.

And then there is the huge market these days for fitness, body sculpting and weight loss. Some trainers do not understand nutrition, so they tell you not to eat this and not to eat that, no protein, no vitamins, just train, all in pursuit of what? Fast weight loss. These are very unscientific methods. We must understand what scientific, appropriately measured exercise is, and more importantly we must understand that as soon as you exercise, you have to pair it with a more sensible diet and supplementation.
