# Three Key Lactobacillus Strains

Today we will sort the probiotics into their types and briefly discuss what each does. Taking the stage today are three leading members of the probiotic family: Lactobacillus acidophilus, Lactobacillus rhamnosus, and Lactobacillus casei. Let me start with Lactobacillus acidophilus. The name may not be familiar, but it has real importance for our gut health.

Lactobacillus acidophilus secretes [[antibiotic-like substances|The speaker names acidolin, acidophilin, and lactacin (嗜酸乳菌素, 嗜酸杆菌素, 乳酸菌素). These are bacteriocins: small proteins that some bacteria produce to suppress competing microbes.|]], such as acidolin, acidophilin, and lactacin, which suppress the growth of undesirable microorganisms in the gut. It has an antagonistic effect on disease-causing organisms, and it regulates the balance of the gut flora.

There is a technical term again: what is an [[antagonistic effect|拮抗作用. In microbiology, antagonism means one organism inhibits another, for example by competing for space and food or by producing substances that suppress it. The speaker's saying, "two monks have no water to drink," is the Chinese version of "too many cooks spoil the broth."|]]? You can look it up, but to sum it up, it is the old saying that two monks end up with no water to drink, where two people working together get less done than one alone. Lactobacillus acidophilus produces this kind of effect on pathogenic microbes: they may be numerous, but they cannot multiply their damage to the body.

So Lactobacillus acidophilus is very important within the gut flora. This is why every Nutrilite probiotic, whether the multi-strain, the women's, or the children's formula, contains Lactobacillus acidophilus. It is the foundation. It regulates the ecology of the gastrointestinal tract.

Tomorrow we will cover [[Bifidobacterium|双歧杆菌. The genus of beneficial bacteria that dominates the large intestine, especially in breast-fed infants, and one of the two groups most used in probiotics.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4908950/]], which many people know as the main probiotic of the large intestine. But Lactobacillus acidophilus is not only in the large intestine. It is found throughout the stomach and the small intestine as well. When Lactobacillus acidophilus in the small intestine declines, pathogenic bacteria increase, and the most obvious result is that the small intestine's absorptive power drops. It stops absorbing nutrients, and the ends of the small-intestinal wall easily develop long-term inflammation. So within the gut flora, Lactobacillus acidophilus and Bifidobacterium both exist in enormous numbers. They help and support one another. You could call them the two main forces among our gut probiotics, protecting the microecological balance of the whole gastrointestinal tract and thereby promoting health.

Lactobacillus acidophilus also regulates the microecology from top to bottom along the whole long route from stomach to intestine. The stomach needs it, the large intestine needs it, and the small intestine needs it. In the large intestine especially, it releases substances that benefit the growth of Bifidobacterium and other probiotics, increasing the vitality of those strains.

So Lactobacillus acidophilus is the big brother of the gut probiotics, absolutely front and center. It helps multiply the numbers of the other strains. In our words, it is truly one who benefits others. The higher the proportion of probiotics in our gut, the better the microecological environment, and the younger and more vital the gut appears. Where does gut health show? Not only in good digestion and absorption and normal bowel movements, and in being less prone to intestinal infections. For the whole body, an ample and vigorous population of probiotics suppresses the various harmful bacteria, which reduces the toxins generated in the body and lightens the liver's detoxification burden.

And when Lactobacillus acidophilus and Bifidobacterium ferment in the gut, they produce lactic acid and acetic acid, which raise the utilization of phosphorus and iron, promote the absorption of vitamin D, produce vitamin K, and reduce the absorption of cholesterol. In some extreme working environments they can reduce the harm of radiation to the body. They are a great help to us.

Now let us talk about Lactobacillus rhamnosus and Lactobacillus casei. Lactobacillus rhamnosus lives mostly in the gut of humans and animals, and in bacterial taxonomy it belongs to the lactobacilli. In [[1983, two professors at North Carolina State University|The strain the speaker is describing is Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (now classified as Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus GG). The speaker's university attribution differs from the published record.|]] isolated Lactobacillus rhamnosus from a healthy human and obtained a patent for it. This probiotic's most notable trait is its outstanding tolerance of stomach acid and bile acid. It can enter the human gut alive, still active.

Most other probiotic strains have already lost a great deal to stomach acid and bile before they ever reach the gut. When Lactobacillus rhamnosus colonizes the human gut, and this is what we discussed earlier about supplementing regularly, once it is supplemented regularly it multiplies in large numbers and attaches tightly to the epithelial cells of the gut.

Think about it. If it attaches very tightly to the epithelial cells, what does it become? A biological barrier, a layer over the gut lining.

We talked earlier about the four barriers of the gut. The strong adhesion of Lactobacillus rhamnosus to the intestinal mucosa provides one such barrier, and it also regulates the gut's microecological system well, bringing the gut environment into balance and improving the function of the digestive system.

Lactobacillus rhamnosus adheres noticeably better to an adult's gut than to a child's, mainly because the adult gut environment is more mature, which makes it very easy for the strain to attach, and this raises the body's immunity.

We know the human immune system divides into innate immunity and [[adaptive immunity|适应性免疫, also called specific immunity. The part of the immune system that learns to recognize a particular pathogen after infection or vaccination, as opposed to innate immunity, the body's general first-line defenses such as macrophages and other white blood cells.|]]. What is adaptive immunity? Another technical term. It refers to specific immunity, immunity aimed at one particular pathogen. It is the ability to resist an infection that the body acquires only after first being infected, or after receiving a vaccine.

Why do we get COVID-19 vaccines? To activate this specific immunity. Innate immunity is mainly carried out by macrophages, white blood cells, and the like. What happens if the innate immune system runs into trouble? The adaptive immune system has to step in. The two work together to complete the body's immune defense. But once the body suffers trauma or infection, the immune system's function comes under strain. So for raising immunity quickly, we can generally use the Nutrilite women's probiotic, which includes Lactobacillus rhamnosus. Can men take it? Of course. The women's probiotic does not mean women only.

Next let me talk about its role in preventing and treating diarrhea. With Lactobacillus rhamnosus you can prevent and treat diarrhea, which is another of its major features. That after everyone drank the milk, Lactobacillus rhamnosus regulated the infants' gut flora and prevented diarrhea.

So Lactobacillus rhamnosus works very well in preventing and even treating diarrhea in infants and adults. Over the years more and more researchers have studied it, because it is widely regarded not only as a probiotic without toxicity or side effects but as one with high application value. It fits the health needs of modern people well, and research into its applications has a very promising future.
