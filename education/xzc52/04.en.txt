# What Health Means Now, and Why the Old Thinking Fails

Hello, everyone. Today is our fourth lesson, and from today our online study of family health management formally begins. I want to warn you in advance: over the next two or three days we will be covering a fair number of concepts and principles. Please do not find them dull, because these concepts cannot be skipped if we want to learn and understand family health management.

When we talk about health, it is a word people use all the time, yet many people have no complete idea of what it means. What did we use to think health was?

We thought it was physical health, that a person with no illness was healthy. But for the World Health Organization today, bodily health alone is not enough. When we meet people day to day, you could say everyone looks healthy. The WHO, though, tells us that health is not only the health of the body. [[It also covers the physical, the interpersonal, the moral and the values side of life, four dimensions in all|The WHO constitution defines health as "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." The moral and values dimension the speaker adds is common in Chinese health education but is not part of the WHO definition itself.|https://www.who.int/about/frequently-asked-questions]], and only then is it genuine health.

So let us look at what health actually includes. It includes the body, the mind and the spirit. It includes your character, your sense of independence, your moral sense, and it includes the health of the environment you live in. It is a combination of factors. So although when we talk about health we are mainly talking about physical health, these other factors are very often important too, and in modern society they are becoming more important all the time.

Why is it that today, from the state down to the individual, health is being raised to a height it has never had before? It is because more than forty years have passed since China's reform and opening up, our standard of living keeps rising, and by 2019 [[China's GDP per capita had already crossed the ten thousand US dollar mark|China's National Bureau of Statistics reported GDP per capita of about 10,276 US dollars for 2019, the first time the country passed 10,000 dollars.|https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.CD?locations=CN]].

You have also seen that 2020 marked the start of eliminating poverty across the whole country. So once food, clothing, housing and transport are taken care of, a need for security naturally follows. Security in what? First, security about health. Second, of course, security about money. We will talk about financial security another time. Today we are mainly talking about the need for security about health, because once people need health security, they correspondingly need the ability to manage health. The ability to manage health has to be a complete framework, and it has to be learned as a system.

Most of us got our health awareness, our health knowledge and our health concepts from the generation before us, and that knowledge is fragmentary.

Sometimes you see grandparents insisting that a grandchild must eat white rice with every meal, that going without white rice is not allowed. Where does that idea come from? It comes from what the grandparents learned when they were small. If we ate only white rice today, would our health suffer? It certainly would, because is the white rice of today the same as the white rice of their childhood? Today's white rice is pure carbohydrate and nothing else. So a lot of the knowledge handed down from the previous generation is not necessarily right when applied today, because the environment has long since changed.

Many people also say, look at our grandparents' generation. They were not fussy about anything and they still lived into their seventies and eighties. They never studied any health knowledge and never had any health management education, so how did they live to a hundred? Why do we need to study all this health knowledge today? We have to turn the question back on ourselves: is today's environment the same as it used to be? It is completely different.

A member of our group in Chengdu left me a message this very morning. He said that in his family, the grandparents' generation produced many healthy people over ninety, while his parents' generation is nowhere near their health or their lifespan. Why such a big difference? When the grandparents were young, there were green hills and clear water, and you could drink straight from the stream. The rice they ate was more organic than what we eat now. Their pressures were different from ours too, nothing like as heavy. The whole social environment was completely different from today's, and that is why there are new things we have to learn.

In other words, we now have to acquire a new body of health management knowledge and begin to understand how to manage health ourselves. That is especially important now. Once our level of wealth reaches a certain stage, these are things we have to learn. If we do not, problems are certain to follow.

If not one person in a household has begun to understand family health management, someone in that household is going to run into trouble. We all know health is enormously important, and that when you lose it everything returns to zero. There is plenty of general education in society, and bits and pieces of nutrition knowledge seem to be everywhere. But why do we need to study this knowledge as a system today? Because with the way society has developed, the disease picture we face now is completely different from the past. Diseases and viruses have changed a great deal.

When the People's Republic was just founded, the country was poor and sanitary conditions were not good. The highest death rates came from bacterial infections, plague and epidemics. Those killed very large numbers of people.

But as Chinese society developed, these epidemic diseases were almost all brought under control, so they are no longer what affects our health. What really affects our health now? [[Chronic noncommunicable disease|WHO's term for conditions such as cardiovascular disease, cancer, chronic respiratory disease and diabetes, which come from a mix of genetic, physiological, environmental and behavioral factors. They caused at least 43 million deaths worldwide in 2021, about three quarters of all deaths.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/noncommunicable-diseases]]. It has reached the stage of a gusher, and that matches the point our society has reached, with money now in a lot of people's pockets. So in this situation, you can no longer look at today's problems with yesterday's thinking.

What is chronic noncommunicable disease? You can look it up in an online encyclopedia. Generally it comes from poor lifestyle, behavioral habits and dietary preferences, along with unhealthy psychological factors.

Thinking about these factors raises a question. Are the causes of these diseases not very different from the causes of the bacterial infections of the past? Disease in the past was caused by infection, so faced with a disease caused by infection, the medical approach of the day was to wipe out the bacteria directly, and then the illness was cured. Does that approach work now? You can see that our entire medical system was also built on top of that earlier system of infectious disease. Our hospitals handle that kind of illness extremely well and extremely effectively.

But when it comes to chronic noncommunicable disease, if you keep using that same approach, big problems are certain to follow. I think everyone can understand that.

Take someone with high blood pressure. If you follow the old approach and simply bring the blood pressure down: in the past, I got sick because of an infection, you wiped out the bacteria for me, and my illness was cured. Now my blood pressure is high, you have brought my blood pressure down, so does that mean my illness is cured? Obviously that logic does not hold, does it? I think everyone can see that. Or take someone who develops [[hives|荨麻疹, urticaria, an itchy raised rash. It is often triggered by an immune reaction, and antihistamines relieve the rash without addressing what set the reaction off.|]]. You suppress the symptoms of the hives, but has the underlying problem been solved? Has the shortfall in the immune system been made good? No, it has not.

So one very large problem today is that many people approach health management with unchanged thinking. Their approach to symptoms is exactly the same as it used to be, carried over from before: a symptom appears, I wipe out the symptom, and once it is gone I consider the matter settled. What you discover is that there is a lot you cannot wipe out, and even when you do, it causes all kinds of problems and damage in other parts of your body.

So when it comes to health, the greatest problem in society today is that people's thinking has gone wrong, and that thinking problem is tied to a shortage of nutritional knowledge and health management ability, and to neglect of nutrition. What we are learning at this stage is not a set of prescriptions for you. We are telling you how to respond in the right way when symptoms do appear, what to do and what not to do. That matters enormously, rather than grabbing at any doctor in a panic the way other people do.

I heard of a case like this. An expert had a friend with high blood sugar, and he told the friend to take it seriously. The friend said fine, I will go to the hospital. He went, the doctor prescribed medication, and he took it for a week. Later the expert ran into the friend again and asked how he was doing. The friend said, I am better. What do you mean, better? Look, my blood sugar is normal. The expert asked, normal while you are taking the drug, or normal without it? He said, normal while I am taking it. The expert said, does normal on medication count as better? Or is the real test that you stop the medication and still have no symptoms?

Think it through for yourselves. Normal when you take the drug, not normal when you do not: can that count as cured? It is not cured. All that has been achieved is drug control. So being normal on medication does not mean the illness is gone. Generally speaking, once you are normal on medication, do you have to keep taking it? Do you have to keep it up, perhaps for the rest of your life?

We say a person takes medicine when they are ill. That has always been our idea of it. If you take medicine throughout your illness, and you keep taking it forever, and you are not normal without it, is your illness cured? It is never cured, because you are taking the drug forever.

So you have not gotten better. Your blood sugar has become normal under the control of the drug. Only the blood sugar is normal, but has the cause of the high blood sugar been found? This is a snapshot of how people today think about health and disease, and isn't there something wrong with it? So we have to spend some time learning some concepts, some knowledge, some nutritional knowledge, all of which we call the knowledge of health management. Because if we do not learn it, the problems will be very large, and they will keep getting larger. That is as far as we will go today. Tomorrow I will give you some data on the health of the Chinese people. Thank you, everyone, and see you tomorrow.
