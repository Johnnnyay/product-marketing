# Why Every Health Factor Comes Back to Diet

Hello, everyone. This is lesson fifteen. Today we come to the last of the controllable factors that affect our health. And I will say this at the outset: whether a factor is controllable or not, in the end it comes back to a balanced diet and our intake of nutrients. Why do I say that? Because every change in the outside world, every change in our state of mind, every change of any kind, finally shows itself inside the body, and what shows inside the body is always some set of biochemical reactions. If you are in a bad mood, does that produce corresponding biochemical reactions? It does.

A bad mood uses up a large amount of the B vitamins. In recent years the air in many cities has also been poor. Air pollution outside and food safety problems outside, once they enter the body, also use up a great deal of your nutrients. So any change in the outside world, including the factors we discussed in earlier lessons, comes down in the end to the biochemical reactions inside us. Changes in those reactions finally bring changes in the health of our cells, our organs and our whole body.

So what is it all rooted in? It is rooted in the substances inside your body, and in how your food and nutrition are put together. Those substances are what we eat at our three meals, which are our source of nutrients from the outside world. However the outside world changes, one thing stays essential, and that is a sensible diet.

Where does a healthy lifestyle begin? It begins with a sensible diet, because food is the material foundation for growth and development, for good health, for slowing aging, and for a long and healthy life.

Suppose you go to the hospital for surgery. The doctor sews up the wound and gives you medicine to fight the inflammation. When the wound finally heals, who does the healing? Your own capacity for repair, of course. That is why we say no illness is ever cured by a doctor. The doctor deals with what needs dealing with, and after that, who makes you well? You do. And what do you do it with? You need material to work with. Your recovery rests on a material foundation, and that foundation is something we have to take charge of ourselves.

Or think about [[Chinese massage, tendon work and cupping|推拿 (tuina, a form of therapeutic massage), 拨筋 (a deep technique worked across the tendons) and 拔罐 (cupping, in which suction cups are applied to the skin). All three are common in traditional Chinese practice and are often described as moving toxins out of the tissues.|]]. What these really do is push and move the toxins in your body so they can be cleared. Once they are loosened, those toxins still have to be metabolized, and for that the body needs water and it needs its own nutrients. If your B vitamins cannot keep up after the massage, you cannot metabolize the toxins away. You feel light and relaxed right after the session, but very soon you want to sleep. Why? The body is tired, because it has used up a large amount of nutrients. So neither Chinese medicine nor Western medicine can put our bodies completely right. What finally puts the body right is the basic material inside us, which means a sensible diet and well-balanced nutrition at our three meals.

Throughout its evolution, humankind has kept searching for, choosing, improving and upgrading its diet and nutrition in order to raise its level of health.

The body's physiological need for nutrients and the supply of nutrients in the diet must stay in balance. When they fall out of balance, for example when social conditions change and the food people used to eat can no longer supply the nutrients they need, people have to upgrade and find substitute sources. That, in fact, is how today's nutritional supplements came into being. Once that balance is lost, health suffers. So we must maintain a sensible diet, and maintaining a sensible diet, including supplementing nutrients, is a very necessary means of doing so.

Human history goes back more than three million years. Is what we eat now different from what people ate three million years ago? Completely different. Three million years ago people ate wild fruits and wild greens. Later they slowly learned to grow crops, and later still they learned to hunt. At first everything was eaten raw. Then came fire, and people began to eat cooked food. In what we eat, humankind has never stopped changing and renewing itself.

Today, too, we need to renew what we eat in order to keep our diet in balance. As human history moves forward, we all have a need to adjust and regulate the structure of our diet. If we do not, our needs and the material supply fall out of balance, and once they are out of balance a whole series of chronic diseases follows.

Cancer, cerebrovascular disease, heart disease, diabetes, gout and obesity: are these not all related to nutritional balance? In the past few lessons we said that our health is shaped by controllable and uncontrollable factors. Whichever kind of factor it is, what finally shows is the biochemistry of our bodies, and biochemistry is built on material substances. If you are happy today, if you feel joy, that joy is a biochemical reaction.

So we can look at everything about a person as biochemistry. Two people fall in love, two people have a fight, and all of it is biochemistry. If you feel irritable today, it is not necessarily because the matter at hand is so troublesome. It may be that your body is short of something, short of some substance, so your biochemical reactions are not right. It seems as if material abundance has reached a peak, and we can eat whatever we want. So why are there still so many problems with nutritional balance?

One of the reasons is the limitations of food itself today. The food we eat now may carry the same name as the food people ate in the past, but are the nutrients inside it the same? They are not.

Let me give an example. Food in the United States is already very safe. Yet in 1948, [[100 grams of spinach in America contained 100 milligrams of iron, while today 100 grams contains only 2 to 4 milligrams|The 1948 figure is the speaker's. It does not appear in published food tables, and spinach has long been credited with far more iron than it contains. What analysis of USDA data does show is a smaller real decline: comparing 1950 and 1999 figures for 43 garden crops, median levels of protein, calcium, phosphorus, iron, riboflavin and vitamin C fell by 6 to 38 percent, most likely because higher-yielding varieties were bred.|https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15637215/]]. Is that a big gap? An enormous one. And that is in America. It is easy to understand that the figure in China is certainly not going to be higher. So is every mouthful of vegetables we eat now carrying fewer nutrients than it used to? And compared with the past, do we eat more or less? You will notice that because most of us exercise less than before, our stomachs have become smaller, and naturally we eat less.

Add to that the problem of food safety, which now seriously threatens our health. Add the overuse of antibiotics and hormones in medicine, which again uses up large amounts of the nutrients in our bodies. Add the fact that people now eat a great deal of meat, and all of it comes from intensive farming. Does farming have problems of its own? Take shrimp farming. When one pond of shrimp has been harvested, what must happen next? Disinfection. Without it, all kinds of fungi and bacteria would remain and threaten the next pond. Fine, so you kill off every fungus and bacterium, and then you fill the pond again. Will there be disinfectant residue in the water? There certainly will.

On top of that, you will find that people who sell chicken do not eat chicken, and people who sell duck do not eat duck. A good friend of ours has a friend who runs a poultry farm on the outskirts of Beijing. That man phoned my friend himself and said, whatever you do, do not eat chicken these days. Why? Because I know how I raise my chickens. I know the process, so do not eat a single one. From hatching to slaughter takes thirty days. Would you dare eat that? These are conditions we have no power to change.

Then there are the changes in how we live. Has our way of life changed compared with ten years ago? Enormously. Were you living ten years ago the way you live today? Did you use your phone ten years ago the way you use it now? Before WeChat and Douyin were invented, did you scroll through your phone for hours as you do now? You did not. Our way of life has changed, and our eating habits have changed too, but what has not changed? The food on the table at our three meals has not changed much.

What you eat now is much the same as what you ate ten years ago. So if you do not understand nutritional supplementation and do not understand how to combine foods, it is very easy for your diet to fall out of balance, and an unbalanced diet will certainly produce all kinds of problems. That, essentially, is how today's [[chronic non-communicable diseases|慢性非传染性疾病, the Chinese term for what the WHO calls noncommunicable diseases, mainly heart and blood vessel disease, cancer, chronic lung disease and diabetes. The WHO counts them as the cause of about 43 million deaths in 2021, and lists unhealthy diet among their main risk factors.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/noncommunicable-diseases]] come about. That is all I will share today. In the next lesson we will discuss the human body and nutrients, and the reference intakes for nutrients. Thank you all for listening, and see you tomorrow.
