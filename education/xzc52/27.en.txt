# The Rest of the B Family: Niacin, B5, B6, B12 and Folate

Today we are going to look at the other brothers in the B family. Let us start with niacin, also known as vitamin B3. Niacin takes part in building up and breaking down carbohydrates, fats and proteins in the body, and in copying and repairing DNA. When niacin runs short, a person can develop low blood sugar, along with abnormalities in the blood and the skin. That person tires very easily, becomes depressed, sleeps poorly, and is prone to a duodenal ulcer. A severe niacin deficiency causes [[pellagra|癞皮病, literally "scabby skin disease." MedlinePlus describes pellagra as a disease caused by too little niacin or tryptophan, marked by scaly skin sores, especially on sun-exposed skin, together with diarrhea, loss of appetite, inflamed mucous membranes, and confusion or delusions. Untreated, it can cause severe nerve damage and death.|https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000342.htm]], which is the classic sign of a niacin deficiency. That is why niacin is also called the anti-pellagra vitamin.

The early symptoms are weight loss, fatigue, a very poor memory and insomnia. If the deficiency is not corrected in time, dermatitis and diarrhea follow, and eventually even dementia. The typical sign of a niacin deficiency is a symmetrical rash on the parts of the body that are exposed: the backs of the hands, the wrists, both forearms, the face, both elbows and the ankles.

What does this rash look like? It begins with tiny blisters. The skin turns rough and the surface becomes raised, with patches of little blisters underneath. Gradually it starts to itch and to peel. On some people it appears on the hands, on some at the wrists, on some across both cheeks, and on some on the feet. It is intensely itchy, and when it itches you scratch, and when you scratch the little blisters burst.

When these symptoms appear, they tell us we are already seriously short of niacin. Some people say this is no big deal, it is just itchy hands and nobody dies of that. But here is the real issue. Niacin is connected with the nervous system, so the symptoms I just described are only the early stage of the deficiency. What shows up later? Irritability, forgetfulness and insomnia. One kind of insomnia is that bedtime comes, you want to sleep, and you cannot fall asleep. The other kind is that you do fall asleep, but you wake at two or three in the morning and cannot get back to sleep. Both of these typical forms of insomnia are related to a lack of niacin. What they really mean is that your nervous system is no longer working quite normally.

If the deficiency goes further and becomes severe, it shows up as mania, hearing voices, confusion and even dementia. Put more simply, a person short of niacin is very prone to all kinds of mental and depressive symptoms. They cannot summon the energy for anything. Pregnant women in particular tend toward depression after giving birth. Holding her newborn, a mother thinks, "When will I ever get this child raised? This is exhausting," and her mood sinks. Sometimes, for no reason at all, she slips away and cries by herself.

Sometimes her husband or someone in the family makes a small mistake and she flies into a rage. In short, she cannot keep her temper under control, and this is even more pronounced around her menstrual period. But after the outburst, what happens? She swings to the opposite extreme and starts blaming herself, sinking into deep guilt: "Why did I lose my temper like that? What was the point?" The self-blame is severe too. If this is not handled well, it can later turn into depression and even suicidal tendencies. This is not someone who has planned suicide for a long time. It is someone whose mood suddenly drops and who suddenly feels like jumping from a building. All of this comes from a severe lack of niacin in the nervous system.

Next let us look at pantothenic acid, also called vitamin B5. [[A study was carried out in Iowa|The speaker is referring to work by Robert E. Hodges and colleagues at the University of Iowa College of Medicine, published in 1958 as "Pantothenic Acid Deficiency in Man," in which deficiency was deliberately induced in human volunteers through a controlled diet. The NIH's MedlinePlus notes that pantothenic acid deficiency is rare in ordinary diets.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1062846]] in the United States in which a group of volunteers was selected from a prison. They were all young men, and the researchers removed all the pantothenic acid from their food. In other words, what they ate contained no pantothenic acid at all.

After a period of time, these young volunteers developed listlessness, headaches, dizziness, weakness, a racing heart, cramps, colds that would not go away, and upper respiratory infections.

So when pantothenic acid is lacking, a person is prone to fatigue and headaches, especially migraines, and to dizziness. I mean unexplained dizziness, which later becomes the kind where you vomit and the whole room spins. That is definitely a pantothenic acid deficiency.

The heart also speeds up. This is called tachycardia, and it is not normal at all. You tend to get palpitations. I had this symptom myself more than ten years ago. You may laugh at me, but when my wife and I were dating and went for our evening walks, where did we walk? Not anywhere romantic. We walked in circles around Fuwai Hospital in Beijing, the national heart and vascular hospital, because I felt at the time that something was not right with my heart. Sure enough, as we walked my heart rate shot up. We were close to the hospital gate, so I pulled my wife along and we rushed inside. By the time they examined me, everything was fine.

I even wore a [[Holter monitor|The speaker says "hot," a shortening of Holter. A Holter monitor is a small portable device that records the heart's rhythm continuously, usually for 24 to 48 hours, to catch irregular beats that a short office ECG can miss.|]] for several days, and they still found nothing. Later I understood what was going on. By rebalancing myself with B vitamins, along with some other nutrients such as protein and Cal Mag, I finally got my tachycardia under control. What else happens when pantothenic acid is lacking? Cramps appear, immunity drops, and you catch colds easily and pick up infections easily.

People short of pantothenic acid also become emotionally low. They often feel uneasy and easily sink into gloom. They complain, they are irritable and quick to anger, and they are often spoiling for a fight. They are extremely negative, and whatever you say to them is wrong. So sometimes when we meet someone who is negative, we say, half joking, "I'm not going to argue with you. Here are a few B vitamins. Take them and then we'll talk." It is not that they do not want to be positive. The biochemistry of their body has no material foundation, so they cannot be positive. Without pantothenic acid nourishing the nervous system, how could they be?

No emotion is just an emotion. What is it? It is biochemical metabolism built on a physical foundation. Without pantothenic acid, a person is simply unlikely to be positive. Immunity also falls. How far can it fall? To the point where even a vaccine cannot produce antibodies. One of the functions of pantothenic acid is making antibodies, and if your immune response is too weak, the vaccination cannot take and no antibodies form.

Some people get the hepatitis B vaccine and simply do not produce antibodies, and this is the reason. A few days ago an old friend sent me a message on WeChat. Her daughter was about to get the COVID vaccine at work, and she wanted to know whether her daughter could keep taking B vitamins after the shot, because there is a long-standing belief that B vitamins cancel out medicines. What was my answer? Keep taking them, so that she produces antibodies better. A lack of pantothenic acid affects the immune system, and it also makes a person prone to all kinds of allergic symptoms.

So if you have allergic symptoms, or your child is especially prone to allergies, that is actually connected with a lack of pantothenic acid. It goes further. Pantothenic acid is tied to how well a person copes with stress, so people short of it handle stress poorly, and when you handle stress poorly, disease comes knocking. Asthma and stomach ulcers are in fact stress diseases. Many people treat a stomach ulcer as a stomach disease, but what is a stomach ulcer really? It is a stress disease, and we will talk about that in detail another time. In short, asthma and stomach ulcers are stress diseases, and they are largely related to a lack of pantothenic acid. Asthma in particular is linked to the pantothenic acid deficiency that long-term stress creates, and we see a lot of it in modern women.

Why do women tend to carry more stress? Because women are more emotional by nature, and they burn through more pantothenic acid than men do. Emotions drive your consumption of pantothenic acid very high.

That is why, if you look today, autoimmune diseases occur mostly in women. Long-term stress causes a lack of pantothenic acid, which leads to asthma and all kinds of autoimmune disease, including [[Addison's disease|艾迪生病. A condition in which the adrenal glands do not make enough of the hormones the body needs to respond to stress and regulate blood pressure. MedlinePlus explains that it is usually caused by the immune system mistakenly attacking the adrenal glands. The link to pantothenic acid is the speaker's view; mainstream medicine does not list B5 deficiency as a cause.|https://medlineplus.gov/addisondisease.html]], rheumatoid arthritis and lupus. All of these are related to pantothenic acid, and a long-term shortage makes these autoimmune problems very likely. Do not treat the diseases I just named as merely individual illnesses. These symptoms are telling you there is a large hole in your immune system. A condition like vitiligo belongs to the same category. It is an immune system problem, a problem of immunity, and it is related to pantothenic acid.

Next let us talk about vitamin B6. B6 takes part in making hormones and red blood cells, and it is essential for metabolism and for the immune system. Vomiting during pregnancy, for example, is a severe lack of B6. Many pregnant women regard vomiting as normal, but why is it that pregnant women abroad rarely vomit? Because they are not short of B6. So never regard vomiting in pregnancy as normal. It happens because the body is short of B6. And if the mother is short, is the baby not short as well? One shortfall is really two people's shortfall. [[Nausea and vomiting in pregnancy|Harvard's Nutrition Source notes that 40 mg of vitamin B6 twice daily reduced mild to moderate pregnancy nausea compared with placebo, and that the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists recommends B6-based treatment as a safe first-line option under medical supervision. Mainstream sources treat B6 as a treatment for morning sickness, not the cause of it being lacking. The adult upper limit for B6 is 100 mg a day.|https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/vitamin-b6/]]

A lack of B6 is related to the balance of sodium and potassium in the nerves, to fat metabolism and to the synthesis of nucleic acids.

What can a lack of B6 cause in a child? It can easily lead to mental and emotional disorder, including autism, depression and anemia, as well as seborrheic dermatitis, inflammation of the mouth and of the tongue. The child is very prone to dark circles under the eyes, the liver and kidneys are easily injured, liver and kidney function weaken accordingly, and the child has trouble sleeping.

The insomnia of a B6 deficiency is very characteristic, and it is different from ordinary insomnia. Some people cannot sleep because they are wound up: bedtime comes, they feel wide awake, and sleep will not come. With a B6 deficiency, you are already utterly exhausted, truly worn out, yet you lie in bed and cannot fall asleep. So tired, and still unable to sleep. That is the typical insomnia of a vitamin B6 deficiency.

If the deficiency is severe, walking feels as if you are stepping on cotton, floating with every step. These are the main symptoms of a B6 deficiency.

Next I want to talk about B12 and folic acid. I will take these two together. What are they in charge of? They are in charge of making DNA, so they are connected with cell division, and of course with the prevention of cancer. We will go into these two in more depth tomorrow.

B12 and folic acid are connected with tumors, because when these two are lacking, cell division goes wrong. When cell division goes wrong, errors creep into the copying, and the result is more tumor cells. So why do we say that people under heavy stress are prone to cancer? Because once B12 and folic acid are depleted, their cells are more likely to divide with errors. When cell division goes wrong, the body produces more tumor cells, it cannot clear them in time, and tumors and cancer develop.

Look at how often breast cancer occurs in women today, and lung cancer in men. A great deal of it has much to do with emotion. And what lies behind emotion? A shortage of the whole B family throughout the body. Why do women today take folic acid when they are pregnant? Because it keeps the division of the baby's brain cells normal, so the baby is not born with a malformation. That is cell division, and it needs both B12 and [[folic acid|叶酸. The CDC advises every woman who could become pregnant to take 400 micrograms of folic acid daily, starting at least a month before conception, because neural tube defects such as spina bifida form in the first weeks of pregnancy, often before a woman knows she is pregnant.|https://www.cdc.gov/folic-acid/about/index.html]]. The two complement each other and neither can be spared. Without one, the other is of almost no use. B12 and folic acid work together.

Every member of the vitamin B family is very important. They work in concert, and only when they are taken in balance can they do their full job of improving our quality of life and our physical condition.

So the B vitamins are a hard, non-negotiable need for people today. Getting them from newer sources, that is, from functional foods, has become very necessary. A shortage will certainly cause all sorts of problems, and people today are all short of them. Almost nobody is not, because the B vitamins are something modern life uses up in large quantities. So how much should you take? That depends on the person, and it depends on the time.

The AI, the so-called adequate intake, is 1.3 milligrams. One tablet of Nutrilite B Complex contains 1.2 milligrams, so clearly one tablet is not enough. If we look at the [[tolerable upper intake level|可耐受最高摄入量, abbreviated UL: the highest daily intake unlikely to cause harm. The figures the speaker quotes (1.3 mg and a UL of 50 mg) match vitamin B6 under China's reference values. In the United States, the adult UL for B6 is 100 mg a day; Harvard notes that long-term intake far above that can damage the nerves in the hands and feet.|https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/vitamin-b6/]], the national standard sets it at 50 milligrams. So the UL is 50 milligrams, and you can do the arithmetic yourself. If one tablet is 1.2 milligrams and you go up to 50 milligrams, how many tablets is that? Forty, right? So anything under forty is no problem, and taking under forty over the long term is no problem either. But usually we do not need to take anywhere near that many. Only a small number of people need that much, which is why the amount always depends on the person and on the time. The B family also includes biotin and a few others, which I will not go into here.
