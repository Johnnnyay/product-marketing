# Vitamin B2 and the Signs of Deficiency

In this lesson we focus on vitamin B2. Why give it special attention? Because when B2 is lacking, the symptoms are especially visible. The first function of B2 is its part in the metabolism of the three energy-producing substances. What are the three energy-producing substances? We covered them in the last lesson, and you can go back and listen. B2 is also involved in the body's nucleic acid metabolism. What is a nucleic acid? Inside the nucleus of every cell in the body is a genetic material called ribonucleic acid, and the body contains many kinds of ribonucleic acid. So when B2 is lacking, the metabolism of ribonucleic acid goes wrong.

Consider today's COVID testing. Why is it called a [[nucleic acid test|核酸检测, the everyday Chinese name for the PCR test for COVID-19, which detects the virus's genetic material. The speaker uses the familiar phrase as a bridge to the idea that nucleic acids are the body's genetic code.|]]? Because the virus first attacks your nucleic acid and corrupts its code, so your metabolism and replication produce errors. For ribonucleic acid, then, B2 is very important. I will not expand on this topic here. If I did, it would also touch on the relationship between B2 and the immune system, which is likewise very important.

So we see that B2 is related to the genetic material and to metabolism. Which are the metabolic diseases? Take [[gout|痛风, gout, is caused by long-standing high uric acid in the blood, which crystallizes as monosodium urate in and around the joints, and in the kidneys. It is now understood as a systemic metabolic disorder with links to kidney disease, cardiovascular disease and diabetes, which is the point the speaker makes.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4311063/]]. How does gout arise? Your uric acid is too high, urate salts are deposited, and gout appears. In severe cases it leads to kidney failure. Gout is a systemic disease, not an organ disease. When some people talk about gout they fixate on an organ, usually the liver, looking for the cause, and that is a mistake. It is not an organ disease. It is a systemic disease, and a systemic disease needs comprehensive management, because a great many organs take part in regulating it. Heart, liver, spleen, lungs and kidneys all need adjusting.

Adjusting these organs is like a row of dominoes. You pushed over one at the start, but now you have to stand up a whole row. That is exactly why systemic diseases, which is to say chronic diseases, are the biggest trouble we face.

B2 also raises the body's utilization of protein and promotes growth and development. Above all it maintains the integrity of the skin and the cell membranes, so it protects the function of the skin's hair follicles, mucous membranes and sebaceous glands.

B2 also takes part in cell growth and metabolism. It is a necessary nutrient for tissue metabolism and repair, it strengthens liver function, and it regulates the secretion of the adrenal glands.

In a person severely short of B2, adrenaline secretion is certainly abnormal, either too high or too low. When adrenaline is too high, much of what you show is a stress response: you are easily tense and your blood pressure rises easily. When it is too low, your blood vessels tend to go slack. So this regulation is related to B2. B2 also takes part in the overall metabolism of other nutrients. It is related to the body's absorption of iron, the storage of iron and the release of iron's energy, and it has antioxidant activity.

So let us focus on the symptoms of B2 deficiency. We said that B2 is related to the maintenance of mucous membrane tissue, which is one large class of its functions, and that shows up clearly in certain conditions. For example the [[oral-genital syndrome|口腔生殖综合症, the classic picture of riboflavin deficiency, ariboflavinosis: cracked and inflamed lips (cheilosis), sores at the corners of the mouth (angular stomatitis), a swollen magenta tongue (glossitis), seborrheic dermatitis around the nose and eyes, eye irritation, and a symmetrical dermatitis of the scrotum or vulva. The US RDA for riboflavin is 1.3 mg for men and 1.1 mg for women.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010026/]], which is a B2 deficiency disease. On the lips it shows as redness, and the corners of the mouth turn milky white, sometimes with cracks and even raw, broken skin.

So to see whether a person is short of B2, where do you look first? The lips. Meet a stranger, look first at his lips, and you can roughly judge whether he is short of B2. What color are normal lips? Red, a tender pink. Short of B2, they turn deep red, and with long-term deficiency they turn purple. So is someone short of B2?

First judge the color of the lips. Second, where? Look at whether the lips are cracked, peeling, or lined with vertical creases. Someone says, my lips are dry because I am short of water. Winter has come and you assume it is dryness and dehydration, but you find that the dry lips of B2 deficiency cannot be fixed with water. However much you drink, the lips keep peeling. Water does not solve it, and neither does lip balm. It is a lack of vitamin B2.

Many young women wear lipstick when they go out, and then you can see even more clearly how many vertical lines the lips have. One stroke of lipstick makes those lines stand out.

So when you look in the mirror before going out, do not only check the color of the lipstick. Take a moment to check whether you are short of B2 as well. By the time a person is severely deficient, only a thin lip is left, and it begins to bleed.

That is the simplest way to see whether someone is short of B2. Just look. You can press pause right here and go and check whether your family is short of B2. If B2 deficiency gets worse, it is no longer just cracked lips but raw, broken skin, and not only the lips: there may be severe deficiency signs elsewhere on the body.

For instance the hands begin to crack, and the heels crack, and you can see the red flesh showing through. These are all signs of severe B2 deficiency. A few years ago I met a young woman, and when we shook hands her hand felt rough and scaly. Embarrassed, she showed me: both hands were peeling all over, itchy and miserable. I told her then that with ten B complex tablets a day, she could see improvement within a week at the fastest. She did as she was told, went home, started on the B complex, and stopped eating anything spicy or salty.

About two weeks later she called me and said her hands were completely better. If you went to a doctor with this, the doctor would not know what you had done to get whole hands peeling, but it is simply severe vitamin B2 deficiency. Take B complex in full amounts, and for the first week you can also grind the B complex into powder and rub it on the hands, and in about a week it may be better.

Then there is stomatitis, sores at the corners of the mouth, and ulcers of the mouth lining. Any inflammation or ulcer of this kind in the mouth is related to B2 deficiency.

The tongue, too, is prone to glossitis, swelling and pain, including [[geographic tongue|地图舌, geographic tongue: irregular smooth patches on the surface of the tongue where the small papillae have been lost, shifting in shape like the outlines on a map. It is generally considered harmless and its cause is unclear; nutritional deficiency is one of the factors sometimes discussed.|]]. Have you seen a geographic tongue? The surface of the tongue peels in irregular patches, or the whole surface has cracked open. Sometimes your tongue feels a little sore, you look in the mirror, and the taste buds have gone flat, or the coating is very thick. These are all signs of B2 deficiency. How do you check for a swollen tongue? Lift your tongue and look in the mirror. If there are many tooth marks along the underside, you are already seriously short of the B group, because only a swollen tongue picks up the imprint of the teeth. Geographic tongue is more serious still, with the whole surface cracked.

Do not underestimate a person with a heavy tongue coating. He will certainly also have a strong taste in the mouth and bad breath, and such a person is certainly seriously short of B, unless it is some other problem of the mouth. In general, what Chinese medicine calls [[heavy dampness|湿气, dampness, a traditional Chinese medicine pattern marked by a thick tongue coating, heaviness, sluggish digestion and sticky stool. The speaker maps this picture onto riboflavin deficiency in nutritional terms.|]] is, in nutritional terms, a lack of vitamin B2. Adults must take this seriously in children. When you get home, look at your children's tongues. If half the tongue has taste buds and half does not, your child is already seriously short of the B group.

What else does B2 deficiency bring? It shows in the eyes, around the eyes, where it easily produces [[blepharitis|睑缘炎, blepharitis, inflammation of the edges of the eyelids and the lash follicles, with itching, burning, redness, crusting and excessive tearing. Its usual causes are bacterial and oily skin; riboflavin deficiency is one nutritional factor associated with eye irritation.|https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/blepharitis-a-to-z]]. That is a word you may not hear often. It brings sensitivity to light, tearing, tiredness, blurred vision, congested conjunctiva, and the growth of new capillaries in the cornea leading to conjunctivitis. Blepharitis shows as a particular sensitivity to light: when light comes, the eyes feel very uncomfortable. Then there is itching, and once the eyes itch, a person keeps rubbing them, especially a child. If a child is always rubbing his eyes, you must look at whether he is short of the B group. A child who keeps rubbing his eyes usually needs B vitamins. This is one sign of B2 deficiency.

Some will say these are allergy symptoms. What is an allergy? It is because you lack B2 that you become allergic. So with a shortage of B2 the eyes water easily. Sometimes a puff of wind and the tears come. Why so easily moved? It is nothing of the kind; it is a lack of B2. Not only do the tears flow easily, the eyes tire easily too, vision is blurred, the conjunctiva is congested, and the eyes are sometimes red. In particular, when your eyes are uncomfortable and eye drops do not help, it is usually a B2 problem. No need for eye drops; replenish B2 and it settles.

When are the eyes prone to bleeding? Overwork, late nights and too little sleep can all congest the conjunctiva and end in bleeding, and so can stress, emotion and drinking. All of these use up large amounts of vitamin B2. More serious cases bring capillary growth in the cornea and conjunctivitis, and it all shows around the eyes.

Third, B2 deficiency shows on the skin as seborrheic dermatitis in the folds beside the nose, between the eyebrows, on the eyelids and behind the ears. I had it behind my ears from my early teens, and only after I passed 30 did the seborrheic dermatitis finally leave me. Many people today work late night after night, and after a few nights find a pimple beside the nose. Why did it appear? Because fat sits there, and short of B2 it cannot be metabolized, so it settles and becomes inflamed. Push a finger along the nose and something white and oily comes out. That case is not so bad, because it is not blocked. If the pores become blocked, inflammation is certain, and the whole [[triangle|三角区, the "danger triangle" of the face, from the bridge of the nose to the corners of the mouth, where acne clusters and where infection is traditionally treated with caution because of the veins connecting to the brain.|]] breaks out in pimples.

So when the whole triangle, including the chin, is covered in pimples, that is certainly a lack of vitamin B2. B2 deficiency also shows in being easily inflamed, with very strong bad breath, so that you smell it from a distance. These are all signs of B2 deficiency, and severe deficiency may even produce lipomas, fatty lumps under the skin.

A couple of days ago a friend sent me a photograph. His friend's two arms were covered in small lumps of every size. How did they appear? He had been through a period of exhaustion, and as soon as he stopped to rest they came out. Put simply, this is B2 deficiency. Put more deeply, this person's immune system has certainly been badly damaged, and we will discuss that later when we come to the immune system. Of course, at that level of severity, alongside taking large amounts of B complex, he also needs to see a doctor.

In short, all of this comes from severe B2 deficiency. The fat cannot be metabolized, so it piles up in the skin. It also shows in the private parts. As B2 deficiency gets worse, symmetrical dermatitis appears in the private areas, in men on the scrotum and in women on the labia. Remember, it is symmetrical, a symmetrical dermatitis, and this is more serious still: a long-term internal deficiency of B2 that no one else can see and only you know about. Long-term B2 deficiency also causes slow growth in children, and mild to moderate iron-deficiency anemia. Today's children are widely short of B2. Why do I say that?
