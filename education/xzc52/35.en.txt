That is why people who are short of vitamin A tend to have low immunity. Whenever you want to raise your immunity, you need to pay attention to getting more vitamin A. If you find your immunity is low, what should you think of first?

Think first of concentrating on vitamin A. That is its relationship with the immune system. Today I will keep it short, because we will have a dedicated section on the immune system later.

Second, let us talk about children's growth and development. We know that the growth of the skeleton needs vitamin A. In fact it is not only height; the development of all the internal organs will be poorer. Before puberty, a child's organs are in a stage of storing up energy. Only with that store of energy will a child grow, and shoot up, at puberty. Nutrients are therefore crucial to building up that energy store in a child's organs, and all of this is tied to vitamin A.

Third, let us talk about vitamin A and inflammation in the body. Where does inflammation come from? We say inflammation is caused by keratinization of the body's epithelial tissue. Almost every organ in the body has epithelial tissue on its surface. When vitamin A is sufficient, our mucous membranes continually secrete mucus, which produces many protective cells that keep bacteria from invading. At the same time it keeps the surface of the tissue clean, so bacteria cannot survive in the mucus, and waste that the body cannot digest can also be cleared away by the mucous tissue. That is why healthy tissue does not accumulate dead cells.

So when vitamin A is not lacking, in the normal state, the body has the ability to clean itself, and our tissues are very healthy. But when vitamin A is lacking, the mucosal cells grow quickly and also die quickly. Once these cells die they pile up on one another, layer upon layer. Dead cells can no longer produce mucus, so the surface of the tissue can no longer be cleaned, and its ability to protect itself is lost. That is why the bronchi, the lungs, the sinuses, the kidneys, the bladder and the prostate are the places where disease easily develops.

Once disease develops, the air sacs of the organ become packed with dead cells, and once that happens, what forms? All kinds of what are called nodules, and the symptoms of all kinds of nodules appear. So wherever there is a nodule, what does that tell you? It tells you that cells there are dying. Thyroid nodules, lung nodules: these nodules must be the accumulation left behind after the mucous tissue has keratinized. And what do the lungs fear most? They fear nodules most, especially [[glass-like nodules|磨玻璃结节, usually called ground-glass nodules in English: hazy spots on a CT scan of the lung. Most are benign or caused by inflammation, but a persistent one is followed by doctors because some are early forms of lung adenocarcinoma. So inflammation is a condition brought on by keratinization of the epithelial tissue, and it can occur in many organs throughout the body. Keratinized tissue sheds, and the shedding produces inflammation, because the organ cannot clean itself. So large amounts of shed keratin and dead cells build up on the surface of the organ. Once an organ cannot clean itself, because without vitamin A it no longer produces mucus, it brings about a second symptom, called effusion, a buildup of fluid.

What kinds of fluid buildup are there? Fluid in the lungs, fluid in the pelvis, fluid in the uterus, and fluid around the heart, because the sac around the heart also has epithelial tissue. All of these fluid buildups bring inflammation, and they must be caused by a lack of vitamin A. That is one kind.

What is the other kind of fluid buildup? Cysts. When trapped fluid turns to pus, it forms a cyst. What kinds of cysts are there?
