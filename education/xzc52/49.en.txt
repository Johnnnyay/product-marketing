In fact, youthfulness shows first of all in young blood vessels, and young blood vessels have a great deal to do with blood lipids. The body has four kinds of blood lipid measurements: total cholesterol, triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein, and high-density lipoprotein. What is total cholesterol? It is one of the important risk factors for atherosclerosis, the hardening and narrowing of the arteries, and most people know something about that.

What are triglycerides? Triglycerides are the most abundant type of fat in the body. High-density lipoprotein is a protein that carries cholesterol from the tissues around the body back to the liver to be processed. It keeps the body's cholesterol at normal levels, so it is a protective factor against coronary heart disease.

And what is low-density lipoprotein? It is a risk factor for coronary heart disease, and it is often used to judge whether a person has an underlying risk of heart disease. Low-density lipoprotein also carries cholesterol, but it does not have much carrying capacity. Often, partway along the blood vessel, it runs out of fuel and unloads its cargo halfway, dumping the cholesterol anywhere, and that causes a traffic jam in the body.

So mainstream medical theory today holds that high blood lipids, high triglycerides, and high cholesterol may affect our health.

Of course this theory is now facing some challenges.
