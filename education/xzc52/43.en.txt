# Probiotics, Immunity, and the Gut-Brain Connection

Today we continue with probiotics, and specifically the relationship between probiotics and immunity. The main thing probiotics do for immunity is keep it stable, and a stable immune system is what builds a stronger constitution and eases allergy symptoms. We know the human large intestine holds bacteria on the scale of a hundred trillion, and among them are good ones, bad ones, and neutral ones. Will a hundred trillion bad bacteria have a bad effect on the body? Of course they will. So we cannot let those bad bacteria erode the body. Think about it: with that many harmful bacteria sitting in the gut, where do our immune cells and immune substances have to go? They all have to be concentrated in the large intestine, working there.

That is why we say the large intestine accounts for [[70 percent of the body's immunity|The figure comes from immunology: gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) is described as representing almost 70 percent of the entire immune system, and about 80 percent of the body's antibody-producing plasma cells are found there.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2515351/]]. In other words, of all the immune capacity a person produces each day, 70 percent is spent in the large intestine. Only the remaining 30 percent goes to the lungs, the skin, and everywhere else.

So if there are enough probiotics, the bad bacteria go down, and right away the large intestine does not need such a large immune force stationed there. Those immune troops no longer have to stay put. Where do they go? They get redeployed to the other organs, and your overall immunity rises quickly.

When enough armed police move into the gut, they take over the policing work that the regular army was doing there, and the army is freed up to keep order somewhere else. It is a simple analogy, but it holds.

We often hear the large intestine called the body's [["second brain"|The gut has its own extensive nervous system, the enteric nervous system, which communicates with the brain in both directions through the vagus nerve.
