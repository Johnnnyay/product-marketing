# Who Is Responsible for Your Health

Welcome to lesson eight. Yesterday we covered the first thing health management teaches us. Do you remember it? The heart of health management is immunity. Health management also answers a second question: who is responsible for your health? You are. You have to learn to make your own decisions. Why is the country pushing health management as a discipline? To tell every one of us that from now on we have to take responsibility for our own health. When the state promotes it at the national level, the message is that this is something you must take full responsibility for, because if people do not, the country cannot carry the load either. In 2018 our country's investment in medical care was [[5.9 hundred million yuan|The speaker's figure is 5.9亿. China's total health expenditure in 2018 was about 5.9 trillion yuan (5,912 billion), or 6.57 percent of GDP, so the intended figure is most likely 5.9 trillion. The 8 percent figure echoes a breakdown widely attributed to the WHO in Chinese health education, in which medical care accounts for 8 percent of what determines health.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7918561/]], yet it accounted for only 8 percent of the effect on treating disease. So the country has realized that with an investment this large, if the people do not learn about health themselves, there will be no remedy at all.

What should we learn, and how? People often ask me which books to read. Here I will recommend three introductory books and four reference books. Do not think that is too many; I will go through them slowly.

The three introductory books are the kind where, if you master even one of them, you will already gain a great deal of health management knowledge. None of them is dry to read, and they connect closely to real life. The first one I recommend is [[The Lost Nutrition|失传的营养学, "Lost Nutrition", a popular Chinese-language book by Wang Tao that explains everyday health problems through the lens of cell damage, repair, and the nutrients needed as raw materials.|]]. It is very readable and suits the great majority of people as a starting point. Once you are comfortable with it, the second book I recommend is [[Healing Without Drugs|非药而愈, literally "healing without medicine", a popular Chinese-language book on improving health through diet and lifestyle.|]]. If both of those are fine for you, I suggest you go on to read a four-volume series, the Nutrition and Health Treasury, written by [[Adelle Davis|An American nutrition writer (1904 to 1974) whose books, including Let's Eat Right to Keep Fit, Let's Get Well, and Let's Have Healthy Children, were bestsellers in the mid-twentieth century and were published in Chinese as the 吃的营养 series. Some of her specific recommendations were criticized by the medical establishment of her day.|]]. The four volumes are, in their Chinese editions, Eating Right for Health Care, Eating Right for Healing, Eating Right for Mother and Baby, and The Science of Eating Right.

Of these three, in my own view, if you finish just the first, The Lost Nutrition, and Healing Without Drugs, you will already have gained a great deal.

Then there are the four reference books. What is a reference book? Do not try to chew through it page by page. You will not get through it, because these are highly professional books. Keep them close at hand, and when you meet a specific symptom or problem, go straight to the relevant section and look it up. Treat it as a dictionary. Do not read from the first page to the last. Used that way, the effect is actually very good.

Of course, if you have some professional background, I do suggest you master all four. They are Functional Foods, which is a university textbook; Pathology; Health Management, also a university textbook; and the fourth is the thick green Dietary Guidelines for Chinese Residents, which is heavier than a laptop. Keep these four as references to consult, and when a specific health problem comes up, look it up in a targeted way, and it will work well for you. So you see, it is fairly simple. It is not that complicated, and it is not hard. Most of the knowledge we teach comes from university textbooks, because most of what is in a university textbook is recognized by experts and scholars. It is not one person's opinion. Once something becomes a textbook, different people can write it, but the content inside does not change much.

Last year, for example, I read a university textbook on functional foods. I found two editions, written by two different groups of professors, and what was inside was essentially the same. So where does our basic theoretical knowledge come from? From university textbooks, including the Health Management textbook. And if you want some general medical knowledge, there is another book you can read, which is Pathology.

Pathology is a medical school textbook. Anyone who becomes a doctor, a nurse, or a health worker has to study it. Why should you read it if you are not going to be a doctor? At the very least, keep it at home as a reference. You are not a doctor, but does your home still keep a medicine cabinet? By the same logic, even though you are not studying medicine and are not going to practice, do we not carry an inescapable responsibility for the health of our family?

If a home should always keep a medicine cabinet, should it not also keep these health management reference books? When we do not understand how many diseases work, problems come easily. Here is a case. A three-year-old child had a cold and a fever and took medicine, and in the end his urine protein was four plus. The doctor said the urine protein was far too high, the protein was leaking badly, so what did the doctor switch him to? A steroid, [[prednisone|强的松. Oral corticosteroids such as prednisone are the standard first-line treatment for idiopathic nephrotic syndrome in children, and 80 to 90 percent of children go into complete remission. Most then relapse, and repeated courses bring side effects such as slowed growth, bone thinning, mood changes and cataracts, which is why doctors try to limit total steroid exposure.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5623563/]], six tablets a day. Did the urine protein clear up? Of course it did. At the strength of six prednisone tablets a day, how could it not? He took them, and the urine protein cleared.

The key is what happened next. The next time he caught a cold again, ran a fever again, coughed again, the urine protein came back. What then? With this way of thinking, the child is put through the same thing over and over. He gets better, catches a cold, gets better, catches a cold, and every cold means prednisone. In the end this child's kidneys will slowly deteriorate, because prednisone is bound to harm the liver and the kidneys.

The final result is chronic nephritis, kidney failure, and even uremia. Where can you read about this? Pathology spells it out clearly: [[the main cause of uremia today|The speaker attributes this to his pathology textbook. The mainstream view, from the US National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases, is that the most common causes of chronic kidney disease in adults are diabetes and high blood pressure; drugs that are toxic to the kidneys are listed among other causes.|https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/causes]] is drugs of the steroid and antibiotic types. That is what the book says. But if you go to the hospital, what can the doctor give you? Either antibiotics or steroids, right? When you go to a doctor, that is what the doctor has to give you; he has nothing else. If they are used improperly, what is the end result? The kidneys are ruined and the urine protein is fixed. The symptom is cured, but the kidneys are finished.

So we have to be clear with ourselves: are we trying to suppress the urine protein, or are we trying to protect the kidneys? In the procedures of some small hospitals, suppressing your urine protein is exactly what they will do, and there is nothing wrong with that. But whose job is it to protect the kidneys? It is your own job. And if the kidneys fail, what then? Dialysis, which brings your creatinine down. That is the hospital's responsibility, and there is nothing wrong with it either. But whose responsibility is it to protect the kidneys? It has nothing to do with the doctor. It has to do with you and only you.

Under the hospital's standardized procedure, if your urine protein is too high, you may well be given prednisone, and if, in the worst case, it goes on until both kidneys fail, then by the procedure you get dialysis. That is the doctor's responsibility and the standardized medical procedure. If you have no basic knowledge of pathology yourself, will our children not end up in a very bad way?

There are also many people today who put far too much faith in the internet. Here is a case. A young man, not yet thirty, stayed home every day. Holed up at home, he lived on instant noodles and junk food until he ate himself into high blood pressure. He did not go to the hospital for it either, because there is so much information online now. He bought some blood pressure medicine online and took it at home. What was the result? He developed gout, which was very painful. So he went online again to look up painkillers, bought some, and took those. The result? Both kidneys failed. Once you reach kidney failure, there is nothing more you can do at home, and the only place left is the hospital.

So we say you must understand some pathology. Sometimes not knowing really does lead to serious trouble. That is why the country is promoting health management. It is telling you that you cannot rely only on Western medicine, and you cannot rely only on Chinese medicine. You yourself must have professional knowledge to make judgments. And you cannot get that professional knowledge from hearsay. You need to properly study a little knowledge that is authoritative and systematic. Once you have some knowledge and some understanding of nutrients, you can even hold a real conversation with your doctor.

Across China's medical system, doctors vary a great deal in their level. Some doctors listened carefully in class when they were students. Some may have passed with sixty out of a hundred and called it done. A lot of what they learned in pathology they may never use, and what we do not use, we forget. That is completely normal.

Between school and work, whatever we learn and do not use, we eventually forget. Your high school physics and chemistry books, if you picked them up today, you might not be able to follow, even though you once studied them. So a medical worker who studied something but no longer uses it often may well have forgotten it. In a hospital, if a doctor only follows the standardized procedure, he is unlikely to remember every other part of pathology that does not concern his work. But who needs to understand it? We ourselves need to understand it, because whose hands is our health in? Our health is held in our own hands.

In addition, once you have read The Lost Nutrition, Healing Without Drugs, and the four Adelle Davis books, then when you actually start putting nutrients together, you absolutely need that big volume, the [[Dietary Guidelines for Chinese Residents|中国居民膳食指南, the national dietary guidelines published by the Chinese Nutrition Society, with editions in 2007 and 2016 around the time of this course. The Society also publishes the Chinese Dietary Reference Intakes, which set recommended intakes and upper limits for each nutrient.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6849297/]]. It contains the dietary reference intakes, how much of each nutrient you should take in, and you have to master that too.

With that knowledge, you stop listening to and believing everything you hear. Otherwise you put too much pressure on your doctor, and your own health risk becomes very high. If a doctor understands nutrients in addition to medicine, his advice will be fairly objective. But if there are things a doctor does not understand, you cannot get nutrient advice from him.

That is why you will sometimes find that during radiotherapy and chemotherapy, some doctors advise patients to take more nutrients, more protein, more vitamin C, while other doctors tell you not to take anything at all. So what should we do? Study what we need to study, because whose health is it? It is our own, right? When it comes to advice on daily life and nutrients, doctors are not the experts, because that is not their specialty. Some doctors have studied other fields on their own; some focus on the specialty they already have. Both are completely normal, because they are not responsible to anyone in those other areas and are not required to be. So who is responsible?

We are, are we not? And if you are responsible, you have to learn. You cannot push everything onto your doctor. Doctors sometimes feel wronged, too. As a doctor sees it: you came in with a cold and a fever, so I bring your temperature down. You have inflammation, so I treat the inflammation. You have cancer cells, so I find a way to destroy them. That is my duty. This is what we call clinical medicine. To give a slightly irreverent example, there is a joke that has long gone around the medical schools.

In ancient times there was a man who specialized in curing hunchbacks. One day a hunchback came to him and asked, can you cure my hunched back? The man said, certainly, and took two boards and pressed them together hard. Was the hunchback cured? Yes. The trouble was that the patient was dead. It is only a joke, of course, but what does it show? It is not that hospitals are bad. Hospitals are very good. They save lives and heal the injured, and they pull a great many people back from the edge of death. The treatments used in surgery today, for example, are truly excellent. But when it comes to health, the hospital cannot study for you and cannot understand for you. We are not saying do not go to the hospital. We are saying you need to know when you should go, what preparations you have made yourself before you go, and at the same time you need to take some responsibility for yourself.

Especially once the hospital has brought your symptoms under control, you yourself must take responsibility for raising your own immunity. Only then will you truly get better, right? These are all things we now have to learn, and if we do not, disasters can happen, whatever age we are. The country understands this problem too, which is why it recommends that everyone learn. Why do so many people know nothing about health management? Because in the past the people it served were a very narrow group, concentrated at the top of society. Leaders at the central and provincial levels had health management services, and so did a group of high-end entrepreneurs and business leaders.

In Zhejiang Province, for example, an Entrepreneurs' Health Management Club was founded in 2018, and only those who paid more than ten million yuan a year in taxes could become members. I think it is safe to say most of us would never get in. So in the past the field of health management operated at a very high level, and ordinary people could not access it. Now things are different. As the country pushes the big-health industry, more and more ordinary people should be able to enjoy health management services. And who will serve you? You serve yourself first, and after that, can you not serve your own family as well?

The scientific foundation of health management addresses our problem today, which is that [[chronic non-communicable diseases|慢性非传染性疾病, chronic diseases that are not passed from person to person, chiefly cardiovascular disease, cancer, chronic respiratory disease and diabetes. The WHO reports that they caused at least 43 million deaths worldwide in 2021, with tobacco use, unhealthy diets and harmful use of alcohol as major risk factors.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/noncommunicable-diseases]] have entered an explosive phase. We then learn which of the risk factors along the way can be addressed through nutrition.

A person generally moves from health to disease by going from a low-risk state to a high-risk state, then to early pathological changes, then to clinical symptoms, and finally to a disease. This process is fairly long, often several years, a decade or more, even several decades. Along the way, genetic, social, natural environmental, medical, and lifestyle factors all bear on the chronic disease that finally appears. So over such a long process, we can use nutrition and nutrients in a targeted way for prevention and intervention, and it is possible to successfully block, slow, or even reverse the development of the illness.

Because chronic non-communicable diseases take so long to develop, even if we cannot cure them all at the root, controlling their high-risk factors is entirely achievable. That is where the value of health management lies. It is the value of our learning, and the foundation of each person's understanding. So health management is not a single method. It is more like a complete and carefully designed program. That is where we will stop for today. Tomorrow we will talk about the four steps of health management. Thank you, everyone.

See you tomorrow.
