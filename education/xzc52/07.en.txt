# What Health Management Really Means

Welcome to lesson seven. Today we are going to study the concept of health management, and the first question is simply this: what is health management as a discipline?

Yesterday I walked you through some of the big-picture health data for China, and I suspect that after hearing it, most of you did not feel optimistic. So does the government know about this situation? Of course it does. That is why, in 2016, [[President Xi|The speaker uses 习大大, an affectionate popular nickname for Xi Jinping, General Secretary of the Communist Party of China and President of the People's Republic of China.|]] put forward the concept of a [[Healthy China|健康中国. The Healthy China 2030 plan, approved by the Chinese government in 2016, set health as a national strategic priority for the first time, with goals running through 2030.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6107941/]] and told the country that without health for everyone there can be no [[moderate prosperity for everyone|The slogan is 没有全民健康就没有全民小康. 小康, "xiaokang", is the official term for a moderately prosperous society, the standard of living the Chinese government set as its development goal.|]]. For the first time, health was lifted to a much higher strategic position. And beginning last year, the ten years from 2020 to 2030 are being treated as the most valuable decade for health and wellness in China.

In fact, 2017 had already become the first year of China's national health strategy. That year a new data report came out, the China Family Health Big Data Report, and its message was that in the future, health management has to be done at the level of the family. So health has gradually moved from the hospital to the community, and from the community into the home. If we do not manage health family by family, moderate prosperity for everyone will be very hard to reach. We have already seen what happens when we rely entirely on hospitals: the diseases keep multiplying and the patients keep multiplying.

We can no longer depend purely on the hospital. At the national level, this idea and this strategy are already in place, and the country has now launched a new academic discipline called health management. It is a brand-new major that university medical schools are only just beginning to offer. The majors we studied in the past, clinical medicine and nursing for example, are all what we call medical majors. Health management is a new one. It used to be called preventive medicine; now it has become health management. When we study it, the hope is that we get a body of knowledge that is systematic and scientific. And what textbook does it use? The textbook used in universities today is simply called Health Management. So under these conditions the country introduced this new discipline. Worldwide, it has only existed for a little more than twenty years, and in China it is genuinely new.

So what is actually inside health management? If you have a copy of the textbook in your hands, you will discover something surprising: there is nothing new in it. Health management contains no new knowledge, as such. What it tells every one of us is that you have to take responsibility for your own health. But can you take responsibility for yourself without the right concepts and the right knowledge? Obviously not. So there has to be a new learning process, a process of building new abilities. Where does that professional knowledge come from, and what exactly do you need to learn?

First, you need at least a little of the basics of Western medicine. When you go for a physical exam, should you be able to read some of the numbers in your own report? If your triglycerides are high or your blood lipids are high, should you understand what that actually means? Of course you should. And if you do not understand, what do you do? You can look it up in an encyclopedia online, and in the future we may well arrange sessions to teach everyone how to read a physical exam report. So facing health management, we need some basic knowledge of Western medicine, including the basics of testing. You need to know when to go to the hospital, what problem you are going there to solve, where the benefits of the hospital lie, and where the possible side effects lie. You have to know all of that.

Second, learn some traditional Chinese medicine. Study a little of the relevant theory, and in particular [[the TCM idea of treating illness before it arises|治未病, "treating what is not yet disease", is a foundational principle of traditional Chinese medicine from the Huangdi Neijing: the best physician intervenes before disease appears. The transcript's characters are a homophone; this is the concept the speaker is describing.|]], which is especially valuable. What are acupuncture, gua sha, tendon stretching, cupping and tuina massage actually for? Learn a little TCM, and do not reject everything the moment the words "Chinese medicine" come up.

Third, learn some psychology. For a great many people, more than half of their health problems are connected to their psychology, so you need a little psychology too. And then your lifestyle habits have to change.

So health management contains no newly discovered knowledge. What it teaches you is how to make decisions: when to use Western medicine, when to use Chinese medicine, when to use nutrition, when to work on your own psychology, and when to change your own habits. That is what health management is. There is no new knowledge, so why push a whole discipline like this? To tell you one thing: can Western medicine completely solve a person's health? No. Can Chinese medicine completely solve it? Also no. So who can completely solve it in the end? It has to be you. This principle we must understand, we must grasp, and we must also learn to put into practice. If you do not understand it, big problems follow.

Take a normal case of a child with a fever. A fever brought on by the flu or a common cold is completely ordinary. Is 38.5 degrees Celsius high? If it is not an inflammation but is caused by a virus, can it be handled at home? Very often, yes, that is roughly enough. Do you really have to run to the hospital for medication? If you go straight to the hospital, what can the hospital give you? [[Motrin|美林, Mei Lin, is the Chinese brand name of children's Motrin, the ibuprofen suspension used in China for fever and pain. Fever in a child is a normal response to infection and does not itself require treatment; ibuprofen needs care in children who are dehydrated or have kidney problems.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4070461/]]. Go and look up for yourself what Motrin, that is, ibuprofen, actually is. It is an antipyretic and analgesic, a fever-reducing and pain-relieving drug. It is suitable for fevers caused by the common cold and influenza in children, and it can also relieve mild to moderate pain in children, including headache, joint pain, nerve pain, toothache and muscle pain. But it must be used under a doctor's guidance. Excessive doses and prolonged use cause side effects, with reactions in the stomach and intestines. It is contraindicated for people with allergies and asthma. And most important of all, it should not be used for pain for more than five days, and not for fever for more than five days either. What usually happens is that we read the list of benefits at the top and know nothing at all about the warnings underneath.

That reminds me of one of my roommates at university. Nothing was ever wrong with him, but at the slightest thing he would pull out two Western pills and swallow them. Back then I actually thought he was good at looking after himself. Nearly twenty years have gone by, and recently I heard he is still doing it. Now that I understand, I asked him: what is that medicine you take when nothing is wrong? He said it was [[anti-inflammatory medicine|消炎药, literally "anti-inflammation drug", is the everyday Chinese term for antibiotics, which are often taken casually in China for minor ailments.|]]. Why take anti-inflammatories? He said he was afraid of getting sick, so every week he took a little medicine to nip any small inflammation in the bud. Just listening gave me a headache. I said, do you not know that you cannot take antibiotics casually? He said, in America everybody takes them. I said, have you not looked carefully at what Americans are actually taking? Pills and nutritional supplements look exactly the same.

This friend of mine has since emigrated to North America, and later he got it straight and came back to tell me. He said, what Americans take is not medicine. What is it called? [[Nutrilite|纽崔莱. Nutrilite is Amway's brand of vitamins and dietary supplements, sold in the United States since the 1930s and widely known in China; the speaker's whole course treats it as the reference standard for nutritional supplements.|]]. You see, with one hint from me, he stopped taking Western medicine from then on and switched to Nutrilite to look after his health instead. As for why he takes it, I suspect he cannot really explain that either.

In China, every winter, there are children whose fever from seasonal influenza ends up, in some cases, in death. Very often the problem is not the hospital. When a child at 38.5 degrees is carried to the hospital, will the doctor say to you, "This is a viral fever, there is nothing much to treat, give the child plenty of water, go home and give plenty of protein and plenty of vitamin C"? Will the doctor say that? No. If the doctor said that, the doctor would be irresponsible, would he not? You carried your child to the doctor, so what do you want the doctor to do? Bring the fever down. The doctor's responsibility is to bring the fever down.

In some small local hospitals there have been extreme cases like this one. The doctor sees 38.5, thinks that is rather high, and goes straight to Motrin. Motrin is given, it is not the right choice, and the child goes into whole-body convulsions, because Motrin can have side effects on the nervous system. The convulsions panic the parents, and they rush the child to a big hospital. At the big hospital the doctor takes one look and says, you have come too late, this child is already in serious shape. What follows? A full-body checkup, the whole battery of tests, and the child is put through the wringer. Meanwhile the parents have no concept of health management whatsoever, no idea of preventive care, and no nutrients of any kind are given to the child. So what you find is that the child's immunity gets weaker and weaker, and after one week, two weeks, the temperature still will not come down. If a mother has no concept of this, does the child not suffer terribly?

We know that [[influenza is a viral infection|Influenza is an acute respiratory infection caused by influenza viruses. Antiviral drugs work best when given as soon as possible after symptoms begin, and severe cases can progress to pneumonia and sepsis.|https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/influenza-(seasonal)]], and no drug cures it. There is no drug that can wipe the virus out; that is common knowledge. At most a drug can suppress the virus's replication. But is there only one way to suppress the virus's replication? Does it have to be a drug? Can we not defeat the virus by raising the child's immunity? Drive the virus out, and the body has produced antibodies as well, so the next time the child meets this virus his immunity is already up. Is that not better?

Instead, by this point the fever has gone on for more than two weeks, and the drugs are no longer working very well. If you insist on using a drug to suppress viral replication, the effect is quite good in the first two days, in other words in the first two days after you catch the flu. But two weeks have passed, the virus is already very strong, and if you use the drug now the effect may lag behind. The parents, with no concept of any of this, still do not give the child anything to raise immunity. What is the final result? The virus moves into the lungs. The lung infection brings complications, and then, generally speaking, there is only one place left to go: the ICU. In the ICU the drugs continue, the child's immunity gets weaker and weaker, and suddenly the child can no longer breathe on his own and has to go on a ventilator. Once the ventilator is on, it is twenty thousand yuan a day, and in the end the bill may run to well over a hundred thousand. This is a true story. Is it not a terrible pity? And whose problem is this? The problem is that the mother did not have this knowledge.

The mother lacked the relevant knowledge. It is not the hospital's responsibility. When you carry your child into the hospital, the hospital follows its standard procedure. You come in with a fever, they bring the fever down. You convulse, they give you drugs to support the nerves. You cannot breathe, they put you on a ventilator. That is the hospital's standard process, and anything beyond it is not within their scope of responsibility. That is perfectly normal; the hospital does not carry that responsibility. And in the hospital, when the doctor tells you not to eat this, not to eat that, no health products, no nutrients, that is also perfectly normal, because from where he stands he does not know what you are taking, and it is not within his scope of responsibility. It is something you yourself are supposed to know.

So why is the country pushing health management? To tell you who makes the decisions: you make the decisions. Everyone understands this. Otherwise, the Western doctor tells you everything outside the hospital is wrong, and Chinese medicine is wrong too. Then you go outside, and the Chinese medicine practitioner tells you Western medicine is wrong and health products are wrong too. Then you find the person selling health products, who tells you that Western medicine and Chinese medicine both seem to be wrong. So who is right and who is wrong?

Have you seen someone around you with [[shingles|带状疱疹, herpes zoster. It is the reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus, the chickenpox virus, which lies dormant in nerve ganglia after childhood infection; the chance of reactivation rises with age and with weakened immunity.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC172899/]]? When it flares up, they run to the hospital for injections and pills. They take the Chinese medicine, they take the Western medicine, they try the folk remedies, and it simply will not get better. Where is the problem? They do not know that shingles is a viral infection. With a viral infection, as long as your immunity does not rise, nothing you do will clear it completely. But once your immunity rises, whatever you take and whatever you do, it will get better. So you often see shingles that will not heal for half a year, a year, two years, with pain every single day. As long as immunity does not rise, it does not heal. And because it does not easily kill anyone, many people do not take it seriously either.

That is the first sentence health management gives you: the heart of health management is immunity. That is where we will stop for today. Tomorrow we will talk about who is responsible for your health.
