# How Sleep Debt Destroys a Body

Hello, friends. Today is January 7, and this morning Beijing's low temperature hit minus 19.5 degrees Celsius, the coldest since 1966, with gusts reaching force six to seven. This winter really is bitterly cold, and everyone's health, daily routine and even financial health are being tested by it. Whether you are at home or at work, be sure to keep warm and protect yourselves from the cold. Today in particular, please look in on the older people in your family. A sudden drop in temperature like this is hardest of all for the elderly to cope with. I wonder how everyone slept last night.

Yesterday I said that a great many people today are carrying a sleep debt. So let me ask you one more question: do you know the three measures of sleep? When you talk to people about sleep, you must bring a health mindset to it, and talk to them about the three key measures of a sleep problem. The first is duration: is it long enough? Do you sleep five hours a day or eight hours a day? Is it enough? Do you know about sleep cycles?

The second question is timing: is it reasonable? You might go to sleep at two in the morning and get up at nine, or go to sleep at ten at night and get up at six. Both are eight hours, but they are not the same.

Third, is the quality of your sleep good? Do you dream a lot or not? Does sleep take your tiredness away? When you get up, does your body feel fresh and clear? These are the three measures of sleep. If any one of the three is off, that person needs to solve the sleep problem, either on their own or with someone's help. And notice that solving the sleep problem is itself a matter of mindset.

First we need to know that poor sleep very easily leads to health problems. Sleep is one corner of a person's overall health. Lift up that corner and you can see into the body's other problems. People with stomach cancer do not sleep well, that is certain. People with liver cancer always have their days and nights reversed and always tend to stay up late. When liver cancer reaches the point where liver function breaks down, the person is wired at night and groggy during the day. Why? The hormones and electrolytes in the body have all changed, so the person stays up late whether they want to or not. People with poor livers naturally end up [[sleeping by day and wide awake at night|Sleep-wake inversion, restless nights with heavy daytime sleepiness, has been recognized since 1954 as a feature of advanced liver disease and hepatic encephalopathy; milder sleep problems are common in cirrhosis generally.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6220431/]].

Once we have this kind of health mindset and can recognize the problem, then we can talk about what might help us improve our sleep.

Earlier I mentioned sour jujube seed. What does sour jujube seed do? It is used specifically to nourish yin. What does nourishing yin mean? It works in your brain, helping it build the receivers for sleep signals. One side sends out a substance called [[GABA|Gamma-aminobutyric acid, the brain's main calming neurotransmitter. The speaker describes it as a nutrient. A 2020 systematic review of 14 human trials found limited evidence that oral GABA reduces stress and very limited evidence that it improves sleep, and whether it crosses into the brain is still debated.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7527439/]], the other side has something that receives GABA, and once the two connect, you fall asleep. Do not worry if GABA is unfamiliar. We will cover it in later lessons. But whatever you do, do not think of it as a sleeping pill. GABA is a substance that eases emotions and relieves pressure. It calms the mind, relieves stress and supports sleep.

GABA is something even children sitting exams can take. We cannot casually recommend it for children, of course, but research overseas on GABA found that when students took it before exams, their emotions were less likely to swing during the exam, and they were calmer and more composed.

Overseas, people now spread GABA directly onto bread. It is a food-grade nutrient, and people who eat bread containing GABA find their emotions steadier and are less prone to anxiety and tension. Add to that the steadying effect of calcium, which also matters a great deal for sleep, and the steadying effect of the B vitamins, which matter for sleep as well. When I explain these things, some people say, how wonderful, how much you know. And I tell them: what looks like fresh knowledge to you now may, ten years from now, have become common sense for the next generation.

Today, to talk about why sleep matters so much, I have to start with a news story from a few days ago. In the early hours of December 29, 2020, at 1:30 a.m., a young Pinduoduo employee named Zhang was walking home with colleagues after working overtime when she suddenly collapsed and lost consciousness. Her colleagues immediately called the emergency line and she was taken to a local hospital in Urumqi. After six hours of emergency treatment, Zhang still passed away. She was only twenty-three. From beginning to end, Pinduoduo's statements never acknowledged that overtime and late nights caused her sudden death, and never mentioned how intense her workload was. Some lawyers also pointed out that because it happened on the way home, it might not even count as a work injury. But in reality, this young woman's sudden death had a great deal to do with the late nights caused by the intensity of her work.

After the news came out, many people began discussing endless overtime, the [[996 work schedule|Working 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., six days a week, about 72 hours, common at Chinese tech companies at the time. China's legal standard workweek is 44 hours, and in August 2021 the Supreme People's Court and the labor ministry jointly issued guidance treating 996 as illegal.|]], alternating long and short weeks, and even the unfairness of swapped days off. Zhang's colleagues said that going to bed after midnight had long been normal for her, and that she had said more than once that she found it hard to fall asleep. Day after day, a workload with no floor had worn through the floor of her sleep.

Very soon a popular question appeared on Zhihu, the Chinese question-and-answer site: at what moment did you realize that health matters most? The top answer was a single short sentence: when I was lying in a hospital bed. Even CCTV's official microblog put it bluntly: happiness may be won through hard work, but you must never trade your life for money.

This event also captures the way many people born in the 1990s live in China's big cities. By one average estimate, [[people born in the 1990s hold four insurance policies each, 2.7 of them health insurance|A figure the speaker cites from Chinese insurance-industry reporting of the time. We could not locate the original survey.|]]. Many use buying health insurance as a substitute for real health awareness. What is their logic? Hurry up and buy while the insurance company's door is still open to you, because right now you still qualify.

Let me say this: you may have bought the most expensive insurance, but you are also working the most punishing overtime. I am not singling out insurance here. I once joked with someone that buying more insurance than other people, and more expensive insurance, does not mean you will live healthier or longer than they do. It just means your coffin will look a bit grander than theirs, because you leave money behind. But who among the living competes over coffins? Of course I think insurance is something everyone needs, and you should buy what you need, but an insurance policy is not health, and knowing how to invest does not mean you live longer. Wouldn't you agree?

So today let us use this story as a starting point to talk about how long-term lack of sleep, or poor sleep quality, in other words too much sleep debt, can destroy a person. First, if you rarely get to bed before midnight and often stay up all night, your [[qi and blood|气血, in Chinese medicine the body's vital energy and the blood that carries nourishment. When both are "empty" or deficient, a person is said to be run down, pale and easily tired.|]] become depleted. Next, lack of sleep forces your body into stress relief on its own.

Why does the body relieve stress on its own? Because if it did not, the brain would break down. Its way of relieving stress is to call up some feeling of relaxation and go looking for something pleasurable. How does the body push you there? By releasing a [[hunger hormone|Ghrelin, the hormone that signals hunger. In a well-known 2004 study of healthy young men, two nights of restricted sleep raised ghrelin by 28 percent, lowered the satiety hormone leptin by 18 percent, and increased hunger by 24 percent.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3632337/]]; you can look it up online. At that point your appetite grows, and you especially crave strongly flavored food, because eating gives the brain a feeling of happiness. So you badly want to eat when you should not be eating, and when you should be eating you have no appetite at all.

Please pay attention. If this happens to you, you need to realize clearly that this is not hunger caused by low blood sugar. It is false hunger. Put simply, you are starting to gain weight. Staying up late and sleeping badly also damage the liver, and the liver is in charge of fat metabolism. With the liver damaged, fat cannot be cleared away, and that forms the first vicious circle: the more you eat, the fatter you get. When you eat at the wrong times, you put on weight.

Next, some people think, I will go to the gym. Even so, you will not slim down, because although exercise burns fat, your body is too depleted to carry the fat away, and what stays behind turns into waste. Where does the excess fat settle? It settles into the blood vessels, wrapping around them and building up inside them, pressing on them, and in the end it makes the resistance to blood flow greater. And once the body has grown heavier, it needs more oxygen, and oxygen has to be carried to the whole body by the blood.

Picture the scene: on one side, demand is higher; on the other, resistance is higher. What can be done? The heart can only pump harder. The flow in the vessels increases, so doesn't blood pressure go up too? This is why so many people get high blood pressure while they are still young. And that is not the end of it. Obesity also causes insulin resistance, which disrupts the way blood sugar is used and metabolized. That means a mass of glucose drifts around your blood vessels like stragglers from a scattered army, with no unit to take them in, and your risk of diabetes rises.

Then comes year after year of 996, year after year of pressure, with no time for health management, and the crises inside the body come close to bursting the dam. At that moment, first comes high blood pressure. High blood pressure makes the blood flow batter the inner lining of the coronary artery walls, gouging pits into a lining that was once smooth. Then comes diabetes. Because the blood carries too much sugar, the cells lining the vessel walls are soaked in sugar water for years on end, so don't they become more and more brittle and soft? Go home, take a vegetable stalk, soak it in sugar water every day, and see what becomes of it.

The holes and pits I just described become softer and softer and bigger and bigger. And do you know what goes after those pits next? The low-density lipoprotein in your body. It loves nothing more than to burrow into these little holes. Who would not want to make a home in a honey jar? And once it settles in, it turns into a harmful substance called lipid peroxide.

As more and more lipid peroxide collects in your body, a little clump here and a little clump there, it forms small plaques on the vessel wall. As those plaques keep growing, the vessel wall becomes pitted and uneven, and then sugar, fat and even calcium in the blood keep piling up in the hollows, so the vessel grows narrower and narrower. And once it narrows, the blood flows faster and faster, doesn't it? Obviously, when the river channel narrows, the current speeds up.

Next, you keep staying up late and keep working overtime. The body is exhausted and the mind is tense. What about the anxiety? There is nothing to be done. The body can only keep running by pouring out large amounts of adrenaline.

Adrenaline is meant for emergencies. The body cannot rely on it for long, because it makes blood pressure spike and the heart race. In that sudden surge, the blood may tear through the inner lining of the vessel wall. The moment the lining breaks, clotting factors appear, and they make the blood clot. The bleeding is stopped by the clotting factors, but when the clotting factors pile up together with all that other debris, what do they become? A blood clot.

That clot grows larger and larger inside the coronary artery, and eventually it may block the channel through which blood flows. Blood can no longer reach the heart, the heart muscle loses its blood supply, and isn't that exactly when an acute heart attack happens? That is the moment that kills.

Based on the information that came out about this event, I have used it as an example to describe what most probably happened. Because Pinduoduo ended up having the story quietly suppressed, we cannot see more of the details for now. I use it only as an illustration. What I want to say is that your body is the last card in your hand. Never gamble it on tomorrow, because what you win may be that you never have a tomorrow again. Success built by overdrawing your life can never pay the bill for your health. Never count on luck. The health debts we run up will have to be repaid sooner or later, so health management for every person and every family cannot wait.
