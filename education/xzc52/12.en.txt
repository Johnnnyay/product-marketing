# The First Controllable Factor: Your State of Mind

Welcome to lesson twelve. Starting today, the next three or four lessons will all circle one theme: what are the controllable factors that affect our health, including the relationship between diet, nutrition, health and disease? Let us review yesterday. In health management we divide the factors that influence health into uncontrollable and controllable. In daily life we have to pay attention to our air, sunlight and water, gradually learn some basics of Western and Chinese medicine, pick up some psychology, and consider our heredity, our family, and our work. All of these bear closely on our health, and together they are called the uncontrollable factors.

Of course, these uncontrollable factors are not entirely beyond control. It is simply that they are not entirely up to us. Today I want to talk about the 60 percent that is controllable. It falls into four areas. What comes first? It may not be what many of you expect. The first controllable factor is an optimistic state of mind. Why put it first? Because in medicine most experts believe that [[an optimistic state of mind accounts for more than 50 percent of our health|The speaker's figure. A meta-analysis of 15 studies covering more than 229,000 people found optimism associated with a substantially lower risk of cardiovascular events and with lower all-cause mortality, comparable in size to other established cardiac risk factors. These are associations, not a percentage of health.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6777240/]]. Mental health matters enormously. It determines many other things, and a great many of our problems are in fact connected to our mental health. So how do we make sure we have a healthy state of mind?

First, a psychologist will tell us that our way of thinking has to become positive. In daily life, we should learn to use positive words when we speak about ourselves. People fall easily into negative vocabulary. For instance, we have been talking about health these past few days, and some people will say "health means not being sick". But not being sick and being healthy are two different things, are they not? In the subconscious mind, the word "not" does not exist.

You will hear friends say, as a matter of habit, "I am at home nursing my illness." We joke that other people keep a cat or a dog at home, so why are you keeping an illness? With nothing better to do, why keep an illness? These are not positive phrases.

[[We should learn to say that we are nurturing our body|In Chinese the common phrase is 养病, literally "to raise or nurture an illness", meaning to convalesce. The speaker asks people to say 养好自己的身体, "nurture the body back to health", instead.|]]. That is a positive way to put it. Do not underestimate this. Many examples from daily life show us that positive expression matters more to each person's health than we realize. Here is one. A woman had breast cancer and went to consult a doctor. In fact it was only a small tumor at an early stage.

After the consultation, what did she start doing the very next day? She started writing an anti-cancer diary online. People said to her, do you want to die faster? Go and look at the people online writing anti-cancer diaries and see how many of them are still alive. With nothing else to do all day, she reminded herself every day that she had cancer, wrote a diary about it every day, and told herself every day how she was battling death. Can the body get well like that? It is not easy. You could write about the good things in your life, or write nothing at all. The key is to design your life well and adjust it: your three meals a day, your diet, your exercise, design all of it. You have to forget that you are a cancer patient. You have to tell yourself that you are no different from a healthy person. Only then do you have a chance of overcoming cancer.

If you remind yourself every day that you have cancer, then truly wanting to be healthy is not easy. This is not about deceiving our own brains. It is because most people in the early stage of a tumor are in fact no different from a healthy person. All you need is to keep your mind steady and not carry more pressure than you used to. So we say we have to express ourselves positively in thought and in language. We should say that we want health, not that we do not want to get sick. If we want health, we must think well, speak well, and listen to good things, and not pay attention to negative things or use negative words, because negativity does affect you. Medicine does provide evidence for this: when a person begins to pay excessive attention to a particular organ, that organ usually does not do well.

Think about it. If you spend every day wondering whether your stomach is a bit off, whether there might be something wrong with your liver, do you think your organs can be well? At the very least, someone who thinks that way every day will not be in a good mental state. This is what we call the principle of attention. Do not keep fretting over one organ.

Here is an example. When we learned to ride a bicycle as children, we all had this experience. You can see the stone ahead and you do not want to hit it, but as long as your eyes stay fixed on the stone, you will certainly hit it. Because your attention is fixed there, you are bound to run into it. This is the adjustment of thinking. Do not think about what you do not want. Think about what you do want. So we must express ourselves positively, and our attention must rest on positive things. This is the first requirement of a healthy state of mind, and it is very important.

If your language, your content, or your thinking is negative, it is not easy to become truly healthy. Beyond that, we should do good things, do kind things, do things that let us feel we are giving. When you give, you feel a certain happiness, your body produces the hormones of joy, and your immune system does better. So do more of what? More good deeds, more kind deeds, more giving. Just as we are now spreading ideas about health and about health management more widely, that counts as doing good. Whether spiritually or materially, you will feel a certain satisfaction. And when you feel satisfied in yourself, that is very good for your health. That is the second point: do more good for the sake of health.

The third is that you have to have a goal, and you have to feel that you are useful. In fact our subconscious is in constant dialogue with our body. In a healthy person, the subconscious tells the body that I am useful. If you tell yourself you are no longer of use, your body will only get worse and worse. Many people talk about how good retirement is, but you find that most people's physical condition falls sharply after they retire, because while they were working they had something to do every day. Once retired, it is easy to feel that you are not of much use, and then there is no goal in life, and you have not set yourself a new purpose. That is one situation.

Another is that in recent years, in many big cities, many professional women have gone home to be full-time mothers and raise children. Many of them are not in good health. Why? She says, I seem to be very busy, I do have things to do. And we say, what you are busy with all day are odds and ends. Your own life has no goal. If a person does not set a goal, what is the ultimate goal a human being has? Death, right? That becomes the subconscious goal.

So once our life has no goal, the subconscious sets the only ultimate goal for you, which is death. The body then starts a mode we might call [[self-destruct mode|The transcript has 智慧模式, "wisdom mode", which is almost certainly a transcription of the homophone 自毁模式, "self-destruct mode". The speaker's description, of the body ceasing to maintain itself, fits the latter.|]]. In other words, if your subconscious feels you are of no use, it automatically starts this mode. And what is it? The body's secretion of growth hormone falls, and then immunity falls, because the body is of no use, and a body of no use does not need to be repaired, so aging speeds up enormously.

What does [[growth hormone|生长激素. In adults, growth hormone helps maintain body composition, bone remodeling and muscle, and its secretion declines gradually with age, a change clinicians call the somatopause, which is linked with loss of muscle mass, vitality and physical function.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12009952/]] do in an adult? It governs the body's capacity for self-repair. So the second consequence of falling growth hormone is falling resistance, with immunity dropping steeply.

So as long as our life has no goal, no matter how young you seem to be, the risk is there. Some say, my company has grown big, so I do not need to set goals anymore. I have money now and life is good, so I do not need goals. My children are grown and I do not have to worry about them, so I do not need goals. Once these thoughts appear, health problems come easily, because when the body starts that mode automatically, it does not notify you, and you do not know it yourself.

Sometimes we see older people move into a nursing home. Once there, what does your subconscious say to you? What am I doing here, am I not just waiting to die? And after a few days someone is carried out, and a few days later another one. What do you think in your heart? Will it be my turn one day?

So however young you are now, however comfortable your life is, life must have a goal. Take [[Li Ka-shing|李嘉诚, Hong Kong's best-known businessman, born in 1928. He stepped down as chairman of his group in 2018 and continued his work with Shantou University and his charitable foundation.|]]. He is nearly 95 this year and only announced his retirement the year before last, and even then he only stepped down from the chairmanship of his group. He himself has not gone idle. He still oversees Shantou University and his own charitable foundation. He is still working. At his age, does he lack money? No. Does he lack respect? He lacks neither respect nor status. So why keep working? Because life must have a goal, and you must feel a sense of value, right? However old you are, you need that sense of value. When we were starting our businesses, we used to think, business is so good, once I retire I will still have plenty of cash flow, I will stop working and still have permanent income. Now that we have that result, how do we look at it?

We say it is best never to retire, never to lose that sense of value. If at 90 or 100 people still want to hear us talk about health management, how wonderful that would be. Imagine it yourself. If you were 100 years old and could still talk to others about health, would people not like you? Would you not feel a strong sense of value? So make yourself feel useful. These are the three most important standards for a healthy state of mind.

In summary: first, your thinking and your words must be positive; second, do more good and kind things, more things that involve giving; third, let yourself feel that you are useful. These three attitudes are the first thing that determines our health. Among the controllable factors of health, the second is sleep. Does sleep matter?
