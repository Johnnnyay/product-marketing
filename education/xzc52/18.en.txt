# How Much to Take: Understanding Reference Intakes

Hello, everyone, and welcome. Let us continue learning together today. In the last two lessons we talked at length about the hidden hunger of the cells, and it all came down to one principle: a shortage of nutrients brings the cells an invisible, hidden hunger, and when that hidden hunger persists for a long time, chronic disease is not far away. That is why nutrients matter. And whenever we talk about nutrients, they have three great functions. One is to build and repair the body's tissues. One is to supply energy. And one is to regulate all the normal activities of life in the body. Those are the functions of nutrients.

The functions of nutrients are written in university textbooks. To learn about nutrients we need a theoretical source, and we must never exaggerate what nutritional health foods do. Where can you learn the functions of nutrients? Besides the books, the entries in Baidu Baike, China's online encyclopedia, are quite detailed too.

When we study nutrients, the first book we should talk about is Chinese Dietary Reference Intakes, especially the 2013 edition. Why read this book? Because much of the data in it was supplied by the [[Nutrilite Health Institute|纽崔莱营养与健康中心 in the speaker's words. The description of it and of its contribution to the Chinese reference intakes is the speaker's.|]], a highly authoritative institution in the United States. It supplied a great deal of nutritional data, which the Chinese Nutrition Society then compiled into the book.

In this book we also find a [[dietary pagoda|中国居民平衡膳食宝塔, the Chinese Food Guide Pagoda, the Chinese Nutrition Society's picture of a balanced daily diet, with grains at the wide base and oils and salt at the narrow top. The amounts here are the speaker's.|]]. The pagoda tells us, for example, to eat 300 to 500 grams of grains a day, 400 to 500 grams of vegetables, 100 to 200 grams of fruit, 125 to 200 grams of animal foods, 100 grams of milk and dairy products, 50 grams of beans and bean products, and less than 25 grams of oils and fats.

Now let us think about a question together. If we eat strictly according to these amounts, will our bodies stay free of problems? Not necessarily, right? Given the way we live now, and if all these vegetables are bought at the market, then with today's lifestyle and today's food safety, you could follow the pagoda strictly and still not avoid chronic disease. Because when was the pagoda drawn up? It was drawn up when we were very poor, when people could not get enough to eat at their three meals. In other words, it is designed to make sure you do not die of a major nutritional deficiency.

The dietary pagoda only tells you that if you eat these amounts, you will not develop a major deficiency disease. But can it prevent chronic disease? No. Can it prevent high blood pressure and diabetes? No, because it only ensures you will not die of malnutrition. That is all those amounts are for.

The modern reference intakes in this book tell you that beyond these amounts, we must upgrade the structure of our diet. So what concept has the state now put forward? It is called health food. What is health food first of all? First of all, it is food. It shares the raw materials of ordinary food, drawn from natural plants, but it goes through advanced production processes that bring the rich active components it contains to their fullest effect. It regulates the body's functions and is suitable for the groups of people who need it. Food of this kind is called health food, or health-care food.

In China today, health foods include types for general supplementation, antioxidant types, weight-loss types and supportive-therapy types. What are the things we usually call supplements, really? Remember, they are called health foods. And what do these foods contain? Nutrients. The nutrients we are talking about belong in the category of health food, so the root of health food is food. A supplement is always a health food, and food cannot take the place of medicine. It does not have the power to treat disease. Everyone can understand that.

So food cannot replace medicine. Food is food, not medicine. And the reverse is also true: no medicine can replace food. If your stomach is hungry, can you fill it up with pills? You cannot. And the symptoms that come from hungry cells, can you fix those with pills? Isn't the answer the same? So we say again and again that medicine cannot replace food, and food cannot replace medicine. These are two completely different concepts.

The supplements we are discussing belong to health foods. Do health foods have functions? They do. Each nutrient in them has all kinds of functions in the body. We must not exaggerate them, and there is no need to. If we learn the function of each nutrient, won't that be enough? And where are the functions of nutrients written? In books, and in Baidu Baike. We only need to learn what is there.

What should we learn first about nutritional health foods? Our course is different from others. Other courses start you with functions. Today I want you first to understand the concept of amount, before we reach functions. Why do you need to know about amount? Ordinarily, one bowl of rice fills you up, and if someone gives you a second bowl you cannot eat it. That one bowl is your amount. But when you take micronutrients, do you feel full? No. Do you know how many micronutrients you must take to be full? You do not. That is why we need to set a standard amount. There are four such amounts. One is the estimated average requirement, one is the recommended nutrient intake, one is the adequate intake, and the last is the tolerable upper intake level. Why do we need to discuss them?

Because if you have not taken enough of a nutrient, you do not know your cells are hungry, and you do not know when they are full either. So we have to set a standard, an amount we can see.

The first is the average requirement, called the [[EAR|The four standard reference values: the estimated average requirement (EAR) meets the needs of half of healthy people in a group; the recommended intake (the RNI in China, the RDA in the US) meets the needs of nearly all of them; the adequate intake (AI) is used where the evidence is too thin to set a recommended intake; and the tolerable upper intake level (UL) is the highest daily intake unlikely to cause harm. In the standard definitions, the AI is a population value, not an individual's personal need as the speaker uses it below.|]] in English. Do not let the English letters put you off. You can look them all up on Baidu. It is the average amount of a nutrient needed by people of a particular age, sex and physiological state. Reaching this level meets the needs of 50 percent of the individuals in that group. So at the EAR level, 50 percent are not short of the nutrient. And the other 50 percent? Aren't they severely deficient? That is what the EAR means.

We use this amount very little nowadays. It is generally used only in very poor countries. What do most of us use?

We use the recommended nutrient intake, the RNI. It is the amount that meets the needs of most individuals in a group of a given sex, age and physiological state. You can look all of this up on Baidu Baike. The RNI is the amount we use most today. There is another amount that has been getting more and more attention in recent years and that people are gradually coming to accept, called the adequate intake, or AI. It is the intake of a nutrient in a healthy population. In other words, the amount of a nutrient we need to stay healthy every day is called the AI.

Then there is the tolerable upper intake level, called the UL. It is the highest average daily amount of a nutrient you can take in. For the general population, taking in less than this amount over the long term causes no side effects at all. That is the UL. As long as you stay within this range, you can take it for a long time with no side effects. So how should we understand the recommended intake and the upper limit?

In general, the recommended intake, the RNI, and the upper limit, the UL, can both be found in books. But the adequate intake, the AI, is hard to look up, because the AI is your own actual individual need, and it differs from one person to the next. In children, needs are much the same. But after we grow up, habits and social pressures differ, so the amount each of us needs differs too. So once you are an adult, the AI actually becomes harder to pin down.

Let us take an example: vitamin C. What does China set as the recommended intake for vitamin C? [[100 milligrams a day for an adult|That is China's recommended intake. The US RDA is 90 mg a day for men and 75 mg for women, smokers are advised to take 35 mg more, and the upper limit is 2,000 mg a day, above which vitamin C can cause stomach upset and diarrhea.|https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/vitamin-c/]]. If an adult has the good habit of eating vegetables and fruit every day, that can basically be met. But the actual need, the AI, is a different story.

The AI is the amount a specific person actually needs, and it differs with each person's habits. For example, a smoker uses up about 50 milligrams of vitamin C with each cigarette. If he smokes ten cigarettes a day, how much does he use up? 500 milligrams of vitamin C. So I have to make sure he truly takes 500 milligrams a day, plus the basic 100 milligrams, which comes to at least how much? At least 600 milligrams.

Reaching 600 milligrams through fruit and vegetables is very hard. How many kilograms of fruit and vegetables would you have to eat? So simply following the recommended intake does not work. Each individual must know his own adequate intake. For this person, 100 milligrams is clearly not enough, and he must supplement to reach 600 milligrams of vitamin C. What can he do if food cannot supply it? He needs to supplement with health foods, good health foods that have been processed and concentrated. Can he get by without them? He can, but will his body develop problems? Over time it will, and he will show the symptoms of micronutrient deficiency.

Isn't chronic disease brought on by exactly these micronutrient shortages? So the AI, the adequate amount, is what keeps each individual healthy, but it is different for everyone.

What is the tolerable upper intake level? Take vitamin C again. Most experts and scholars believe that a daily intake below 2,000 milligrams is safe. That is the UL, the tolerable upper intake.

That means if you take 2,000 milligrams of vitamin C a day for a long time, even for life, there will be no side effects. What is that called? The UL. Now that these four amounts are clear, everyone should take the amount that fits his own needs. Do you understand this point? Nobody will tell you exactly how much you must take. Who decides? You decide. You know your own habits best and your own constitution best, and you decide how much to take based on your own situation.

Some friends say, I have started taking Nutrilite, but why haven't I seen any results? I would ask you, what result do you want? You first have to know what goal you want to reach, and that goal depends on how much you take, doesn't it? If we take only 100 milligrams of vitamin C a day, does it have an effect? It does. What effect?

If you take at most 100 milligrams of vitamin C a day, you will not develop severe scurvy, with bleeding gums, bleeding under the skin, and finally severe bleeding of the internal organs and death. So with 100 milligrams of vitamin C a day, you will not die from lack of vitamin C. Some people say, is that really a thing? Can a lack of vitamin C really kill? It can. In the early days of ocean voyages, sailors went to sea for three months or half a year at a time, and without vitamin C they developed this severe scurvy, bled internally, and died. So 100 milligrams of vitamin C a day ensures you will not die from a lack of it. That is what I call the survival dose. So does 100 milligrams of vitamin C have an effect?

Of course it does. But is it the effect you want? Can it guarantee your health? Can it reach the amount your health needs? Clearly not, because the amount each of us actually needs every day is much larger than that. The healthy amount differs for each person. Suppose you take 600 milligrams of vitamin C a day. Will you see an effect? You will not see one either, because 600 milligrams is the minimum a person needs each day. It is all used up. You have nothing left over in savings, so you cannot see your body repairing anything.

When can you see it? When people with the same habits take it steadily for five, ten or twenty years, those who take it and those who do not end up in different places.

So you can see that because habits differ, each person's needs differ. People who stay up late and people who do not, people who smoke and people who do not, need completely different amounts of vitamin C, and taking it or not makes a different difference for each. And there is another way to see the effect: stop taking it, and you will see the effect right away.

Take smokers. If a smoker goes a week without carotenoids, he immediately has a lot of phlegm, because if carotenoids cannot keep up, nothing protects the mucous membranes, there is a great deal of keratinized tissue, and naturally there is a lot of phlegm. So when friends say they cannot see any results, if you do not know about these signs, how can you say you see no results? Each person's needs are different. You have to make sure you know your healthy amount and take enough to repair your body, right? Someone like me, who lectures heavily every day, finds 600 milligrams of vitamin C is not enough. I need at least 2,000 milligrams, or it does little in the way of repair. So nutrition science tells us that to repair the body, you must take an amount above the AI and not above the UL.

Objectively speaking, going over the UL for a long time may do your body no good. But if you do not reach the AI, you will not get the repair you are after. If your body already shows obvious problems, if your deficiency symptoms are already quite serious, you can take up to the UL, and once the symptoms disappear or ease, go back down to the AI. For example, in winter people in northern China are badly dehydrated, and many children have cracked hands, feet and corners of the mouth. That shows a serious shortage of the B vitamins, and the amount must be larger. Even then it is not really large. It just sits between the AI and the UL, so there is no problem.

Then, starting in 2013, the Chinese Nutrition Society added three more amounts. There used to be four, which we have just covered, and now there are three more. The first is an acceptable range for the macronutrients, with a minimum and a maximum. The minimum is a firm standard. Take energy, for example. The minimum energy our bodies need each day from carbohydrate is 800 kilocalories. If you take in less than 800 kilocalories for more than 48 hours, your brain has no nourishment.

That is because [[the brain uses only carbohydrate for its energy|This is the speaker's view. Research shows that after roughly 10 to 14 hours without food the liver's sugar stores run low and the body starts making ketone bodies from fat, and in prolonged fasting ketones can supply about half of the brain's fuel, with glucose supplying the rest. The brain still needs some glucose, which the body can also make from protein.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8954770/]]. When carbohydrate runs short at that point, brain cells begin to die. You will see some people drink only vegetable juice and eat no carbohydrate, and after 48 hours their brain cells begin to die.

Some people now practice bigu, the traditional grain fast, and some blindly chase weight loss by cutting out carbohydrate, going ten days or two weeks without any carbohydrate at all. But you should know that after 48 hours brain cells begin to die, and the death of brain cells cannot be reversed. So everyone must understand that there is an acceptable range for the macronutrients. One end is the minimum. At the other end, athletes need very large amounts, so there is also an acceptable maximum. The standard gives this range.

The next one is very important. It sets a proposed intake for preventing chronic non-communicable diseases, which is of real practical value. It is called the PI. In other words, if I want to prevent chronic disease, how much of a nutrient should I take in? In China it is called the PI. Take vitamin B1. Our average recommended amount is 1.2 milligrams, and the suggested tolerable upper limit is 50 milligrams, a fifty-fold difference. And the proposed amount of vitamin B for preventing chronic disease? [[35 milligrams, as a reference|The PI figure of 35 mg and the upper limit of 50 mg are the speaker's. The US RDA for thiamin is 1.2 mg a day for men and 1.1 mg for women, and the US sets no upper limit for thiamin, because no harm from high intakes has been reported.|https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/vitamin-b1/]].

In other words, if we want to hold off the drift toward chronic disease, we need to take in 35 milligrams of the B vitamins every day. How much vitamin B1 does one Nutrilite tablet contain? 1.2 milligrams per tablet. So each person must work out, according to his own needs, how much to take. And there is one last amount, the seventh, called the specific proposed level. Who is it for? It is for our third broad class of nutrients, called phytochemicals, such as proanthocyanidins, and the tea polyphenols in the green tea we drink. We used not to count these as nutrients, but now they are all counted as nutrients, and for each of them a minimum and a maximum daily intake has to be set. So a specific proposed level was established.

Take [[proanthocyanidins extracted from grape seed|Proanthocyanidins, also called OPCs, are the polyphenols that give grape seeds, cranberries and cocoa their astringent taste. The upper limits quoted here are the speaker's figures; the US has not set a Dietary Reference Intake for them.|]]. The UL, the tolerable upper intake, for proanthocyanidins is 2,000 milligrams a day in the United States, and 800 milligrams in China. Among our nutrients, each [[Yangcang Shanheng|养藏善衡片, a Nutrilite tablet sold in China that contains grape seed proanthocyanidins.|]] tablet contains 40 to 50 milligrams of proanthocyanidins. From that we can calculate the most tablets we can take a day. Twenty, right? So anything under twenty a day is safe, and you can take up to that amount long term with no problem, but do not exceed it. How much to take depends on your own needs. Through reading and study, you adjust it yourself according to your habits and your actual situation.

For example, if your work has been stressful and tiring lately and you keep staying up late, your liver is surely not doing well. Or if some inflammation has been bad lately, you should take more, and the amount should be larger. If your body has been doing well lately and you feel in good shape, you can cut back.

So nobody can tell you exactly how much is correct for you. You would never ask me whether you should eat one bowl of rice or three bowls at dinner. You would not ask that, because nobody else can tell you. Yet many people keep wanting to ask, or want to argue: why do you tell me to take six tablets and not two?

How can anyone else know how many you should take? You have to look at what you need, don't you? If you already have a deficiency and it is showing badly, that means your daily consumption is very high and you are certainly not getting enough, so you should take up to the tolerable upper limit. Until when? Until the deficiency symptoms disappear, and then you bring it back down. So everyone's amount is different. With the B vitamins we just discussed, some people take three, four or five tablets a day, and some take twenty. Why?

Because that person can feel that he has a fairly serious B vitamin deficiency. He worked it out against the UL and found that twenty tablets was safe for him, so he kept taking twenty until his symptoms finally cleared, and then he brought the amount back down.

So it is not a matter of someone else telling you how much to take. It is a matter of you understanding how much you need, learning through study what amount is right for you, and all of this can be gained by continuing to learn in this environment of ours.
