# Immunity First: Why Health Management Starts With Mindset

Welcome to the 2021 online course in family health management. A cold wave has just swept into Beijing, and with the epidemic tightening again, primary and middle schools here are getting ready to start their winter break early. For every industry in China, the epidemic has become a shadow that will not lift. Some companies may not survive this winter, and some people may find their finances staying uncomfortably close to the edge. Through all of it, the one thing none of us can afford to let fall is our health.

I think every one of us was schooled by the epidemic in 2020. It was not a lesson we chose. It pushed each person's health and immune defenses right into the eye of the storm, and from the level of the nation down to individual choices about careers and starting a business, that unsettled year brought about a great many changes.

The epidemic made each of us feel, very clearly, a respect for nature, and it made us start to reflect on how we understand health. Ignorance about immunity can cost us our lives.

When I was preparing to record the 2021 edition of this course, I actually meant to cut some of the epidemic background material. I did not want this year's course to carry too much of the epidemic's imprint. But with new outbreaks appearing in several cities, and with the country racing to develop vaccines, we have no choice but to begin our discussion of health management with the epidemic.

A recent set of numbers caught my attention. On December 28, People's Daily and Toutiao both ran a news item: a survey by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention found that [[4.43 percent of community residents in Wuhan|The China CDC serosurvey tested 34,857 people in Wuhan, the rest of Hubei and six other provinces in April 2020. Wuhan's rate of neutralizing antibodies was 4.43 percent, and 76 percent of those who tested positive reported no symptoms.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7864613/]] tested positive for COVID-19 antibodies. If you study that number carefully, there is a lot inside it. First, far fewer people than that ever showed symptoms of COVID. So a good share of this group must have come into contact with the virus and then, before anyone could even check whether they had symptoms, recovered on their own in a very short time. How did they recover on their own?

Let me tell you a second thing. As you all know, the United States and the United Kingdom are the two countries with the highest COVID infection rates in the world. The infections are widespread, but the death rate they have produced is not high. If you follow the news, or if you have lived abroad, you will know that their medical conditions are better than ours overall, although their efficiency is not higher than ours. I have heard of two cases among my own friends who tested positive in the United States. Apart from medication, they simply rested and looked after themselves at home, and after a while they recovered on their own. So where did that recovery come from? If anyone doubts this, we can pull up the news and look again.

Quite a few well-known politicians also recovered through medical care plus good self-care. So did many football stars in Britain, Spain and Italy, including China's own top striker, [[Wu Lei|武磊, the Chinese national team forward who was playing for Espanyol in Spain in 2020. Chinese fans jokingly call him "the hope of the whole village", meaning the one player carrying the country's football hopes. He tested positive in March 2020 and recovered.|]], who plays in Spain and whom fans call the hope of the whole village. He caught COVID, and in less than a month, through medical care and self-care, he recovered. We all know that a specific drug for COVID is still being developed. So how did all these people, in China and abroad, recover on their own? The answer is their own immunity. Not only did they recover, they also produced antibodies.

Of course, the COVID situation is far more complicated than the way I am describing it. But from this set of data alone, you can see what it is that saves you at the critical moment. So can you accept the view that immunity comes first and medicine comes second? If you can, it means you have gained one of the most important ideas in the whole discipline of health management: the idea of strengthening immunity.

At about this time last year, in the first lesson of this course, I said that we should first spend more time on mindset. Which matters more, mindset or knowledge? And which would you rather listen to? We all know that mindset is very important, but it can sometimes feel dry, because you keep repeating the same point. Knowledge feels more interesting, so many people would rather jump straight into learning facts.

But I have to tell you, if we do not start out with a certain health mindset and simply learn health management out of curiosity, the knowledge will soon start to feel dull. A voice in your head will say it would be easier to wait until you are sick and ask a doctor, and you will lose the interest to keep going. As long as health has not turned into your own actual behavior, it is not doing anything for you.

Friends often tell me that after last year's course they badly wanted their family and close friends to learn more about health, but somehow those people would not take any of it in. They ask me how they can get the people around them to understand health. It is a frustrating problem, so where does it come from? Remember this: if the health mindset has not taken root first, you will focus on the health knowledge itself and conclude that knowledge is what matters most. This is an extremely common pattern.

So today I want to remind every friend here in particular: what matters most is always the health mindset. Without it, health knowledge on its own is useless. Knowledge can be stored in your phone or kept in your head, but you do not learn a whole pile of health management knowledge in order to open a museum of health facts. We learn it so that health shows up in our behavior and gets carried out in our daily lives.

Knowledge has to act through your mindset, so without the mindset the knowledge does very little. For example, if you do not have the idea of supplementing nutrients, or the health management habit of eating a nutritious breakfast, no amount of knowledge can rescue your health. So the mindset plays the decisive role. When we pass the health mindset on to others, telling them to pay attention to their health, that taking a certain nutrient can help them avoid a certain illness or support their body with a certain problem, that is fine as far as it goes.

But then again, I have put this question to quite a few people. Think about it: among those of us who know something about health, deep down, isn't our understanding of nutrients really about treating illness? When you see a nutrient, what is the first thought in your head? What can it cure? What is it good for?

And then when you see someone else with a health problem, you ask whether you could give them a prescription. You may not use the word "prescription", but the meaning is about the same: can you give me some advice about what I should take? Then once you tell them, say, protein, vitamin B, vitamin C and calcium, and you have named all four, they ask you how many of each to take, one by one. What does that mean? It means: give me whatever works best and is most convenient. Think about it, isn't that the mentality most of us have? So what do we mean by the [[big-health view|大健康, "big health", is the Chinese policy term for a broad, whole-of-life approach to health that shifts the focus from treating disease in hospitals to prevention, lifestyle and personal health management.|]]? Big health does not mean waiting until you are sick, going to the hospital, and trusting no one but doctors and drugs. Nor does it mean accepting nutrients but still taking them as if they were medicine.

So what is big health? It is a person's own ability to manage their health. It is not centered on the hospital. It is centered on your own capacity to manage your health. In other words, it is not about dealing with the hospital every day and treating everything as a drug. China's national big-health policy states it very clearly: health management is about moving further and further away from disease and medication, and closer and closer to your daily routine and your living habits.

If you either reject functional foods altogether, or use nutrients only to treat illness, you move further and further from your daily routine and your physical health, and further from the country's big-health strategy as well. And once big health truly becomes a national strategy, it is bound to become the strategy of many companies too.

Keep watching this, everyone. This is a national strategy from the top down. The Healthy China strategy is certainly meant to bring nutrition and nutrients into every household. Nutrients are not a supplement to China's drug treatment system, an add-on to oral medicines or prescription drugs. So what are they? They are an ability that belongs to each independent person and each family member. Do you want to have that ability?

The United Nations says that human lifespans will one day cross a century, a hundred years. To be honest, whether or not we ourselves live to a hundred may not matter that much. But health management has a great deal to do with whether you and your whole family live well for the rest of your lives, and whether your quality of life while you are alive stays high.

Tomorrow I will talk about how to build a health mindset in yourself and in others. The fact that we decided to join this course today says that the people in this group value their health. And not only do we value it, we are also willing to act on it, and we are willing to take on some responsibility for our own health. So please remember that the mindset has to be built first. Before we begin, I hope every one of us will quietly give ourselves some encouragement: within our whole family, we are willing to take on this responsibility, and that is why we are here today.

We are willing to give our time, which shows that we are willing to take responsibility for our health and to take real action for it. People like us are going to be more fortunate for it.

Because once you understand health management, then in the future, in your household and in your wider family, you will have some basic knowledge and some important health concepts. When a problem comes up you will not panic, you will not get flustered, and you will not grab at any doctor or any cure in desperation. When something happens, we will know how to deal with it, how to solve it, how to find the right doctor, how to use the right nutrients, or how to begin changing some of our own habits and use nutrition to support the body. With a real health mindset plus some professional knowledge, I believe our lives will be calmer, our households and families will be more harmonious, and at the very least, health will not become a hole or a burden in the family finances.

So here I want to mention the 2019 to 2020 National Health Insight Report. It surveyed Chinese people on how they define health, and it produced a ranking. In first place was mental health. Second was sleeping well. Third was good digestion. Only fourth was not getting sick. Fifth was good skin, sixth was a good figure, seventh was good hair, eighth was not getting injured, and ninth covered other health wishes.

Today let us take the first two items and talk about what health is. Do you understand it? How much does health really matter to you? First, the item chosen most often was mental health. You can see how much pressure society puts on people today. Most of us would count as being under pressure. Not everyone has chosen a secure career where their income keeps coming even if the work stops, and not everyone has income from assets. So most people really are under pressure.

Recently a fairly senior executive about my age told me that five years ago he did not yet understand what a midlife crisis really is, and he had not had the chance to choose a secure career and experience how differently it can shape a life.

But as you get closer and closer to retirement, and you keep looking at how much retired people get paid, you feel the pressure and you feel anxious. He said that before thirty-five, when you can still look around the job market and try this company or that one, you may not feel any pressure. But when no company will hire someone approaching fifty anymore, you truly know what pressure is. That may sound a little harsh to say here, but that is exactly the sense of crisis people feel.

So look at life today: financial pressure, age pressure, the pressure of education, the pressure of medical costs. These are all very real, right in front of us. And in second place was sleeping well. In a survey of Chinese people's health needs, sleeping well actually ranked second.

I do not know whether any of you are carrying a sleep debt, but I now feel deeply how important sleep is. Recently, to prepare these few dozen online lessons and to study some things about running a community, for a short stretch of a few days I went to bed rather late but still got up early. I would often wake at a little after four in the morning. Why wake at four? It has to do with the lungs, what Chinese medicine calls [[insufficient lung yin|肺阴不足. In the traditional Chinese "meridian clock", 3 to 5 a.m. is the hour of the lung meridian, and waking regularly at that time is read as a sign of weakened lung yin, the cooling, nourishing aspect of the lungs. This is a traditional concept, not a diagnosis in Western medicine.|]]. There is a saying that [[after forty, a person's yin is halved|From the Huangdi Neijing, the classic of Chinese medicine: "At forty, the yin qi is naturally halved, and daily vigor declines."|https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/The%20Great%20Treatise%20on%20Yin-Yang%20and%20the%20Corresponding%20Manifestations/1428403]], meaning that once you pass forty, your yin naturally drops by half. Especially if you go to bed late, your yin simply cannot come up, and when it cannot come up it cannot hold down the fire. So either you wake early or you cannot fall asleep and just toss around. And this particular pattern, not enough yin and waking before dawn, does even more harm than ordinary insomnia.

People like this can still sleep, of course, but they turn over and over in bed like a pancake on a griddle. They lie one way for a while, then have to switch to another position. Either the nose is bothering them and will not clear, or a joint or a leg is uncomfortable somewhere, and they keep tossing like this until close to five, when they may drift off again. But once they fall asleep, they hardly need an alarm clock, because they are up again by a little after six.

I used to think, how could I ever get up at six? Six in the morning had nothing to do with me. So now the question is: if you sleep badly at night and still wake up early, what do you do? How do you solve it? You can go to the hospital and get sleeping pills. If you look them up, the sleeping pills commonly used in clinics today are mainly for insomnia from all kinds of causes, and they are also used against anxiety.

The main pharmacological action of sleeping pills is to induce sleep, with a milder sedative effect. They work by suppressing various parts of the central nervous system to treat insomnia. They can also help regulate mood disturbances, including the symptoms of autonomic nervous system imbalance. [[Most sleeping pills cause relatively little dependence and are quite safe|This is the speaker's view. Clinical guidance is more cautious: benzodiazepines and the "Z-drugs" are generally recommended for no more than about four weeks because of tolerance, dependence and withdrawal risk, and behavioral treatment is the preferred first-line approach for chronic insomnia.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10433200/]]. The drugs we have now are safe and good, and if you have reached a genuinely medical condition, you should take them when you need to. The problem is that many people have not reached that point at all. They are still a long way from it.

Take my own case just now: getting older, the yin declining, and sleep getting worse as a result. Do you really think that has to be treated immediately, without a moment's delay, with sleeping pills? That runs against the national big-health policy. The policy says you should move further from medication and closer to daily life. That is what health is. So what do you do? Later in the course we will talk about functional foods, that is, nutrients. For example, there is a functional food on the market called [[Beiyishu tablets|倍益舒片, a sleep-support product sold in China. Its key ingredient is suanzaoren, the seed of the sour jujube (Ziziphus jujuba var. spinosa), used in Chinese medicine for sleep. A 2018 review of 13 randomized trials found suanzaoren formulas improved sleep over placebo with few side effects, though trial quality was mixed and follow-up was short.|https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2018.00076/full]], and inside it is sour jujube seed, which nourishes yin. People who know how to use it find it works well. They may not notice anything in particular, but their sleep quality goes up.

But I have also heard someone say that after taking it, she felt she could not wake up. She said it worked even better than sleeping pills, that it was practically a miracle drug, and that once her sleep improved she did not dare take much of it anymore. Why? Because she had a lot of housework to do and was afraid it would stop her getting things done. You see, this person was still taking it as a drug. Take it when there are symptoms, stop when there are none. Other people say, why not just buy sour jujube seed and eat it every day? That is another example of a mistaken understanding. Is sour jujube seed alone enough? Clearly not.

So you see, everyone, these are very widespread misunderstandings about the health management mindset. How to build a health mindset in yourself and influence your family's, we will continue tomorrow. Thank you, everyone.
