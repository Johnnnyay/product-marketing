# Building a Health Mindset in Yourself and Your Family

Hello, everyone, let us carry on with our study today. Yesterday we talked about how to build up our own understanding and how to influence others to learn about health. We said that first you have to pass on a health mindset, and that anyone who truly wants to benefit from health management must have that mindset too. If nutrition and nutrients are sitting right in front of us, and we still take them only when something is wrong, skip them when nothing is wrong, and forget them when we do not think of it, then our mindset is still about treating disease. We have not yet walked through the door of [[big health|大健康, "big health", the Chinese policy term for a whole-of-life approach that shifts the focus from treating disease in hospitals to prevention, lifestyle and personal health management.|]], and nutrition and nutrients have not yet entered our lives.

As for how to build a health mindset in other people, what I want to say is this. A health mindset is a person's ability to look ahead and predict the future. When you are twenty and you look ahead to thirty, your health may not change much in those ten years, so even if your ability to look ahead is weak, it does not seem to matter. But when you are thirty and looking ahead to forty, if your foresight cannot keep up, you need to know that these ten years may be the decade of greatest change in a person's life. If your foresight about your own body and your own health falls short, something serious can happen.

Here is an example everyone will understand. With a new car, skipping a bit of maintenance in the first three years does not really matter. But after ten years of driving, if you dare to skip its maintenance, that car is certain to break down. Everyone understands that. So for the many people who still do not take health management seriously, I suggest one thing in particular that they should go home and do. It takes two words: stay in bed. Yes, you heard right, stay in bed. But there is a requirement, and it is very simple. You go home by yourself, or if you want to supervise someone else, you go to their home or have them come to yours, it is the same either way. You have them lie down, but the lying down comes with a rule.

The rule is this: nobody helps you turn over, and you are not allowed to turn over by yourself. Then you take your phone and time yourself, and you see how long you can lie there. Lying flat or lying on your side, either is fine, but with nobody to help you, you may not turn over. Start the timer and see how long you can last. Some people think they could lie there for eight hours. Go ahead, try it, see whether you can manage eight hours. Forget eight hours. You will not even last one hour, if nobody turns you and you are not allowed to turn yourself.

Perhaps at that moment you will feel deep down how important health is, because most illnesses end the same way, with a person lying in bed who cannot turn over unless someone turns them.

And while you lie there, do not waste that hour just lying still. Think about something and look ahead. Here is the question to ask yourself: who is the person you could wake up in the middle of the night, without a second thought, without worrying about being a burden, without feeling you owe them an apology, and freely call on to help you turn over? Think about who that is.

Some people say, a paid caregiver. Let me tell you, in the end a caregiver would practically pay you to let them go. Some of you may think of your parents. Do not forget, if you are the one lying there unable to move, your parents are at least twenty years older than you. Could they still look after you?

Some people say, my children. Think about it yourself. Are your children the people you can wake in the dead of night without a second thought, without considering their feelings or their work the next day, without worrying about troubling them, just to turn you over? There is an old saying that [[at the bedside of a long illness there are no devoted children|久病床前无孝子, a Chinese proverb meaning that even the most loving children wear out when a parent's illness drags on for years.|]]. Is it your children? Not necessarily. Some people say, I would call my spouse. Leaving aside whether your spouse could even do it, could you bear to? Either way, I am telling you, whoever it is, a person has to come to mind.

Whoever it is that came to mind, the one you could wake in the middle of the night without hesitation or worry to help you turn over, remember this: from today on, you must love that person well. Agreed? You must not let that person end up in the hospital. Agreed? Let me say one more thing and see whether you agree. You yourself can skip health management. Whether you take nutrients or eat a nutritious breakfast does not matter, but the money you save must be spent on that person. You cannot let them go before you, can you? Whatever it takes, you have to help them live to a hundred. Hearing this, some friends may laugh. We are laughing, but what we are discussing is a very serious and very real problem. If you really were lying in bed with nobody to help you, unable to turn over, and you were desperate to move, think this question through at that moment and you will truly be moved to tears.

You may never have thought of loving your family from this angle, but everyone understands it right away. We say to love others as you love yourself, and it works the other way too: love yourself the way you would love others. The best way to love your family is to take good care of yourself and not become a burden to them. It is not a question of money.

You have to work a real health management mindset into your own life and your family's lives. Sometimes it hits me that I am nearly fifty. When I fill out a form and write my age, I think, is this really me? Is this my age? There is that feeling of, how can this be my age? And at a moment like that, don't you feel a kind of panic about your health? Without health management and without these health concepts, can you really feel at peace inside? I certainly could not.

Look around you. Many of the people we see every day have developed one health problem or another, and some have already left us. Perhaps things have changed for relatives and friends in your own family as well. Add to that everything we see online every day. Aren't we moved at all? Is health management just a matter of taking a few pills or a few nutrients when I have a headache or a fever today? Absolutely not. Let me tell you, if you simply take [[a B-complex vitamin properly every day|The speaker's claim. The evidence is narrower: a Cochrane review of 15 trials with 71,422 people found that lowering homocysteine with vitamins B6, B9 and B12 did not prevent heart attacks or reduce deaths, with a small reduction in stroke (4.3 percent versus 5.1 percent).|https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD006612_homocysteine-lowering-interventions-b-complex-vitamin-therapy-preventing-cardiovascular-events]], people who do that have lower rates of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease and of stroke than other people. We have not even mentioned vitamin C or any other nutrient yet. Just this one B-complex, as long as you keep taking it every day. The key is whether you really do keep taking it every day, and what is in your head when you take it.

Are you thinking, once I actually have high blood pressure or high blood lipids, once a checkup finds atherosclerosis, then I will take vitamin B and see whether the B vitamins can cure me? Is that the purpose you are taking them for? Or is it that today, while I am still fairly healthy, I want to manage myself well and prevent trouble before it starts, so I keep taking my B vitamins every day? Do not wait until the tank is empty to think about filling it with good fuel.

When some people see us bring out a nutritious breakfast, they say, does that stuff even work? What disease does it cure, is it a miracle drug or an elixir? I tell them that I have always wanted to keep myself in this good condition, my overall physical condition, and to keep my collagen up, because [[after forty, a person loses ten percent of their muscle every year|The speaker's figure. Published estimates are lower: muscle mass declines by roughly 3 to 8 percent per decade after age 30, and the loss speeds up after 60. Harvard Health gives 3 to 5 percent per decade and recommends resistance training and adequate protein.|https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/preserve-your-muscle-mass]]. What causes that? Is it a lack of exercise? No. Exercise is one thing, but if your body does not have amino acids converted from high-quality protein, the more you exercise, the more you are tearing your own body down.

So, everyone, we must have a health management mindset ourselves and bring our whole family along with us. And there is something in particular I want to share here: we should not only have the health mindset of a consumer, we should have the health mindset of an operator, of someone running something. Do not misunderstand me. It does not have to mean running a business. It means treating your health the way you would run an enterprise. Spreading health is a very good way of managing your own health, and that is especially true within the family. If you do not spread it, then in the future you will not be able to bear the health consequences of the people in your family.

What you are spreading is not getting others to take a few products. It is helping others to have a health mindset. If you can spread a health mindset within your family, you will find you have more and more in common with more and more people, and that still is not knowledge. Knowledge without a mindset has no power. So what is a mindset? What to eat, why to eat it, and how to eat it. That is all there is to it, nothing profound. But if all you can do is recite knowledge, people may stand there with their hands in their pockets watching you and say, you always have an answer for everything, but how would I know whether any of it works? So how do you break through that? There is only one way.

First, you do it yourself. We have seen plenty of so-called messengers who cannot do it themselves but badly want others to. They always run into the same problem. You talk for ages and the other person replies with one sentence: do not bother me, I am not taking it, if you want to take it, you take it. You might say, you need to take it, you need to manage your health, otherwise if something happens in the future, who will look after you? And the other person may push back: you do not need to worry about me, when the time comes I will pull the plug myself, you will not have to lift a finger.

And then you are left speechless. He is going to pull his own plug, so what more can you say? I do not know how many of you have been shut down like that. So what do you say? My special suggestion is this: tell him to get a piece of paper right now. What for? To write a guarantee. Guarantee what? Guarantee that he will get the kind of illness where his hands and feet still work. You see what I mean? Write a guarantee, because at the very least his hands and feet have to work for him to pull the plug. Some people say, fine, I will not pull the plug, I will jump off a building. If your hands and feet do not work, where are you going to jump from?

You cannot even climb over the balcony railing, so how are you going to jump? So after all this, we come back to the same point: we must have a health mindset. Building a health mindset is a process of widening the common ground in how you and all the family and friends around you think. We must have the mindset of health management. Once you have built it for yourself and for others, all the knowledge that follows can be taught inside that shared ground.

If you and the other person do not share a health mindset, then all the knowledge you study and hear afterwards will go in one ear and out the other. That is as far as we will go today on building a health mindset. After yesterday's lesson, many friends told me they were interested in sleep debt, so tomorrow I will tell you about a tragedy that sleep debt caused. See you tomorrow.
