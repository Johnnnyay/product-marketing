# The Four Steps of Health Management

Welcome to lesson nine. Electric vehicles have been all over the news lately. We know the number one electric car maker in the world is America's Tesla, and there is a homegrown Chinese brand following close behind it: [[NIO|蔚来, "Weilai", a Chinese electric vehicle company founded in 2014 by Li Bin. The transcript renders the name with the homophone 未来, "future".|]]. Even Tesla's boss Elon Musk has said that Tesla's only real rival will come from China. He did not name anyone, but anyone paying attention can see that NIO is the rival he means.

Recently a reporter asked NIO's founder, Li Bin, how his company's philosophy differs from Tesla's. Li Bin said that Tesla is mainly trying to win over people who have not yet decided to buy an electric car, while NIO wants to serve well the people who have already bought one. That remark gave me a lot to think about.

So who are today's four steps of health management for? They are for people, ourselves included, who have already accepted health management as a way of living. Today, then, let us talk about the four steps. Health management sets out to achieve several things. The first is to learn a method for managing your own health and for everyday nutritional supplementation. The second is to change unreasonable eating habits and an unhealthy lifestyle. The third is to reduce: reduce the amount of medication you take, and reduce your hospital and medical bills. The fourth is to lower: lower blood lipids, blood sugar, blood pressure, and body fat, and lower your risk of chronic disease.

So what does health management set out to do? Let me say it once more: one learn, two change, three reduce, four lower. Yesterday we said you have to study health management yourself, which means you have to take responsibility for yourself. Once you have learned, do you need to change some habits? Of course you do. If you do not change, who can do anything for you? Only when you truly change can you reach the goals of reducing and lowering. If we cannot change, no one can be responsible for our health. You may have noticed that in recent years there were many swindlers cheating older people out of their money. Because those people did not understand, they were easily taken in. And it was not only a lack of knowledge; the problem was in their way of thinking and their understanding.

Suppose someone tells you, my health product is wonderful, it can cure diabetes at the root, and it can cure your high blood pressure too. When you hear that, if you have no theoretical foundation, you will be fooled.

There is a saying among people in this field that I think is excellent. If someone truly invented a drug that could completely cure diabetes, that person would certainly win a Nobel Prize. Turn it around: if the drug has not won a Nobel Prize, it cannot possibly cure your diabetes.

Put that way, you can see that we might as well wait until some drug wins the Nobel Prize and only then believe it can cure our diabetes. It will not be too late, will it?

That includes a brand we all know well, the world's top brand of nutritional supplements, [[Nutrilite|纽崔莱. Nutrilite is Amway's brand of vitamins and dietary supplements, which the speaker treats throughout the course as the reference standard for supplements.|]]. Someone may ask why I mention this brand. It is because behind it stand the standards for nutritional supplements. We are familiar with it because it embodies many standards for nutrients, much as we are familiar with the iPhone because behind it sit many industry standards. Even so, if someone tells you that a certain Nutrilite product can completely cure your diabetes, that statement is definitely wrong and definitely unprofessional. Why?

Because it has to be paired with a change in your lifestyle. Suppose that one day a drug really is invented that cures your cancer. If you do not take responsibility for yourself and do not change, will the cancer come back? It will certainly come back.

For every chronic non-communicable disease, there is no drug, no surgery, and no health product that can completely cure it. Who caused these problems in the first place? Your own eating habits caused them. You did not have good habits to begin with. So even if you correct every outside factor and every symptom, as long as your habits do not change, will the problem come back? It will come back just the same.

So there is no health product and no drug that can completely cure our chronic non-communicable diseases, unless we do what? One learn, two change, three reduce, four lower. That is the most important idea behind the health management we practice.

But some people do not see it this way. They come to you and say, just tell me what to take, why do I have to study? Our answer is that no one can be at your side every day. You need to know which of your own habits have led to the state your body is in now, and how to rebalance it yourself. Only if you study can anyone else help you, right? No one is your bodyguard. Every person has to learn a method of self-care and health management. If you do not change, nobody else can do anything.

So those are the stage goals of health management: one learn, two change, three reduce, four lower. Next, who does health management serve? The first group is healthy people. These are people who already love being healthy and have realized how important health is, but because their health knowledge is limited, they need scientific, professional, systematic, and personalized health education and guidance, along with regular consultation and assessment, to keep their health at a low level of risk. Health management is not something you start after you fall ill. When is the best time to manage your health? While you are healthy.

The second group is people in [[sub-health|亚健康, "suboptimal health status", a term widely used in China for a state between health and disease: vague complaints, fatigue and lack of vitality without a diagnosable illness, seen as a reversible stage that can precede chronic disease.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3479966/]]. Their limbs feel weak, they are worn out in body and mind, and they do not sleep well. Their line of work, the competition in society, and the burdens of family all put them under a lot of pressure. They know themselves that sub-health is not good, but they do not know how to improve it. They need to build a true understanding of health in order to raise their overall level of health. That is the sub-health group.

The third group is people who are ill. While receiving treatment, they actively want to take part in improving their own health. Alongside clinical treatment, they need all-round changes to lifestyle and diet to control their own risk factors, lower their level of health risk, slow the course of the disease, and improve their quality of life. In other words, when we are ill and go to the hospital, we must also shoulder the responsibility that belongs to us.

So when a person is ill, what do they need? They need health management. And who manages your health? You manage it yourself. Some people say, my health is fine right now, so why should I take on this responsibility?

In reality, the point is not how healthy you are right now. The point is what happens once something goes wrong. In a society this large, one person's health may not seem like much. But for a family, and for a wider family, one person's health matters enormously. When anyone in a family has a health problem and goes into the hospital, you find that the family's work and daily life are thrown into complete disorder. So every person's health within the family matters, to themselves and to others as well. Sometimes when you talk about health management with a stranger and the person says, I will not listen, I will not hear it, we tell ourselves that this person is simply not fortunate enough yet, and he can come back when his good fortune is complete.

You can have that attitude because the person has nothing to do with you. Of course you can. But what if that person is a member of your own family: your mother or father, your son, your husband, your wife? Can you still feel the same way? Can you say, Dad, you are just not fortunate enough, it does not matter if you do not take anything, come and find me after you get sick? Could you say that? The real question is, when he gets sick, who looks after him? We do, do we not? So when it comes to our own family, we have to work hard to learn and to grow, and in health management we have to find ways to change them.

It is like a child who will not study. If it is someone else's child, you might say, come back when you enjoy studying and understand why it matters. But if your own son will not study, what do you do? You find a way to help him come to love studying. We may take one attitude toward others, but toward our own family, at least, we should take responsibility, and we cannot push that responsibility away. So we are calling on every extended family and every household to ask: who will take on the role of family health manager and carry this responsibility?

And turned around, this is also very good for our own health. You will find that in a family, the people with a strong sense of responsibility tend to be healthier and live longer, because a strong sense of responsibility makes us feel useful. There is a story about this on the internet.

Baidu Baike tells the story of two men who shared a hospital room. They were about the same age and had about the same illness. One was a rich man from the city and the other a poor man from the countryside. The rich man had many relatives and friends, and they came to visit him wave after wave. Every visitor comforted him the same way: you do not need to worry about anything, everything at home is well taken care of, just rest and recover, there is nothing for you to worry about. Do we often comfort our own relatives like that? We certainly do. The poor man was different. Farm work was so busy that few people from home came to see him, and those who did sat by his bed and sighed: you are the pillar of this family, everything at home is waiting for you to get well and take charge again, the busy farming season is here, and without you at home everything is in a mess. Two months later a miracle happened. The poor countryman recovered and was discharged, while the rich man's condition worsened and he died. The rich man's relatives could not understand it, and they demanded of the medical staff why the same illness with the same treatment could be cured for him but not for them.

It happened that they asked a head nurse, someone with decades of experience, and she told them this result was not surprising. From the way you comforted him, she said, your patient already felt that he was no longer of any use to your family and no longer had any responsibility, so his body produced very few antibodies to fight the disease. The patient from the countryside was different. Everything at home still depended on him, and he had an important responsibility, so [[many disease-fighting antibodies in his body were activated|This is the speaker's anecdote. Research has not shown that a sense of responsibility activates antibodies, but large observational studies do link a stronger sense of purpose in life with lower mortality: in one US study of nearly 7,000 adults over 50, those with the weakest life purpose died at markedly higher rates. Such studies show association, not cause.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6632139/]]. That is a miracle created by a sense of responsibility.

So in future, when you visit someone in the hospital, do not comfort them by saying, just rest and do not worry about anything. Say instead: you need to take good care of yourself, because we are all waiting for you to come home, we all need you. You can see that a person's sense of responsibility matters a great deal to their physical health.

Why have so many of our friends been working so hard lately, online and offline, to spread the idea of family health management? I hope it has to do with this sense of responsibility. A sense of responsibility like this means we feel we are useful, right? Take my own family. In 2001, when my wife was in her twenties, she entered the health industry, and at around forty she retired early. I used to think that retiring with nothing to do would be wonderful. But past a certain age, you find that having nothing to do is simply a disaster. No one needs you anymore. Is that not a disaster?

So imagine that one day we are eighty or ninety. Will anyone need us? Look at it the other way. At our age now, we do not spend every day with people in their eighties and nineties. It is not that we do not want to; often we feel we just do not have much to talk about. But if [[Li Ka-shing|李嘉诚, Hong Kong's best-known businessman, born in 1928, founder of the Cheung Kong group and in his early nineties at the time of this course.|]] were willing to spend every day with us, would you want to talk with him?

Li Ka-shing is in his nineties too, and I am sure you would want to spend every day with him, because you feel you could learn so much from him, right?

So think about it now. One day, when we are eighty or ninety, will anyone need us? If no one needs us, can we do anything at all? Will we have any value left? And will our health not slide very quickly at that point? That is why I say now, let us do more, especially in the area of health. Do a little more for health and you will live a little longer. Imagine that today a hundred-year-old person came to teach you about health management. Would we listen carefully? Of course we would. Picture a hundred-year-old telling you how to live to a hundred. Would we not listen very carefully?

Of course. So in health management you will find that the older you get, the more valuable you are, because you are living proof of what you teach. Whatever your age, you can pass the idea of health on to others, and that makes you useful, right? If we live to a hundred and people still listen to us, what does that mean? It means we have value, right? All right, that is all for today. Starting tomorrow we will move into the next topic: what are the uncontrollable factors that affect our health? See you tomorrow.
