# Easing Stress: GABA and Sour Jujube Seed

Today we look again at two nutrients, GABA and sour jujube seed, this time from the angle of relieving stress. In the last lesson we learned that GABA's formal name is gamma-aminobutyric acid. Until now most people have thought of it mainly in terms of improving sleep quality, but research on GABA in fact shows that it improves sleep quality by easing a person's tension and stress. So it does not only improve sleep. Its easing of mood and stress, we can say, has a larger and broader effect on the body.

So in this lesson we will look more closely at GABA and sour jujube seed, and see how these two nutrients work to ease the heart, ease stress, and ease sleep. First, stress. Today we can say everybody has it. Students have the pressure of studying and exams, office workers have the pressure of work and socializing, middle-aged people have elderly parents above and children below, and in old age there is even more to worry about. Add declining health of one's own, and most people today are under enormous pressure. This pressure shows up not only in how we study and how efficiently we work. It also stirs up swings in mood and in the end affects your sleep, and if sleep goes badly, a whole series of health problems follows.

The most common is a disturbance of nerve function, which appears first as anxiety and even depression. Nine times out of ten it comes with disturbance of the digestive system, including diarrhea and constipation alternating with each other. Next comes memory decline, and in more serious cases it affects the health of the heart and blood vessels. Think about it: when a person is stressed and tense, the blood vessels contract easily, so blood pressure swings and the heart rhythm becomes irregular. Developing further, it leads to problems in the endocrine system.

For example, irregular cycles in women, breast lumps, and menopausal syndrome; and in men, hormonal disturbance, prostate abnormalities, and so on. You have all heard the old saying that worry can turn your hair white overnight. That is a classic case of stress and emotion causing trouble in the body.

When we mention changes in mood and mental state, it can sound rather vague. But if you know a little about brain waves, it becomes clear: a person's swings of thought and mood can be shown quite objectively in the swings of their brain waves.

There are two kinds of brain waves here. The first is the [[alpha wave|α波, the brain rhythm of about 8 to 12 cycles per second, recorded on an electroencephalogram and associated with a relaxed, calm, wakeful state. β波, beta waves, are faster rhythms associated with alert and active thinking. The speaker's division into calm alpha and agitated beta is a simplification of a much more detailed picture in neuroscience.|]]. When people are in a calm, relaxed, pleasant state, it is mainly the alpha waves in the brain at work. The other is the beta wave. When a person is irritable, anxious, flustered, or sleepless, it is mainly beta waves showing.

So what does the gamma-aminobutyric acid we are discussing today do? GABA is an important neurotransmitter in the brain that produces calm and ease. What does a nerve in our brain usually look like? It looks like a fork, with a branch in front and a branch behind. If the nerve in front can release GABA well, the nerve behind can receive the front nerve's signal through its [[GABA receptors|The receiving sites on a nerve cell that GABA binds to. When GABA attaches to them, the receiving nerve becomes less likely to fire, which is why GABA is described as the main inhibitory, or calming, signal in the brain.|]].

Once the two connect, where a moment ago your brain was full of beta waves, agitated and tense, these two nerves have established communication with GABA's help, and immediately your alpha waves begin to increase, and you settle into calm and ease. That is why some people call GABA the vitamin of the emotions, which tells you how important a role it plays.

As we also said in the last lesson, why do we need to take GABA? Mainly because as we age, the amount our bodies make declines. That decline not only affects our sleep quality. It comes with a whole set of symptoms: declining language ability, declining eyesight, declining physical agility, and so on.

And when a person is under heavy stress and sleeping poorly, their need for GABA rises, while their own ability to make it struggles to keep up, so the gap grows wider and wider.

We say GABA does three main things: it eases stress, eases the heart, and eases sleep. Once we take enough GABA, we can increase the calm and relaxed alpha waves in the brain.

There is a clinical trial with a very good result: after enough GABA was taken, not only did the number of alpha waves increase clearly, it also effectively suppressed the beta waves. What does that tell us? It tells us that GABA can better suppress beta waves when a person is faced with stimulation from outside. In other words, when your mood is agitated or your sleep is poor, it can help you settle your emotions faster, ease your stress, and support your sleep.

In Japan there was [[an experiment done with students|The speaker's account of a Japanese experiment; he does not name the study, and I was not able to locate it. A review of GABA as a food supplement notes that some studies report calming effects, but that several were carried out by researchers with financial ties to GABA manufacturers, and that independent evidence is needed.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4594160/]]. The students who took GABA answered more questions and answered them more accurately than the group that did not. Real-time monitoring during the exam also showed that the tension of the students who had not taken it climbed steadily throughout the exam, while the students who had taken GABA stayed emotionally steady very easily.

From this series of experiments, the Japanese researchers found that GABA has a good stress-easing effect on the brain. So if we understand GABA only as something that improves sleep quality, that is too narrow. How could you give GABA to your child sitting the college entrance exam if you were afraid they would fall asleep in the exam hall? From this experiment with students taking GABA, we can understand its essential role. Its core function is to ease a person's stress, and life gives us no shortage of stressful situations.

Someone asks: if GABA eases stress, can it ease blood pressure? We find that when a person's mood swings, blood pressure swings too. And in another body of research on GABA, it was indeed found to help regulate blood pressure. One set of experiments showed that after taking GABA, the rise in systolic and diastolic pressure caused by emotional swings in the test subjects was reduced to a certain degree. It is because of this large body of clinical testing, and because of the mechanism, that [[GABA's blood-pressure-easing function was established|The speaker's conclusion. Trials of GABA in food products have reported modest reductions in blood pressure, but the evidence base is small and the question of whether oral GABA reaches the brain is still unsettled. This is not a treatment for diagnosed high blood pressure, which needs a doctor's care.|]].

Next let us talk about easing the heart. A good deal of research has found that GABA can have a heart-easing effect. When we mention menopause, many people know that women at this stage especially need calm and peace of mind. Experimental results showed that with GABA supplementation, 65 percent of cases of menopausal insomnia or depression improved, and 75 percent of menopausal syndrome improved.

As we said yesterday, taking GABA can lengthen a person's deep sleep and raise the quality of their sleep.

What GABA addresses is not the problem of falling asleep quickly. It is not a sleeping pill. It is very healthy and safe, and what it addresses is raising the quality of sleep. It can ease your stress by relieving your tension, and improve your sleep. Even if your sleeping time is short, it can let you get more high-quality deep sleep within that limited time. That is the sleep-easing effect we are describing. You may not sleep as long as someone else, but the quality of your sleep will be higher than theirs.

At this point we can sum up briefly. First, GABA is the vitamin of the emotions, an important neurotransmitter in the brain that settles us. Second, GABA can soothe the nerves and bring a person's mental state into calm and ease.
