# The Antioxidant Trio and the Minerals That Guard Aging

Dear friends, it is a joy to be with you again. My topic today is guarding the code of aging, and it covers the remarkable antioxidant trio together with the minerals. We all use the words oxidation and antioxidant, but few of us really understand what they mean, so let me start with a picture. Bite into an apple, get called away to do something else, and leave it on the table. What happens? It turns yellow and brown. Leave an iron nail outside in the wind and rain for long enough. What happens? It rusts. That is oxidation, and in the body it is the damage done by free radicals, unstable molecules produced during normal metabolism and by outside exposures. Antioxidants are the molecules that neutralize them. In the last two lessons we studied what protein does and what the B vitamins do, and today we move on.

Let me begin with a quick review. Vitamins divide into water-soluble and fat-soluble. The water-soluble ones are the B group and C. The fat-soluble ones are A, D, E and K. Today we will focus on A, C and E, because K and D are rarely deficient in practice. Minerals divide into major minerals and trace minerals. There are many major minerals, and today we will focus on calcium and magnesium. Among the trace minerals we will talk about iron.

Let me start with vitamin C. Go back to that bitten apple that has turned yellow. What do you do about it? What do you do about the rusted nail? And, for the women listening, what do you do when dark spots appear on your face? If you have enough vitamin C, the yellowed apple turns white again. Dust vitamin C powder onto the cut surface and you can watch it happen, and a woman's face responds in the same way. Vitamin C is the water-soluble antioxidant. It does its main antioxidant work in the blood plasma and inside the cells.

Vitamin C has many jobs. Today a great many children cannot concentrate in class. They are irritable, their memory is slipping and their learning is falling behind. Many parents take these children to the hospital, run tests, and discover that the heavy metal lead is above the safe limit. What then? This is where we recommend vitamin C, because vitamin C helps the body excrete heavy metals. And what about the spots on the face I mentioned, or skin that darkens the moment the sun touches it? Vitamin C is very good at inhibiting the activity of tyrosinase, the enzyme that drives pigment production, and at suppressing the melanin bodies, so it helps keep the skin fair and even. On top of that, vitamin C has a very strong immune function.

You will remember that during the pandemic, vitamin C sold out everywhere. Why? Because vitamin C promotes the formation of antibodies, and in particular it strengthens the fighting ability of the white blood cells. That is why, during the pandemic, people with good immunity had almost no symptoms, while people with weak immunity sometimes lost their lives. Vitamin C also promotes the absorption of iron.

Many of my friends notice that if they stand for a long time their head begins to swim, or that when they squat and then stand up quickly they see stars. Their fingernails are pale, the inside of the lower eyelid is pale, and their lips are pale. That is anemia. And they say, "I have been taking iron, I have been taking protein, how can I still be anemic?"

The answer is that iron needs vitamin C to be absorbed. At the same time, vitamin C keeps the blood vessels elastic and is very good at preventing atherosclerosis. So look at what we have gathered: anemia, low immunity, hardening of the arteries, heavy metal overload and all the pigmented spots on the skin. These are all the cells going hungry. What is the one thing they share? A shortage of vitamin C, which leaves the cells in a state of hidden hunger. If vitamin C is topped up, and topped up in full, every single day, because it is water-soluble and cleared from the body within twenty-four hours, then the symptoms of that [[hidden hunger|隐性饥饿, the term the World Health Organization uses for a lack of one or more essential micronutrients in a person who is eating enough calories. It is called hidden because it has no obvious sign of starvation, yet it undermines growth, immunity and long-term health.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10960185/]] disappear.

Beyond its antioxidant role, vitamin C has another very valuable job: it promotes the synthesis of collagen. Many friends wonder what that actually means. Plenty of young women who care about their looks notice that the face has hollowed or the skin has gone slack, and they rush off to the beauty clinic for fillers, hyaluronic acid or botulinum injections. My advice to every beauty-conscious woman is this. Give your body enough vitamin C, calcium and protein, and your own liver can manufacture collagen. It will make your skin bouncy, bright and fair. And collagen is not only for the skin. It is just as important for the bones and the internal organs. Vitamin C also does an excellent job of blocking the formation of nitrosamines inside the body.

Many of our friends like to eat leftover rice and dishes from the night before, and they like pickled and cured foods. Those foods contain a great deal of nitrite, which forms nitrosamines in the body, and vitamin C helps lower the rate at which cancer develops from them.

Let us move on to vitamin E, our fat-soluble antioxidant. It works mainly in the cell membranes, the organelles inside the cell and the fatty tissues. Its medical name is tocopherol, which comes from Greek roots meaning to bear offspring, so it literally means the fertility factor. As the name suggests, it supports reproductive ability in both men and women. It can improve the vitality and count of sperm and it supports ovarian function. It also brightens the skin and helps fade spots.

Many people ask how that is different from vitamin C. Vitamin C prevents spots from forming. Vitamin E takes the melasma and pigment spots that have already formed on the face and makes them lighter, sometimes until they disappear.

We also see this in people who have had surgery, whether a cesarean section, an appendix removal or anything else. Some people heal with a scar so fine it looks like a strand of hair. Others are left with something that looks like a centipede crawling across the skin. What I want you to understand is that the centipede on the surface is only a cosmetic problem. Do you know what the real issue is?

If you are prone to heavy scarring, it tells you that your body is short of at least one thing, vitamin E. And if you have ever had pneumonia, a fatty liver or any other inflammation in your internal organs, those organs scar too. You simply cannot see it with the naked eye. Vitamin E is very good at repairing scars both on the skin and inside the body. It also keeps the blood vessels elastic so that blood flows normally. What excites us most is that it slows aging. It has been said that it can extend life [[2.4 times|This figure is one the speaker quotes from the course materials. Now let us look at vitamin A, and here I want to explain why we talk about carotenoids. Carotenoids can be converted into vitamin A, and the body converts exactly as much as it needs. Where does the surplus go? It is stored under the skin. If you notice your skin taking on a yellow tint, cut back a little or pause for a while, and once the yellow is gone you can resume. Taking vitamin A itself, in its preformed state, can easily build up to toxic levels, which is why [[carotenoids|Provitamin A carotenoids such as beta-carotene are plant pigments that the body converts to vitamin A only as needed, so they do not cause vitamin A toxicity. So what does vitamin A do? Many of our primary and secondary school students are already nearsighted and wearing glasses at a young age. Nearsightedness is not actually the frightening part. The frightening part is that so many of us stare at phones, tablets and computer screens. These are all light-emitting surfaces. They give off blue and violet light, which passes through the eye's optical system and lands on the macula at the back of the eye. It can cause the macula to degenerate, and in severe cases it can effectively burn the macula out. Severe macular degeneration leads to blindness. So taking carotenoids in good time is an excellent way to protect against [[macular degeneration|The macula is the central part of the retina responsible for sharp vision. The second job of vitamin A is the health of the skin and mucous membranes. Many of you will say that the B vitamins do that too, and that is true. The B vitamins cover the skin and mucous membranes. Vitamin A goes further. It not only covers them, it maintains their health, including the health of the lining tissues inside the body. When vitamin A is lacking, those tissues keratinize, they turn hard and horny, and keratinized tissue becomes an excellent food source for bacteria.

So think about every place in the body that is lined with skin or mucous membrane: the eyes, the nose, the mouth, the esophagus, the stomach and intestines, the prostate, the vagina, the liver, the lungs and more.

Every one of these conditions is connected to a shortage of vitamin A: pharyngitis, enlarged adenoids, rhinitis, gastritis, stomach ulcers, duodenal ulcers, enlarged prostate, prostatitis, prostate calcification, cervical erosion, and even kidney stones.

When the pandemic came, we all felt very clearly what a shortage of vitamin A means, with that razor-blade throat and the ear pain. Vitamin A stimulates the body's production of immunoglobulins. It also promotes growth and development.

We see many children whose growth has stalled or slowed, and their anxious parents want to give them growth hormone injections. My suggestion is to make sure the child has enough vitamin A through carotenoids. Then, when puberty arrives and the internal organs have plenty of energy, the child will grow very quickly. Vitamin A also helps prevent chronic disease. Every nodule, cyst, fluid build-up and stone takes years to form, at least five or ten, and behind them all is the same thing, excessive keratinization of the epithelium. All of it is a signal that the cells are hungry for vitamin A. Let me be precise. I am not saying that vitamin A treats these diseases. I am saying that these diseases arrive because the cells are starved of vitamin A, and therefore, when vitamin A is replenished, the symptoms can go away.
