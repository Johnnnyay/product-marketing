# When the Blood Is Clean, Disease Recedes

Hello to every friend who loves health. My name is Ling Min. I am from Shenyang in Liaoning province, a nationally registered dietary nutritionist and a nutrition and health consultant certified by the Chinese Nutrition Society. Our topic today is that when the blood is clean, disease recedes. Traditional medicine has a saying: [[turbid blood breeds a hundred illnesses, and clean blood makes a hundred illnesses disappear|血浊治百病，血净百病消, a traditional Chinese medicine maxim. "A hundred illnesses" is an idiom for illness of every kind. The teaching is that the quality of the blood, meaning how much waste it carries, sits upstream of most chronic disease.|]]. If the toxins that build up in the gut are not cleared in time, they enter the bloodstream, clog and damage the blood vessels, and set off all kinds of chronic disease. So while we clear toxins from the gut, we also have to clear toxins from the blood.

When I introduced myself in the last class, I said that I began paying attention to health because members of my family developed health problems and left us too early. So today I want to start with my own story.

In 2005 my older sister suffered a sudden brainstem hemorrhage. The doctors could not save her, and she died. She was only 49 that year. You can imagine what her sudden death did to our family. My sister was on the heavy side, 1.68 meters tall and weighing over 150 jin, about 75 kilograms. She had known for a long time that she had high blood pressure. At an earlier hospital checkup her systolic pressure had measured over 200. The doctor told her this was very dangerous and advised her to be admitted for treatment. She said, "I am used to it. I tried taking blood pressure medication, and when the pressure came down I actually felt worse. At this high pressure I do not feel anything at all." Nobody expected that her neglect of her own health would cost her her life.

At that time I myself was a heavyweight of more than 150 jin. At the annual checkup my workplace organized, my readings were always high: a systolic pressure of 170 to 180 and a diastolic pressure around 100, plus severe fatty liver.

My sister's death was a warning to me. I started paying attention to my own health, and at the same time I began using my spare time to actively study everything related to health. I did not want to remain a health illiterate. What we do not know, we must learn. And through learning I came to understand that our family's lifestyle and diet had real problems.

We liked animal foods and refined rice and flour, and our oil and salt intake was well over the limit. Over a long period, toxins in the body built up past the threshold, systemic inflammation increased throughout the body, and the result was high blood pressure and fatty liver. It took a family member's life to make me take my own health seriously. The change started with lifestyle and diet: less salt, less oil, less animal food. I used dietary fiber and probiotics to clear the gut of toxins and maintain a healthy gut ecosystem.

I used lecithin and a herbal formula to clear the blood of toxins. I raised the share of whole grains and plant foods in my meals to correct my nutritional imbalance, and I added complete nutritional supplementation to address the [[hidden hunger|隐性饥饿, the term the World Health Organization uses for a lack of one or more essential micronutrients in a person who is eating enough calories. It is called hidden because there is no obvious sign of starvation, yet it undermines the cells' ability to repair and defend the body.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10960185/]] in my cells, so that my body could repair itself. At the same time, every day I used my free time to exercise: badminton, tennis, cycling and so on. In less than half a year my weight dropped by almost 30 jin, about 15 kilograms. When I went back for a checkup, the results showed that my severe fatty liver was gone and my blood pressure was back within the normal range.

I had once had exactly the same symptoms as my sister, but I did not follow in her footsteps, because I hit the brakes in time on the road toward disease. Through active learning I understood that health depends on yourself, and that the body's healing begins with doing it no harm. From that point on, I consciously returned to a harm-free lifestyle. What is a harm-free lifestyle? Teacher Pan Jinzhen will explain it in detail in her class on regulating lifestyle. It amounts to shutting off the tap at the toxin inlet, reducing the harm toxins do to the body, and at the same time carrying [[clear, regulate, replenish and nourish|清调补养, the four-stage framework of this whole course.
