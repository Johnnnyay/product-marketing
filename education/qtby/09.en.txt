Now let us talk about sufficient sleep. Good sleep habits and a regular daily schedule are the foundation of health management. Heart disease, obesity, dementia, cancer and diabetes are all tied in countless ways to insufficient sleep. Adequate sleep not only helps the body detoxify and repair its cells better, it is also the precondition for being energetic during the day. How much sleep does an adult need each night? Six to eight hours, and the younger you are, the more sleep you need.

Friends in the livestream room, do you feel that waking up is like a lottery? Some mornings you feel refreshed, and some mornings you can barely open your eyes. Scientific research has confirmed that [[sleep runs in cycles, from non-REM sleep to REM sleep, each lasting about 1.5 hours|Harvard Health describes each sleep cycle as averaging about 90 minutes, moving through light sleep, deep sleep and REM (rapid eye movement) sleep, with four to six cycles over a typical night.|https://www.health.harvard.edu/sleep/what-is-sleep-architecture]]. A full night's sleep generally needs four to five of these cycles. Many people set an alarm for getting up, but very few work out their sleep cycles and set a time for going to bed. Suppose you need to get up at 5:00 in the morning. To protect your sleep quality you want at least four cycles, which is six hours, plus 30 minutes to fall asleep, so six and a half hours in all. That means you should set an alarm to be in bed by 10:30 at night. If the alarm drags you out of deep sleep in the middle of a cycle, a forced reboot, you feel terrible all over: you cannot open your eyes, your head is heavy and foggy, and some people cannot get going for the entire day.

So understanding your sleep cycles can raise your sleep quality. For a midday nap, 15 to 30 minutes is enough; you should get up before you sink into deep sleep. If you want to sleep longer, then sleep one full cycle: half an hour to fall asleep plus one and a half hours, two hours in all. [[Going to bed early nourishes the liver's yin, and rising early generates yang energy|早睡养肝阴，早起生阳气, a traditional Chinese medicine principle. Yin and yang are the complementary qualities of rest and activity. Sleeping early is said to replenish the liver's restorative (yin) side, and rising early to build the body's active, warming (yang) energy. The saying that follows, 日出而作，日落而息, is the classical ideal of working at sunrise and resting at sunset.|]]: work when the sun rises and rest when it sets. For modern people surrounded by technology, this is easy to know and hard to do. However much you hate to go to sleep, put down your phone. Falling asleep before 11 at night counts as sleeping; falling asleep after 11 counts as staying up late.

Now let us talk about moderate exercise. We all know that life is movement, but modern people have fewer and fewer chances to move. Mental work has largely replaced physical work. We drive when we go out and take the elevator when we go up. If we can lie down we will not sit, and if we can sit we will not stand. As a result, more than half of the people in our country are overweight, and metabolic diseases rise year after year. Not exercising slows the body's metabolism, hampers the excretion of toxins, and raises the risk of chronic disease.

People who do not exercise have plenty of excuses: too busy, too tired, no time. In truth, in the fast pace of modern life, exercise does not have to take any particular form. You can use whatever is around you to get moving anytime, anywhere.

A friend of mine is extremely busy at work, and after work she still has children and a household to look after. Her approach to exercise is to drive as little as possible and take the subway and bus instead, to stand rather than sit on public transport, to take the stairs while others take the escalator, and when time allows, if she can get there without a vehicle, to walk. In this way she still manages 6,000 to 8,000 steps every day. In short, eating and moving must balance. Find a way to get moving, find a form of exercise you are good at and can stick with, and build a good exercise habit. Some people ask, is more exercise always better?

We all know that [[Ma Hua, China's first fitness aerobics star|马华, the aerobics instructor whose short televised workouts made her a household name in China in the 1990s. Excessive exercise brings on a prolonged [[immune window|免疫空窗期, the "open window" hypothesis in exercise science: after prolonged or intense exercise, certain immune cell counts fall for a period of hours, which may leave the body more open to infection. Excessive exercise also pushes the body toward an oxidative state, and in the most serious cases it ends in sudden death.

So both not exercising and over-exercising damage health. Exercise must be moderate, and nutritional replenishment after exercise is very necessary. It guards against the low immunity of the immune window, helps the body quickly repair the wear and tear of exercise, and makes up for the large loss of nutrients during exercise that would otherwise leave the cells in hidden hunger.
