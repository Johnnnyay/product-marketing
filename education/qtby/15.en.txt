# A Nourishing Breakfast for Health Every Day

My hope is that by sharing a little of what I know, I can influence more and more of the friends around me to build a correct understanding of health while they are still young.

Today I have the honor of exploring with you a topic that has benefited me enormously: a nourishing breakfast so that health accompanies you every day. First, let us look back at the road of [[clear, regulate, replenish and nourish|清调补养, the four-stage framework of this course. Clear removes toxins from the gut and the blood, regulate corrects lifestyle and the balance of the organs, replenish supplies the cells with complete nutrition, and nourish is the daily and seasonal care that keeps the result.|]] that the earlier lessons laid out. From those lessons I am sure you all now know that the underlying logic of disease is the accumulation of toxins together with nutritional imbalance and hidden hunger in the cells, which together lead to the failure of organ function. In response we have the clear-regulate-replenish-nourish plan for managing health. Clear means expelling gut toxins and blood toxins. Regulate means adjusting the way we live and restoring balance among the organs. Replenish is what Teacher Ding Guizhen covered in detail over the last few lessons, how to correct the nutritional imbalance and [[hidden hunger|隐性饥饿, the World Health Organization's term for a shortfall in one or more essential micronutrients in a person who eats enough calories. With me, we enter the stage of nourish.

Let me first ask the friends in front of the screen: do you think nourishing matters? You will probably say of course it does, since I have written it right there on the slide. So let me use an example, and together we can examine whether nourishing really matters as much as we claim. My own profession is environmental engineering, and when I first learned about the clear-regulate-replenish-nourish plan, I thought it was simply brilliant, because its underlying logic is exactly the same as the logic of environmental remediation and the way we manage nature.

Look at the photograph on the left. This is a polluted river. You can see with your own eyes that it is filthy and it stinks. The remediation process for a river like this can also be described as clear, regulate, replenish and nourish.

First, clear. We remove the rubbish from the channel and dredge the silt from the riverbed. Then regulate. Look at the sewage outfalls; they have to be adjusted or shut. Upstream and downstream have to be adjusted and dredged so the water flows again. Next comes replenish, repairing the banks. Where there are gaps we rebuild and stabilize the slopes to stop erosion, and we plant and landscape. After all that effort we arrive at the river on the right. Does it not look wonderfully pleasant and easy on the eye? But is that the end of it? Can we now leave it alone? Do you believe that?

If we do no maintenance and no management from this point, then in perhaps less than a year the river will look like the left-hand picture again. You do not know whether someone will go on dumping rubbish, whether a factory will keep quietly discharging waste, or whether the upstream and downstream channels will silt up again. So, friends, if I give you two choices, which do you pick? Choice one: once the river is green again, I stop caring for it, and when it has turned back into a stinking ditch I come back and clear it, regulate it and repair it all over again. Or choice two: once the river is green again, I spend a small amount of time every day managing and maintaining it so that it stays green and natural for the long term.

Which would you choose? I am confident that everyone online would choose the second, just as I would, because daily maintenance is without question the lowest-cost and best-result approach. The same logic applies to health management. After clearing, regulating and replenishing, the body has improved a great deal.

That is exactly the moment to do a good job of nourishing, and nourishing has a four-word secret. The first two words are: it rests on nutrition. Teacher Ding Guizhen told us in detail over three lessons that the human body is built from seven major nutrients, so the main raw materials for repairing and maintaining the body are those same nutrients. The second two words are: it rests on breakfast. What is the easiest and best way to nourish the body every single day? A nourishing breakfast.

Breakfast is the starting point of complete nutrition, and therefore the starting point of complete health. Let us look at why breakfast in particular. The ancients explained it very clearly. The day's plan is made in the morning. The old texts say that the stomach is what nourishes the five organs. The [[Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic|黄帝内经, the Huangdi Neijing, the foundational text of Chinese medicine, compiled roughly two thousand years ago. It describes the stomach and spleen as the acquired root of life, the source from which the other organs draw their nourishment.|]] says the stomach is the acquired root that nourishes the five organs, and that stomach qi is the root of the body's qi. If food does not enter the stomach, in half a day the qi weakens and in a full day it fails. Skip breakfast and very soon a person has no energy, and it becomes hard to concentrate on work for the whole day. Now look at the classic chart that has been handed down, the midnight-to-noon meridian clock. What is [[the meridian clock|子午流注, the traditional Chinese medicine idea that qi flows through the twelve meridians in a fixed daily cycle, with each organ's channel most active for one two-hour period. It underlies the advice about which organ to care for at which hour throughout this course.|]]?

Midnight-to-noon refers to the hours of the day. Flow means flowing, and pour means pouring in. In different hours, what should we pour into the body? Look at the chart on the left. There is a green shaded band on the right side of the ring, and the outer ring reads five, six, seven, eight, nine, the hours of the twenty-four hour day. From five to seven, the hour of mao, the large intestine meridian is at its most active. If you drink some warm water at this time, it is an excellent window for emptying the bowel. And from seven to nine? That is the stomach.

The stomach meridian is on duty, and the stomach is ready to go to work, digesting and absorbing what we eat. Then from nine to eleven the spleen meridian takes over, carrying the nutrients the stomach has digested and absorbed to the whole body for every organ to use. This is the wisdom of our ancestors about how to live well. Traditional dietary wisdom also stresses eating with restraint and rhythm. Restraint here does not only mean moderation; it also means rhythm. So breakfast matters a great deal, and it is not enough to eat at the right time. More importantly, we have to eat the right food.

Friends, are you eating the right food? According to the statistics in China's breakfast behavior white paper, the nutritional quality of our nation's breakfast is generally inadequate. Look at this set of numbers. More than half of the population eats too few types of food at breakfast and gets too little nutrition. More than ninety percent eat a breakfast that is mainly carbohydrate, such as steamed buns, bread rolls and rice noodles. It looks like variety, but only one nutrient is actually going in. Fruit, vegetables and legumes are all lacking. The breakfasts of Chinese students are especially worrying. This affects not only the development of young people but also their ability to learn, and it plants an enormous hidden risk for their future health.

Let us look at the breakfasts we actually eat day to day and ask how nutritious they are. Do you see your favorite here? Beijing friends love sugar pancakes with tofu pudding. Lanzhou has its beef noodles. Harbin has fried dough sticks with tofu pudding. These are the breakfasts we take completely for granted. So what is the problem with them?

When a professional nutrition body analyzed them, three problems emerged. First, too few types of food. Second, low nutrient density and poor balance. Third, far too many fried foods, which are not a good breakfast choice. So three "nots": not enough, not balanced, not good. The same analysis shows a pattern of two highs and four shortfalls. Fat and carbohydrate are too high, while vitamins, minerals, unsaturated fatty acids, dietary fiber and protein are seriously short.

Now come south. Do you recognize any of these? Chongqing noodles, crossing-the-bridge rice noodles, soup dumplings and so on all share the same problem. Refined carbohydrate and saturated fat are too high, while vitamins, minerals, unsaturated fatty acids, dietary fiber and protein are seriously short. What does a breakfast like that do? The excess refined carbohydrate and saturated fat lead to the accumulation of toxins in the body. The shortfall in vitamins, fiber and protein leads to nutritional imbalance and hidden hunger in the cells.

Remember why we said the body gets sick? The underlying logic of disease is that too much toxin accumulates from what we take in, and the cells are nutritionally unbalanced and hungry. Look at the picture on the left. A healthy cell needs this whole range of nutrients: vitamins, minerals, phytonutrients, high-quality protein, unsaturated fatty acids, dietary fiber and more. What we actually give the cell today is carbohydrate alone. Every other nutrient is either short or missing. Keep the cell in that condition long enough and you get toxin accumulation on top of malnutrition.

Slowly the tissue develops problems. Then the organ produces symptoms. Then the diseases we recognize appear. So whatever condition the body shows, the underlying logic is that the cells are sick, and a sick cell means toxin accumulation, nutritional imbalance and hidden hunger. Every one of these is closely tied to our breakfast.

Look at the numbers in the bottom left. What harm comes from eating a poor breakfast or skipping it altogether? First, lower work efficiency and poorer concentration in study. The risk of overweight and obesity rises 48 percent, the risk of type 2 diabetes rises 21 percent, the risk of heart disease rises 24 percent, and the risk of death from cardiovascular disease rises [[40 percent|These percentages are as the speaker presented them from the course slide. Cohort studies do link regularly skipping breakfast with higher cardiovascular mortality, and a 2023 meta-analysis found that breakfast skipping raised LDL cholesterol, although the exact figures vary by study.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10715426/]]. Some of you may wonder whether a poor breakfast can really do that much damage. So let us look at the latest national health statistics. Cardiovascular disease is now the number one killer in our country, and close to 300 million Chinese carry cardiovascular risk. According to a national authority, citing a report published this June in the authoritative journal [[The Lancet|The reference is to the Global Burden of Disease dietary risk analysis published in The Lancet, which ranked diets high in refined grains and low in whole grains, fruit and vegetables among the leading dietary contributors to cardiovascular death worldwide.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6899507/]], the main dietary risk factors behind cardiovascular disease are too much refined rice and white flour and too much saturated fat. Is that not exactly the two highs we identified? They drive toxin accumulation. And alongside them, too little fruit, vegetables and whole grains.

We also said that the four shortfalls in breakfast lead to nutritional imbalance and hidden hunger in the cells. Long-term toxin accumulation and long-term cellular hunger are therefore the main dietary risk factors for cardiovascular disease.

So, friends, do you now see why breakfast matters? There is a saying that dripping water wears through stone and grains of sand pile into a tower. An unhealthy breakfast once or twice may not matter. But if it goes on for one year, two years, five years or ten, the big data we already have tells us that the long-term damage accumulates into exactly the diseases and the statistics we are looking at now.

So how should a good breakfast be eaten so that its nutrition is both complete and balanced? This brings us to China's most authoritative health body, the Chinese Nutrition Society, which has tailored the most suitable pattern of healthy eating for Chinese people. It is called the [[Chinese Dietary Pagoda|中国居民膳食宝塔, the Chinese Nutrition Society's food guide. It stacks the food groups in tiers by recommended daily amount, with grains at the base, then vegetables and fruit, then animal foods, then dairy and legumes, with oil and salt at the top. It is China's equivalent of the food pyramid or plate guides used elsewhere.|]]. I am sure many of you have seen or heard of the pagoda, but very few people know how to actually eat by it, and even fewer really do.

Fortunately, here we have to thank one institution in particular, the Nutrilite Nutrition and Health Research Center, which did something remarkable. It took the Dietary Pagoda apart systematically, tier by tier, and worked out which specific nutrients sit behind each food and what those nutrients do. Together with an expert panel from the Chinese Nutrition Society, it launched the breakfast nutrition and health model best suited to Chinese residents. In plain terms, if you want the most nutritious and complete breakfast, eat according to this healthy breakfast model. Look at what it contains: high-quality fats, high-quality protein, multiple vitamins and minerals, phytochemicals, dietary fiber, and probiotics to support absorption in the gut. Eat these seven nutrients well every day and health will accompany you every day. And there is an exciting discovery I want to share.

When we look at this authoritative breakfast model alongside the Nutrilite nutritious breakfast matrix, we find that it is in fact putting clear-regulate-replenish-nourish into practice. How do we clear gut toxins? Through probiotics and dietary fiber. How do we clear blood toxins? Through a breakfast rich in high-quality fats, with good phospholipids and the 3-6-9 oils, which help clear the blood. How do we reduce inflammation in the body and balance the organs? Through the concentrated fruits and vegetables that are rich in phytochemicals. And finally, how do we supply the cells with complete nutrition and avoid hidden hunger? By replenishing high-quality protein together with multiple vitamins and minerals.

With those three steps we complete clear, regulate and replenish, and we arrive again at nourish, which here means nourishing according to the day's meridian clock. Between seven and nine in the morning, when the stomach meridian is on duty, if we eat breakfast carefully according to the Chinese Nutrition Society's breakfast nutrition model, you will find that the moment all seven categories of breakfast food go into the body, the whole clear-regulate-replenish process is complete as well.
