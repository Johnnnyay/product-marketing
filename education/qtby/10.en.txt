Today the baton passes to me, and my topic is inflammation and phytonutrients. I have given it a slightly playful name: anti-inflammation notes, the secrets in plants. [[Inflammation|炎症. Harvard Health describes acute inflammation as the body's immediate protective response to injury, with the classic signs of redness, warmth, swelling and pain, and chronic inflammation as that same response persisting long after it should have shut off, so that it begins to damage healthy tissue.|https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/understanding-acute-and-chronic-inflammation]], what people in everyday life call "getting inflamed," is the body's defensive response to a stimulus. Its familiar signs are redness, swelling, heat and pain, along with some loss of function. These signs are in fact part of the process by which the body repairs the damage the stimulus caused. When ordinary people hear the word inflammation, their first reaction is that it must be bad. In reality, in most cases the inflammatory response is beneficial. It shows that the body has immune capacity. If the body is injured and there is no inflammatory response, that means its immune mechanism is paralyzed. But there are also times when the immune mechanism attacks the body's own tissues, which is what we commonly call the various allergic and autoimmune reactions, and the inflammation that results from these is harmful.

Rheumatoid arthritis and lupus are examples. Here there is a piece of logic that you must keep clear: the inflammatory response is beneficial, while the inflammatory triggers that cause it are harmful. But beneficial or harmful, none of us wants inflammation showing up in our bodies all the time.

So how does inflammation actually arise? To sum it up, as you can see in the picture: viruses, bacteria, oxidation, injuries of every kind, toxins and so on. None of these are strangers to us. You could say that as long as we are alive, we are facing inflammatory triggers every moment. What effect does inflammation have on us? Let us look together. The first is its effect on the immune system, mainly the various allergic reactions and skin problems. These are not life-threatening, or at least in most cases not, but they have a considerable effect on quality of life, and they also disturb our hormone balance.

The second is its effect on the nervous system, the various conditions shown in the picture. Among them, Alzheimer's disease has seen a new discovery in the last couple of years. At first scientists believed the disease was tied to [[beta-amyloid in the brain|β淀粉样蛋白, a protein fragment that clumps into the plaques found in the brains of people with Alzheimer's disease. A 2024 narrative review describes how an imbalanced gut microbiome, through inflammation and a weakened blood-brain barrier, may promote both amyloid plaques and tau tangles, and notes that microbiome-targeted therapies are being studied but still need larger human trials.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11646158/]]. You can picture it with the folk expression "a head full of paste." But after targeted treatments were tried against it, the results were not ideal. Recent research has found that Alzheimer's disease, and also childhood autism, depression and so on, are directly related to imbalance in the gut flora. Scientists are now studying how to treat this class of diseases by managing the gut flora.

The third is its effect on the circulatory system. The fat inside human blood vessels is very afraid of being oxidized. Once it is oxidized, inflammatory factors appear, and these cause inflammation of the inner lining of the vessel wall. The repair actions the immune system takes to clear that inflammation thicken the inner lining, and that is how hardening of the arteries and lipid plaques form. At the same time these inflammatory triggers can turn blood lipids into a clot, creating the risk of a blockage. The picture on the left shows a vessel that has already hardened, and an actual clot that was removed.

The fourth is its effect on the urinary system. The kidneys filter the body's waste water, and the glomeruli are the filters inside them. Inflammation of the glomeruli burdens the whole kidney, and if it persists for a long time it leads to kidney failure and uremia. Diseases of the prostate and bladder likewise develop from simple, localized inflammation.

The fifth is its effect on the digestive system. The stomach and intestines are constantly at work digesting food and rarely get to rest, so once inflammation appears there it is not easy to recover, and it tends to recur and worsen. The upper photo shows a duodenal bulb ulcer and the lower photo shows enteritis. You can see obvious congestion of the mucous membrane, and if this progresses step by step it becomes cancerous.

The sixth is its effect on the respiratory system. The upper picture is a typical case of tissue overgrowth in the nasal mucosa. When inflammation appears in the lungs, it does irreversible damage to the alveoli, which shrink and lose their elasticity, like plump grapes turning into shriveled raisins. Lung cancer forms in exactly this way, one step at a time.

The seventh is its effect on the muscles and bones. I am sure the great majority of people have experienced frozen shoulder, tenosynovitis and the like. This is a special form of inflammation that we call non-infectious inflammation. It is not caused by bacteria from outside. Rather, dead cells from the body's tissues that ought to be metabolized and carried out of the body are not, because as the body ages its metabolic capacity weakens. These dead cells build up locally, and as their numbers grow they affect the normal tissue around them. When that happens, the body's immune cells gather there in large numbers to clean up, and that process produces the soreness, swelling, pain and restricted movement.

The usual approach is to use traditional Chinese methods such as gua sha scraping and cupping on the uncomfortable spot, which brings good relief. The principle is that local stimulation promotes blood flow, which carries away the accumulated dead cells. In fact, for this sort of problem, as long as we keep a sensible lifestyle and diet, take in a moderate amount of nutrients rich in phytonutrients day to day, keep our immunity relatively stable and get moderate exercise, it will heal on its own after a while, without treatment. That is the immune mechanism automatically completing the cleanup of the affected area.

The first is tea polyphenols, a phytonutrient abundant in tea leaves. Their most formidable component is [[EGCG, whose antioxidant activity is 100 times that of vitamin C and 25 times that of vitamin E|表没食子儿茶素没食子酸酯, epigallocatechin gallate, the main catechin in green tea. The multiples the speaker quotes come from laboratory antioxidant assays. The NIH's complementary health center notes that green tea catechins have been studied mainly for weight, cholesterol and cancer risk, with modest or inconclusive results in people.|https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/green-tea]], making it a very powerful ingredient for preventing cells from being oxidized.

The second is [[bilberry|越橘, a European relative of the blueberry, sold as an extract for eye health. The NIH notes its traditional use for vision and blood sugar, including the wartime story of British pilots eating bilberry jam, and reports that rigorous studies have not clearly shown it to help any health condition, including night vision.|https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/bilberry]]. Beyond its basic benefits, its most formidable property is inhibiting the activity of the hepatitis B virus and of tumor cells, and it is also the guardian of our eyesight.

The third is [[glycyrrhizic acid|甘草酸, the main active compound of licorice root, 甘草, a staple herb in traditional Chinese medicine. The NIH notes that glycyrrhizin in large amounts or over the long term can cause serious effects such as irregular heartbeat, and advises people with heart, kidney or blood pressure conditions to consult a healthcare provider before using licorice products.|https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/licorice-root]]. It has many actions. Beyond broad-spectrum anti-inflammatory effects, the key point is that it strongly inhibits the multiplication of cancer cells and of the HIV virus. The fourth is [[allicin|大蒜素, the sulfur compound released when garlic is crushed, responsible for its smell and much of its studied activity. The NIH reports that garlic supplements may reduce blood sugar, total and LDL cholesterol, and blood pressure to a small extent in people whose levels are high.|https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/garlic]]. As everyone knows, allicin kills bacteria and raises immunity. Its other action is actually just as strong: maintaining blood sugar balance. Friends with abnormal blood sugar can take in more of it in daily life.

The last is [[Cistanche tubulosa|管花肉苁蓉, a desert parasitic plant known in Chinese medicine as "desert ginseng," traditionally used to tonify kidney yang and improve vitality. A 2023 review in Frontiers in Nutrition catalogs more than 120 bioactive compounds in cistanche and reports neuroprotective, immune-modulating, antioxidant and liver-protective activity in laboratory and animal studies.|https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10042234/]]. Most friends know it only for warming and tonifying kidney yang, but its actions do not stop there. It is equally powerful at protecting the liver and maintaining the permeability of blood vessels, and its effect on the permeability of the tiny blood vessels of the glomeruli in particular is striking. There really are a great many phytonutrients in nature that benefit the human body, and we cannot cover them one by one here.
