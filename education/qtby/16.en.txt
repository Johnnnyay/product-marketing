Since we are right in the middle of high summer, I will start by sorting out the summer heart-care topic with you. Summer belongs to the fire element, which connects to the heart. So we really do stress daily care. First, though, let us understand the heart from different angles, mainly the angle of Chinese medicine and the angle of Western medicine. And let me be clear at the outset: today I am not teaching medicine. I am passing on common sense. In Chinese medicine the [[heart|In traditional Chinese medicine the heart is said to govern the blood and vessels and to house the shen, the mind or spirit, so it is called the sovereign organ. This is a functional concept that covers circulation, consciousness and emotion together, not only the anatomical pump.|]] refers to the blood vessels and to the emotions and mind. Chinese medicine says the heart governs the blood vessels, driving the blood through every large and small vessel so that it circulates through the whole body. The heart also houses the spirit, which means consciousness and thought. That is why the heart is called the sovereign organ. What does Western medicine mean by the heart? The heart and the cardiovascular system. The heart's job is mainly to pump, supplying blood to every organ and driving circulation throughout the body. The cardiovascular system is a circulatory structure made up of arteries, veins and a network of capillaries, and its main function is to deliver blood carrying oxygen and nutrients continuously to the whole body. So the heart is extremely important.

Now that we understand the heart, we need to know what signals it sends when it is not comfortable, especially in summer. Shortness of breath, heavy sweating and low stamina, for example. Some friends develop insomnia, anxiety or a restless, oppressed feeling. In more serious cases there is a racing heartbeat, a fast heart rate, rising blood pressure, tightness or pain in the chest. If you have symptoms at that level, please see a doctor promptly.

Before we think about how to care for the heart, let me take you through how the traditional schools each see it. Chinese medicine emphasizes protecting the heart through diet, daily rhythm and emotion. The yin-yang school says we must avoid overspending our yang energy while also nourishing yin to restrain excess heart fire.

The five-element school says summer belongs to fire and resonates with the heart. Modern medicine focuses mainly on preventing cardiovascular disease in summer, and on improving what? Our emotional state, to promote overall health.

Having looked at each school's view, you may feel that each has its own emphasis, so whose advice should you follow? Do not worry. Let us start with the first item under healthy lifestyle, managing emotions for the heart. We said a moment ago that when summer arrives many people become anxious, irritable, agitated or low. Negative emotions of this kind produce large amounts of internally generated harmful factors, and they impair the liver's metabolic and detoxifying functions. They inevitably increase the load on the heart, they can trigger irregular heart rhythms, they disturb the circulation, and they cause the capillaries to constrict so that the whole body is short of blood and oxygen. So what do we need to do? Notice, and deliberately remind ourselves, when our mood is changing. Then tell ourselves to learn to adjust and relax: listen to gentle music, do some soothing exercise, find a good friend to talk to.

The most important thing of all, though, is to find a positive environment and do something of value, and to avoid fixating on the small self. As we say, upgrade the small self to achieve the greater self. Heal yourself by helping others. Then you no longer have to worry about being trapped in negative emotion. Behaviors like these inevitably lead to toxin accumulation and inflammation, and they trigger obesity, rising blood sugar, rising blood lipids and rising blood pressure. In serious cases they lead to atherosclerosis.

Please pay attention, because all of these are early signs of cardiovascular disease. As they accumulate over the years they become very dangerous and threaten the health of the heart. So what do we do?

After eating, there is one more reminder, and that is drinking, by which I mean drinking water. Maintain the water balance. Summer temperatures are high and we sweat easily, so we must replace fluids promptly. The third heart-care recommendation concerns exercise. Summer arrives and many friends love to get moving, so I have three suggestions here. First, prevent exercise injuries: warm up and stretch thoroughly, have enough water and electrolytes ready, and wear suitable exercise clothing.

Second, control the timing and intensity. Avoid the hottest hours, moderate your intensity, and do not do strenuous exercise, especially if you already have cardiovascular disease. Avoid it entirely in that case, or you will add to the load on the heart and vessels and risk triggering an event.

Third, it has to suit the individual. Take your own constitution and differences into account. I recommend gentler forms such as [[Baduanjin|八段锦, the Eight Brocades, a traditional Chinese mind-body exercise of eight flowing movements. Next comes sleep management for the heart.

Good sleep lets the heart repair fully, so I have three suggestions here as well. First, go to bed early and rise early. Summer days are long and we should follow the rhythm of nature. Pay attention to the length of sleep and to the sleep cycles. We covered this in the lifestyle management lesson, and I recommend going back to listen. Then, take a midday rest. Between eleven and one, when the heart meridian is on duty, take a noon nap. Even ten or twenty minutes will do. It relieves fatigue effectively and benefits the heart.

The last point is to avoid staying up late. Late nights inevitably lead to toxin accumulation and inflammation, and they increase the burden on the heart.

At this point let me remind you of something: build your own health file, and keep watching how your body changes. That includes the physical examination reports we talked about earlier. Friends who already have cardiovascular conditions in particular should have regular electrocardiograms and blood pressure and blood lipid checks so that problems and changes in the heart are caught early.

Also pay attention to the signals the body sends. I mentioned chest tightness, chest pain, palpitations and shortness of breath. If you have these, see a doctor promptly.

We also need to watch the everyday signals. For example, if the edges of your tongue carry tooth marks, be careful. Some friends sometimes feel a tingling, ant-crawling numbness through the body. Be alert to signals like these, because they are early signs of cardiovascular disease.

Finally, keep an eye on your body composition analysis and review it regularly. Focus on body fat, visceral fat and muscle mass, especially visceral fat.
