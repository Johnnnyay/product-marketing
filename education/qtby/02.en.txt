# Your Personal Health Record Is Your Dashboard

Good evening, everyone. My name is Liu Jin, and I am very glad to have this chance tonight to talk with you online about health. Those of you who joined the last class heard Teacher Jianjun give a wonderful explanation of the root logic of disease and of how to clear, regulate, replenish and nourish. I am sure that after listening you are looking forward to the rest of this health course. Tonight I want to talk about something that comes before we even begin the work of clearing, regulating, replenishing and nourishing. There is one very important thing to do first: we have to learn to read our health dashboard. And reading it begins with building a personal health record.

My own connection to health management also began with a personal health record. In my day job I lead the technical department of an environmental engineering company.

I remember that in 2020 my company required me to go to the hospital for a physical. Before the exam it had honestly never crossed my mind that anything could be wrong with my body. I thought of myself as young, I was only twenty-eight, and at the time I loved exercise. I could even run a marathon.

Yes, work was busy, and there was plenty of overtime, late nights and business dinners, but I felt fine in every respect. So when the results came back, I was genuinely shocked. Several readings had arrows next to them. One of them was something called [[aspartate aminotransferase|天门冬氨酸氨基转移酶, usually abbreviated AST, an enzyme found mainly in the liver and also in the heart and muscles. When liver cells are damaged they release AST into the blood, so a high AST reading is a common sign of liver stress or liver disease.|https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/ast-test/]]. I had no idea what it was, but I could see the reference range printed beside it was 0 to 40, and mine was 246. Six times over the limit. I did not understand it, but six times the limit gave me a real fright, and I wondered how serious it might be. Sure enough, that afternoon the hospital called. The doctor asked whether I had ever had hepatitis, whether I felt unwell anywhere, whether I had habits like drinking and staying up late. As I listened my heart was pounding, because the business dinners, the drinking, the overtime and the late nights were all very real. Could something actually be wrong? The doctor told me to rest properly for a few days and then come back for a liver function test.

Around then I also learned that the liver has no pain nerves, so by the time it produces a problem you can feel, things may already be quite serious or quite advanced. I do not know whether you have ever had a stretch like this, but for those few days I was on edge the whole time. I had worked in Shenzhen for many years and almost never went to sleep before midnight. During those days I forced myself, every night, to switch off the lights and be in bed before twelve, and to rest properly.

In the end I was very lucky. When I was retested, the reading had returned to normal. It had almost certainly been related to the long stretch of late nights and drinking that preceded the exam.

The test turned out to be a false alarm, but it taught me a real lesson. I came away with two strong convictions. First, disease has everything to do with young people too. Do not assume that because you are young, disease is far away. Second, health really does seem to be one of those things you only wake up to at the moment you are about to lose it, and it is far too important for that. In a way, it was a blessing in disguise. After that, with the influence and encouragement of my parents, I began attending the [[Beiruide|贝瑞德, the name of the health education platform and reading-group community whose teachers deliver this course.]] health reading group, learning to raise my understanding of health and how to protect it. Along the way I earned the [[national health manager and national public nutritionist certifications|国家健康管理师 and 国家公共营养师, two vocational qualifications administered in mainland China for health management practitioners and community nutritionists.]]. So I am grateful for the opportunity tonight to exchange ideas with you. Before we learn how to build a personal health record, though, I want us to first take in two important ideas about health. They are the two ideas that helped me most on my own path of learning health management.

The first idea comes in the form of a question: who is the person who will turn you over? I suspect that the first time you hear it, you are puzzled. What does it mean, the person who turns you over? It comes from a test, and the test is extremely simple. Two words: stay in bed.

You can see the picture on the right, a person lying in bed. But this exercise has a rule. If no one helps you, you cannot move and you cannot turn over. See how long you can lie there. Flat on your back or on your side, either is fine, but without someone to help you, you may not turn.

Dear friends, have any of you tried this? Before I did it, I too thought, how hard can it be? Lying still for an hour or two should be easy. Only when I actually tried it did I realize how difficult it is.

I remember that at ten minutes I felt fine, quite comfortable. As it got close to twenty minutes my whole body started to feel uneasy, not so comfortable anymore, and I badly wanted to shift my hips or move a leg, but I was not allowed to move. At around thirty minutes I had a very distinct feeling that time itself had slowed down, and every minute was a kind of torment.

Perhaps that is the point of the test. Only when we actually reach that moment do we truly feel how much health matters. Because in the end most diseases put you in a bed, and if no one helps you, you cannot turn yourself over. And while you are lying there doing the test, the important question arrives. Ask yourself: if that day really comes, who is the person who will turn you over? Who is the one you could wake in the middle of the night without hesitation, without worrying about being a bother, without feeling you owed them an apology? Who is the person you could call over to turn you?

Everyone watching right now, who came to mind? The question, of course, places us inside a very real scene. If you actually became ill and could no longer care for yourself, how would that feel? And what would it mean for your family? It is exactly as Teacher Jianjun said in yesterday's class: protecting your health is not only being responsible to yourself, it is being responsible to your family. So who is the person who turns you over? Starting today, treat that person well.

For the second idea, I want you to look at this picture and do a little exercise with me. We are going to guess a proverb. We all learned it or heard it as children, so let me reveal the answer: [[picking up the sesame seed and dropping the watermelon|捡了芝麻，丢了西瓜, a Chinese proverb: to grab something small and trivial while losing something large and important. The English equivalent is "penny wise, pound foolish."]]. The meaning is plain. The man on the left has picked up a sesame seed and let go of a watermelon. He grabbed something minor and lost something major and important. What does that have to do with us?

Bring it back to daily life. Ask the people around you, and ask yourself: why do we work so hard and push so hard every day? Most people's answer comes down to two words: making money. And making money is a fine thing. We work hard to earn so that we can improve our lives. But if in the course of making money you ignore your body, or fail to look after it, or sacrifice your health by staying up late and working overtime to earn more, then that is exactly picking up the sesame seed and dropping the watermelon. Why? Because of something you may not realize.

If your body is healthy, with no illness and no pain, then you are already worth at least ten million. That is not my claim. Look at this chart. It comes from a United States organization that publishes the average cost of various kinds of organ transplants. The organs we know well, the heart, liver, spleen, lungs and kidneys, are listed in the left column. If any one of them fails and you are in a position to have it replaced, this is the average amount it costs. Look at the figures. For any single organ, the transplant plus the aftercare runs to at least several million dollars.

So right now, put a hand on your heart, on your belly, on your arm. Do you realize that everything you have just touched adds up to a treasure worth at least tens of millions? You are worth a great deal. Of course I say "tens of millions" only to make it easy to grasp, because every part of our body is, in truth, priceless. Even if you could buy a kidney for ten million, that does not mean it would be installed in you and work as a healthy one. So if you are healthy right now, with no illness and no pain, what is that worth? Priceless, isn't it?

Or at the very least, tens of millions. So we are extremely valuable. Which brings us to the next important question. This precious body of ours, this exquisitely engineered and expensive instrument, do you know how to operate it correctly and how to care for it? If you have a car, whatever the price, you take it to the dealership for regular servicing and inspection, and you wash it regularly, inside and out. If we think of the body as a car, do you know how to drive it properly, how to care for it, how to maintain it?

That is exactly why we have to learn to build a personal health record.

Follow my train of thought here. Look at the picture on the left. Most of you will recognize it as a car's dashboard. What does a dashboard do? Through it we can see how fast the car is going, how much fuel is left in the tank, the engine's revolutions, and the temperature of the coolant.

In short, the dashboard lets us see the car's operating data and condition clearly, and lets us notice a fault in time, which keeps us safe on the road. So is the dashboard important? Very. Now here is the key. Suppose one day you get into a car, start it, and the dashboard looks like this. The car starts normally, but the dashboard is like the one on the right. Let me ask you: with a car like that, would you dare to drive it?

If you would, I admire your courage. But even if you dared to drive it, I am sure nobody would dare to ride with you, because it is extremely dangerous. The dashboard cannot tell you how much fuel you have. There is no speedometer, so on a road with a limit of 100 you might be doing 140 without knowing. There is not a single warning light, so what happens if you step on the accelerator and discover the brakes have failed? And yet, do you realize something?

We would not dare to drive a car with no dashboard. But in real life most of us drive exactly such a car through our days, every day. That car is our body. Do you know where your body's dashboard is? Do you understand your own health status? Do you know what your health risks are? Do you know your bone age? Do you know what distress signals your body is sending? If you are not clear on any of this, are we not all driving a car with no dashboard, careening through our lives?

Have you ever seen or heard of someone whose body, that car, suddenly stalled one day and broke down, and was towed off to the repair shop? Where that repair shop is, I will let you imagine. So tonight I want to congratulate you, because starting today we are going to make up this important lesson together.

We are going to learn how to build a personal health record. A personal health record is the dashboard of your body. Through it we can read and understand the state of our body and its data, and only then can we know how to drive this car properly and keep our journey safe.

No one wants to be driving along and suddenly stall and be towed to a repair shop we never wanted to enter. So let's look at what exactly a personal health record is. It is very simple: three documents in all. To keep it easy to follow, we will stay with the car analogy. What does the picture below look like? A dealership inspecting a car, isn't it? Dear friends, a car goes in for a scheduled inspection every year. Do you get a professional physical every year?

The first document in your personal health record is the physical exam report. It gives a professional, comprehensive picture of your baseline condition. The second document, what does this picture mean? If you are an experienced driver or a veteran mechanic, you can usually tell how a car is doing just by driving it for a bit. If the car shakes badly, the engine may have a fault. If you press the brake and it feels soft, you know the brake pads probably need replacing.

The car cannot talk, but you can read its signals. In the same way, our body cannot talk, but when something goes wrong it gives us plenty of signals.

Let me ask you: have you had mouth ulcers? Have you had what we call "getting heaty," with a sore throat or breakouts? Have you had insomnia? Do you catch colds often? Every one of these symptoms is the body sending us a distress signal. So how do we learn to read those signals?

We learn by studying our [[98-item health assessment form|98项健康评估表, the self-assessment checklist used in this course. It records basic information, eleven lifestyle habits and 98 possible symptoms, grouped by body system, that the person marks as frequent or occasional. It is a structured self-report tool, not a diagnostic test.]]. With it we can learn to read the body's signals. If we do not understand those signals, disease and danger may be waiting not far ahead. The third document: here you see a dashboard covered with warning lights. Every car runs a self-check the moment you start it. The engine and the air conditioning each check themselves. Is there a way for the human body to run a self-check every day as well? There is, and it is the body composition analysis report.

We can check it daily or at regular intervals, tracking things like weight, visceral fat, muscle percentage and so on. If we learn to read a body composition report, it is as though we can give ourselves a health self-test every day. It is like learning to take our own pulse.

Those three documents together make up your personal health record. Through them we assemble our personal health dashboard. In the classes that follow, our professional teachers will explain each of the three in detail. For the physical exam report, what should we focus on? What is the root cause behind each reading? Is there a way to adjust or improve the exam results? The 98-item assessment will get the same item-by-item professional walkthrough. Are you looking forward to it? I certainly am, because the personal health record is simply too important. Without it, what are we? We are driving a car with no dashboard down the road of our lives. Just thinking about it, that is a dangerous thing to do, isn't it?

Let's finish by reviewing tonight's key points. First, we shared two very important health ideas. Ask yourself often: who is the person who will turn me over? I strongly encourage you, before you go to sleep tonight, to go to bed a little early and try it. See how long you can lie without turning over. Second, no illness and no pain means what? It means you are already a multimillionaire. So remember, we must not pick up the sesame seed and drop the watermelon. Learn to care for your health properly. With no illness and no pain, you are a multimillionaire.

Third, why do we build a personal health record? Let's answer it together: because we cannot drive a car with no dashboard down the road. The car on the right, the one with no readings, would you dare to drive it? Most of us would not. And finally, what is a personal health record? It is three documents: the physical exam report, the 98-item health assessment form, and the body composition analysis report. In my own case, it was a physical exam that showed me an abnormal reading, and that was what made me start taking health seriously, start learning health management, and start practicing it in earnest.

Over the several years since, I have found that not only have I become healthier, with better numbers, but my whole energy and outlook are better than they were in the years before. And I now have the ability to help the people I love, and the people who love me, manage their health too.

All of this began with building a personal health record together. When we have a grip on these three documents, we have a grip on the dashboard of our life's health, and only then can we drive this luxury car of ours safely and joyfully down the long road of life. A life in full speed begins with mastering your health dashboard. That is the end of my sharing tonight. My sincere wishes to all of you on the road of health management.
