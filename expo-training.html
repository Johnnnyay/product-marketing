Expo Specialist Training

Last time most of us waited for Irene to close. This time every one of us is a specialist: you take one guest through health and skin, show them a demo, ask for the sale, and walk them to checkout with the product in their hand.

Fri, Oct 305:00 to 7:30 PMRight before FNH

What we decided

From our retro after the Sep 25 evening:

  • One guest, one specialist. You stay with your guest from hello to checkout, through health and skin. No handing them from station to station.
  • Two sides of the room. Health sits next to XS (protein is right there). Skin on the other side. Hair is optional this time. Irene booked a bigger room.
  • Booking is optional. If a guest booked a slot and shows up on time, they go next. Late or no booking, they join the waitlist, first come first served.
  • Check-ins before they arrive. Guests fill in the health and skin check-ins from the event page before they come. It made Irene's skin consultations quick last time.
  • Demos are yours. Run the demo for your own guest when it fits their pain point. We will also announce a few demos to the room.
  • Every sale goes through checkout. Wendy runs checkout. Even if a product is not there that night, take the order at checkout and we place it after. Never leave it to the guest to follow up.
  • Invite people with a real interest. Not "come have fun", but "you mentioned your sleep, come do a check-in".

Before the night

1. Invite the right people

Find out what they care about first, then invite them to the thing that helps with it.

Say it like this"You mentioned you've been struggling with your sleep. What have you tried so far? We're doing a health night on Oct 30 where you can do a quick check-in and see what might help. Want me to save you a spot?"

2. Re-invite last time's guests

Keep it simple, don't overthink the reason. They already know we have products.

Say it like this"Hey! We're doing another one on Oct 30, Halloween edition. Come by to tell me how the products are working, or to check out something new."

3. Send the check-ins

Every guest gets the event page link and fills in the health check-in (and skin) before they come. Ask them to text you when it's done.

4. Master one pain point

Pick one from the playbook below and know it cold: what's going on, the one line that explains it, the bundle, and the demo. Liver, sleep and stress, gut, and acne cover most of the people our age.

The walk-through

About 15 to 20 minutes per guest. If you have been talking to the same person for 20 minutes, ask for the sale or let them go. More time does not close more.

  1. 1
    1 MIN

    Greet and set the frame

    Say it like this"Hi, I'm Lister. Who invited you? Great. Tonight we'll look at your check-in together, I'll show you what's going on and what could help, and if something clicks you can grab it at checkout."
  2. 2
    3 MIN

    Read their check-in

    Open their answers in the event page's admin view. Pick the one or two things they marked often. That is your focus. Ask one question about it: "When does it hit you most?"

  3. 3
    5 MIN

    Explain and show

    Open that area's quick guide on your phone (links in the playbook). It's written the way Johnny explains it: what's going on, then the reassurance. Then show the demo that fits.

    Say it like this"That's a sugar spike. You don't have to stop eating rice, you just slow the sugar down."
  4. 4
    3 MIN

    Recommend one bundle and ask

    One bundle, not a menu. Show the price list. Then ask, out loud, and wait.

    Say it like this"Based on what you marked, this is where I'd start. Do you want to take it home tonight?"
  5. 5
    2 MIN

    Close or release

    Yes: walk them to Wendy with the product in their hand. No: no problem, find out who invited them so that person follows up, note it, and go get your next guest.

Asking for the sale

Irene's rule: there is no secret strategy. You ask, and if it's a no, the world still turns. The thing we were missing last time was the ask itself.

When they sayYou say
"How much is it?"Show the price list. "This is the price. Do you want to take it home tonight?"
"I'll think about it.""Totally. What's the part you're not sure about?" If still no: "No problem, who invited you? They'll check in with you next week."
"I'm just here for fun.""Love that. Want the two-minute version of your check-in? It takes no time."
"I already take vitamins.""Great, what are you taking? Let's see if it covers what you marked."
"Too many things."Drop to one product. "Then start with just this one."
Product not there tonightTake the order at checkout anyway. We place it after.

Don't

  • Talk for 20 minutes to build the relationship. It doesn't close the sale.
  • Hand them a menu of five bottles. One bundle.
  • Tell them to follow up with someone later. Lead them to the end tonight.
  • Promise results or say anything cures or treats. These are foods, not drugs.

Demos

Vitamin C apple

Our best demo, and we sold none last time because we didn't run it.

  • Slice an apple into small pieces (bring a fruit knife)
  • Coat half the pieces with dissolved vitamin C
  • Show both after 10 to 15 minutes: plain slices brown, the vitamin C ones stay fresh
Bridge

"Vitamin C is the body's main antioxidant. This is what it does on an apple."

XS mocktail

People loved the drinks last time.

  • XS, lemon, lime and mint in a cup
  • Two cans tied with a ribbon at 2 for $5
Bridge

"Zero sugar, B vitamins. This is what I drink instead of soda."

Twist Tubes

Twist, shake into water, drink. Good for anyone who travels or skips tablets.

Run the demo for your own guest when it fits. When a few people gather, announce one to the room: "We're doing the vitamin C demo now."

Training session

About an hour, the weekend before the expo.

  • 10 minWhat changed and why: one guest, one specialist, ask every time.
  • 15 minIrene walks one volunteer through health and skin live, start to checkout.
  • 25 minRole-play in threes: specialist, guest, observer. Three rounds, swap each time. Guests play: "tired after lunch", "breakouts before my period", "just here for fun, how much is it?"
  • 10 minPractice the vitamin C apple and the XS mocktail.
  • 5 minPick your pain point, confirm roles.

The observer checks

  • Under 20 minutes
  • Used the guest's check-in answers
  • Explained it in one or two plain sentences
  • One bundle, not a menu
  • Asked for the sale out loud
  • Ended at checkout or with the inviter's name noted
