Root Cause Analysis
Metabolic Health / Visceral Fat
Visceral fat at 12 at age 25 is the most significant finding in this assessment. Visceral fat is metabolically active — it secretes pro-inflammatory cytokines, contributes directly to insulin resistance, and is more strongly associated with blood pressure elevation than subcutaneous fat. A frequent takeout and processed food diet is the most consistent driver: these foods are calorie-dense, high in refined carbohydrates, sodium, and omega-6 fats, and nearly devoid of the fiber, phytonutrients, and micronutrients that regulate metabolic signaling. Tyler's 0-1 servings of produce per day compounds this: without antioxidants and fiber from food, the inflammatory signals from visceral fat accumulation go unmitigated. The blood pressure concern (often) at 25 is a downstream signal of this metabolic load — not a primary disease, but an early warning that the system is under strain.
Gut / Fiber Deficit
The combination of bloating often with dry, cracked stool that occurs 2+ times daily despite drinking more than 2L of water is a specific pattern: insufficient dietary fiber. Water alone cannot create the stool bulk and motility that fiber provides — fiber absorbs water in the gut to form a gel-like mass that moves efficiently and exits with appropriate consistency. Without it, the colon draws water back from the stool (drying it), while fermentable carbohydrates from processed food create gas and bloating. Tyler is taking probiotic and stress relief probiotic daily, which supplies the beneficial bacteria — but probiotics require prebiotic fiber as their food source to colonize effectively and provide the bloating relief he needs. This is the most direct supplement gap in his stack relative to his symptoms.
Diet vs. Supplement Stack
Tyler's supplement stack is genuinely impressive — omega, magnesium, B vitamins, CoQ10, liver support, vitamin D, memory builder, stress relief probiotic, Lions Mane, and Ashwagandha, all taken daily with consistency. This level of commitment shows real awareness of health. The challenge is that frequent takeout and processed food creates a dietary environment that the stack is working against: processed foods typically contain seed oils high in omega-6 (partially counteracting the omega supplement), refined carbohydrates that spike insulin and drive visceral fat storage (counteracting the metabolic intent of CoQ10 and liver support), and near-zero produce that starves the gut microbiome the probiotic is trying to build. The supplements are doing meaningful work; the opportunity is to reduce the dietary headwind so they can work with the food rather than against it.
Sleep / Cognitive Pattern
Staying up late often, with occasional brain fog and focus difficulty, and a strongly sedentary day — this cluster persists despite the cognitive supplement stack (Lions Mane, Memory Builder, Ashwagandha, B vitamins). The most likely explanation is that the dietary pattern is keeping the body in a mild chronic inflammatory and insulin-volatile state that no supplement can fully override when it is operating continuously. Late nights elevate cortisol the next morning; processed food creates repeated insulin spikes that cloud cognitive function; sitting for extended periods reduces cerebral blood flow. The supplement stack addresses chemistry; the lifestyle pattern addresses the inputs those supplements are working on.
Summary
Tyler is one of the most supplement-committed people seen in this assessment group, and that commitment is not wasted — the stack is well-chosen and covering many genuine gaps. The core finding is that the diet is presenting a stronger headwind than the supplements can fully overcome: visceral fat at 12, metabolic age 3 years older than actual, and persistent bloating despite good water intake all trace back to frequent processed food and near-zero produce. Adding prebiotic fiber (the one concrete gap in the stack) and reducing processed food frequency — even by two or three meals per week — combined with more produce, will work with the supplement stack rather than against it. The composition numbers are fully reversible at 25 with consistent dietary change.
30-Day Plan
1Replace Two Takeout Meals Per Week with Whole Food
  • The goal is not perfection — replacing 2-3 processed meals per week with a home-cooked or restaurant whole-food option starts shifting the visceral fat and metabolic age trajectory meaningfully
  • Prioritize meals with vegetables, lean protein, and minimal refined carbs — this is not a restriction, it is adding what is currently missing
2Add Produce — Minimum 3 Servings Daily
  • 0-1 servings currently; the supplement stack needs phytonutrients and fiber from food to function as intended
  • Easiest path: a handful of leafy greens or raw vegetables with lunch and a vegetable side at dinner — no cooking required for the lunch portion
  • Any fruit is a fast win: an apple or banana with the morning supplements takes 30 seconds and adds a produce serving immediately
3Sleep Boundary — Midnight Cutoff
  • Staying up late often is the most directly addressable cognitive and metabolic intervention available
  • Each hour of sleep before midnight contributes more restorative value than an hour after — the cortisol pattern the next day, cognitive function, and even visceral fat metabolism are all affected
  • Target midnight as a firm boundary this month; move it to 11:30 in month two
4Movement Breaks — Counter the Sedentary Pattern
  • Prolonged sitting (often) with a BMI of 29 and visceral fat of 12 creates compounding metabolic slowdown through the day
  • A 10-minute walk after at least one meal per day is the most evidence-backed single habit for reducing post-meal blood sugar spikes and supporting visceral fat reduction over time
