Root Cause Analysis
Fatty Liver / Hepatic Load (Primary Driver)
Frequent self-reported fatty liver at 29 reflects the liver's ongoing struggle to process the metabolic load from excess caloric intake. The liver converts excess calories — particularly from refined carbohydrates and dietary fat — into triglycerides for storage, and when this exceeds its clearance capacity, fat accumulates in liver cells. The binge eating pattern (marked Often) means Marco is regularly presenting the liver with caloric surges beyond what it can immediately process. Omega-3 EPA has direct clinical evidence for reducing liver triglyceride accumulation by downregulating the enzyme pathways that produce liver fat. Soluble fiber supports liver detoxification by binding bile acids in the gut and preventing their reabsorption, reducing the liver's recycling load.
Constipation / Gut (Secondary Driver)
Hard pellet stool despite adequate water is a fiber story, not a hydration story. Marco drinks 1.5–2 L of water, which is reasonable, but without adequate fiber the water passes through without forming a stool-bulking gel. Bristol Type 1 (hard pellets) indicates transit time is too slow — waste is sitting in the colon longer than it should, allowing reabsorption of metabolic byproducts the liver is trying to clear. Binge eating occasionally suggests hunger and satiety signals are dysregulated — soluble fiber directly addresses this by slowing gastric emptying and reducing appetite spikes. The gut and liver are in a bidirectional relationship: constipation worsens liver load, and liver dysfunction reduces bile output needed for normal digestion.
Anxiety / Autonomic Sensitivity
Anxiety marked Often at 29 with frequent motion sickness (vestibular sensitivity), occasional dizziness, and headaches suggests some autonomic nervous system hyperreactivity. Omega-3 DHA is the dominant fatty acid in the brain and has the strongest evidence base for reducing anxiety and supporting vestibular function. At BMI 32.6 with NAFLD, the inflammatory load itself raises cortisol and sensitizes the stress response — reducing inflammation through diet and omega-3 supplementation is a direct anxiety intervention, not just a metabolic one.
Summary
Marco's profile has a clear connection: fatty liver, constipation, and binge eating are all feeding each other through the gut-liver axis. The liver is stressed, the gut is slow, and hunger signals are dysregulated — producing a cycle of overconsumption and under-clearance. The interventions here are targeted: fiber to clear the gut and buffer blood glucose, omega-3 to reduce liver fat and calm the nervous system, and movement to improve metabolic clearance. His good water intake and sleep timing are genuine assets.
30-Day Plan
1Fiber at Every Meal
  • Add one fiber source to every meal: vegetables, legumes (beans, lentils), or a fiber supplement.
  • His stool (hard pellets) will normalize within 5–7 days as fiber intake increases — this is one of the fastest-responding changes.
  • Aim for 25–30 g fiber/day. A serving of beans at lunch + vegetables at dinner = approximately 12–15 g.
  • High-fiber breakfast: oats, fruit, or a high-fiber protein smoothie add another 5–8 g.
2Reduce Binge Eating — Structured Meals
  • Eat at consistent times: breakfast, lunch, dinner at set times reduces the blood sugar swings that trigger binge eating.
  • Protein + fat + fiber at every meal slows gastric emptying and extends satiety — the combination matters.
  • He already eats breakfast with protein (good). Add a fiber source to breakfast to further extend satiety.
  • If binge urges hit, drink 500 ml water first and wait 15 minutes — often it is thirst or blood sugar, not true hunger.
3Movement — Reduce Liver and Visceral Fat
  • 20–30 minute walk daily — exercise is the single most effective intervention for NAFLD independent of diet.
  • Break up prolonged sitting: stand and walk for 5 minutes every hour. This alone improves post-meal glucose clearance.
  • Resistance training 2x per week (even bodyweight exercises) builds skeletal muscle that acts as a glucose sink.
4Reduce Processed Food — Support Liver Recovery
  • Processed food (fast food, packaged snacks, fried items) are the primary dietary source driving NAFLD progression.
  • Replace one processed meal per day with a whole-food option — don't aim for perfection, aim for reduction.
  • The liver recovers fastest when the daily caloric and inflammatory load is consistently reduced.
