Root Cause Analysis
Stress / Nervous System (Primary Driver)
Adelita's nervous system profile is consistent and significant: hard to relax, mentally tense, anxious, emotionally out of control, startled awake at night, eyes sensitive to light — all marked Often. This is not everyday stress; this is a sustained high-cortisol state. At 48, perimenopausal hormonal fluctuation commonly amplifies cortisol reactivity and disrupts the HPA axis, creating a feedback loop where stress raises cortisol, cortisol disrupts sleep, poor sleep raises cortisol further. Skipping breakfast often adds a 6–8 hour fasting cortisol spike each morning. The irregular menstrual cycle likely reflects this HPA disruption rather than a separate cause. Magnesium is one of the most depleted minerals under chronic cortisol load — it is consumed by the stress response itself, which then worsens anxiety, muscle tension, and sleep quality.
Digestion / Gut (Secondary Driver)
Poor digestion and gastritis both flagged Often suggest this is not a passing issue. The gut-brain axis means chronic nervous system activation directly suppresses digestive enzyme output — the body diverts resources away from digestion when it perceives stress. This creates a cycle: poor digestion → reduced nutrient absorption → nutrient deficits → worsened mood, energy, and hair health → increased stress reactivity. The constipation and bloating are downstream of this enzyme deficit combined with 1–1.5 L water intake (low for her needs). Until digestion is supported, supplements and food both have reduced effectiveness.
Nutrient Absorption Gap (Consequence)
The hair cluster (falling out, dry, dull, grey — all Often) combined with body water at 43.9% and visceral fat at 12 suggests the body is operating in a low-nutrient-availability state despite a self-reported high-protein diet. Hair follicle cycling requires B-vitamins, zinc, iron, and protein — all of which depend on adequate digestive enzyme activity and adequate hydration to transport. At 1–1.5 L water/day, cellular hydration and nutrient transport are both compromised. The metabolic age running 5 years ahead of actual age suggests this has been a pattern building over time — it is reversible with consistency, but the window for action is now.
Inflammation (Underpinning)
Visceral fat at 12 is metabolically active tissue — it releases pro-inflammatory cytokines continuously. Combined with the stress cortisol load, this creates a chronic low-grade inflammatory environment that slows healing, disrupts hormonal signaling, and accelerates metabolic aging. The metabolic age gap (53 vs actual 48) is the measurable output of this inflammation. Reducing visceral fat is a long-term project, but improving sleep, reducing cortisol through digestion support, and increasing water and produce intake all reduce the inflammatory burden incrementally.
Summary
Adelita's picture has a clear center of gravity: a chronically activated stress response is suppressing her digestive function, which is reducing nutrient availability, which is showing up as hair loss, metabolic aging, and low cellular hydration. This is not a supplement gap — it is a system that needs to be calmed down and supported at the gut level first. Water, breakfast, digestive support, and nervous system recovery are the four levers. The smart scale data is excellent because it gives us a precise baseline to measure against at the 6-week recheck.
30-Day Plan
1Breakfast — Break the Cortisol Spike
  • Eat within 60 minutes of waking — every day. This is the single highest-leverage habit change.
  • Include protein at breakfast (already doing this occasionally — make it daily): eggs, Greek yogurt, or protein shake.
  • Even a small meal reduces the morning cortisol spike that is damaging her nervous system and hair follicles.
2Water — Rebuild Cellular Hydration
  • Target: 1.5 L this week, 2 L by week 3. Current 1–1.5 L is below threshold for her body composition.
  • One full glass immediately upon waking before coffee or food.
  • Water bottle visible at the desk at all times — refill at every meal.
3Evening Nervous System Wind-Down
  • Screens off 45 minutes before bed — blue light directly suppresses melatonin and delays cortisol clearance.
  • 10-minute walk or gentle stretching after dinner — activates the parasympathetic nervous system.
  • Consistent sleep time: aim for the same bedtime ± 30 minutes, which helps regulate cortisol rhythms.
4Movement — Address Visceral Fat
  • Stand or walk 5 minutes every hour during prolonged sitting.
  • 15–20 minute walk daily — visceral fat responds well to consistent moderate movement.
  • Tracking: note energy before and after walks. It should be noticeably better within 2 weeks.

Adelita's pattern of anxiety, tension, startled sleep, and emotional dysregulation all reflect the physiological effects of magnesium insufficiency under sustained stress load.
Take 1–2 capsules with dinner or before bed. Effects on sleep quality and relaxation are typically noticeable within 1–2 weeks. Start with dinner to assess tolerance.
Digestive Enzyme
With poor digestion and gastritis both marked Often, Adelita's gut is consistently underperforming on nutrient extraction. This is the infrastructure fix that makes everything else — food, other supplements, water — more effective.
Take 1 capsule with each main meal. Results for digestion comfort (less bloating, less post-meal heaviness) are typically felt within 1–2 weeks.
