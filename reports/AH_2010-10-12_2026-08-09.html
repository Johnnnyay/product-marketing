Root Cause Analysis
Sleep Timing (Primary Driver)
The human body's growth hormone, cortisol, and melatonin cycles are set by sleep timing, not just sleep duration. Staying up late Often — likely past midnight given the teen pattern — shifts the cortisol awakening response and suppresses the slow-wave sleep in which growth hormone is released. At 15, this matters more than at any other age: the brain is actively pruning and rewiring, emotional regulation circuitry (prefrontal cortex) is developing, and hormonal cycles including the menstrual cycle are consolidating. The lose-temper-often, dark-circles-often, and emotional-dysregulation-occasionally pattern is a direct consequence of this sleep timing mismatch — not a character trait. Shifting bedtime earlier by 30 minutes each week toward 10:30 PM would have measurable mood effects within 2–3 weeks.
Dehydration (Amplifier)
500–1000 ml daily is under half of what Arianna needs. The brain is approximately 75% water — at this level of dehydration, cognitive speed and emotional regulation both degrade measurably. The dark circles (often) and brain fog (occasionally) are the two most visible outputs. Hydration also affects the gut, which produces serotonin — the neurotransmitter that regulates mood and sleep quality. Improving water intake to 1.5–2 L will likely reduce dark circles, sharpen focus, and reduce emotional volatility within 1–2 weeks.
Hormonal / Menstrual Pattern
Moderate period pain, heavy flow, and PMS mood swings in a teenager with no omega-3 intake is a prostaglandin imbalance picture. Prostaglandins are hormone-like compounds that drive uterine contractions during the period — when the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio is skewed toward omega-6 (from a diet without fatty fish or supplementation), the prostaglandins produced are more inflammatory, causing stronger contractions and heavier flow. EPA from omega-3 competes with and replaces these pro-inflammatory prostaglandins, reducing period pain and flow over 2–3 cycles. At 15, establishing this nutritional support early has long-term benefit for hormonal health.
Summary
Arianna is doing well on produce (4–5 servings/day is genuinely impressive for a 15-year-old) and her baseline diet is solid. The three things to address are sleep timing, water, and menstrual support — all of which interact. Better sleep reduces emotional reactivity. More water reduces dark circles and brain fog. Omega-3 support reduces period pain and PMS over 2–3 cycles. These are foundational habits that will pay dividends well beyond the next 4 weeks.
30-Day Plan
1Sleep Timing — Start Earlier in Small Steps
  • Shift bedtime 30 minutes earlier each week: if currently midnight, target 11:30 this week, 11:00 next week.
  • Screens off 45 minutes before target bedtime — blue light delays melatonin onset by 1–2 hours.
  • Keep the room dark and cool. A fan or white noise helps if external sounds are disruptive.
  • Consistency on weekends matters — sleeping in 2+ hours on weekends resets the biological clock backward.
2Water — Simple Daily Minimum
  • Target: 1.5 L daily. Start with a 500 ml bottle at breakfast and another at lunch — that alone reaches 1 L.
  • One glass immediately upon waking, before the phone.
  • Water bottle visible on the desk at school — easy to reach = more likely to drink.
3Continue the Produce Strength
  • 4–5 servings/day is excellent — maintain this. It is already providing fiber, antioxidants, and B-vitamins.
  • Add iron-rich foods in the week before the period to compensate for heavy flow: dark leafy greens, lentils, fortified cereals.
  • Vitamin C alongside iron sources (a squeeze of lemon, orange juice) doubles iron absorption.
