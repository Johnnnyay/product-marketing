Root Cause Analysis
Sleep / Nervous System
The 1-3 AM waking window is a well-documented cortisol micro-spike that the body uses to stabilize blood glucose during sleep. In most healthy adults this happens silently, but when the nervous system is running above its baseline — from work/life pressure (often), mental tension (often), and inadequate wind-down — the same cortisol pulse is strong enough to pull someone into full wakefulness. Hunter's pattern is reinforced by three corroborating signals: cold sweaty hands and feet often (vasomotor instability, consistent with sympathetic overdrive), occasional heart palpitations (adrenal load), and restless sleep (shallow sleep architecture). Staying up past midnight keeps cortisol artificially elevated at exactly the window when it should be at its lowest. This is a nervous system that does not get to fully stop.
Gut / Digestive Pattern
Loose stool 2+ times daily with 3-4 wipes, a high-meat diet, and 0-1 servings of produce per day is a specific pattern: undigested protein reaching the lower gut feeds certain bacterial populations that produce inflammatory byproducts, while the near-zero fiber intake means there is nothing to buffer, slow, or bind the transit. The result is loose, frequent, irritating stools that gradually erode the gut lining. At 81kg consuming 1-1.5L of water per day, Hunter is also mildly dehydrated — which concentrates the gut environment and further stresses mucosal integrity. This is not a dramatic gut disorder, but it is a quiet inflammatory contributor that compounds everything else over time.
Inflammation / Joint Health
Joint pain reported as "often" at age 26 is not normal aging — it is a signal worth taking seriously. A high-meat, near-zero-produce diet creates a predictable omega-3 deficit: animal fats (especially without fish or plant-based omega-3 sources) are predominantly omega-6, and the ratio of omega-6 to omega-3 in the typical Western high-meat diet runs 15:1 to 20:1. The healthy target is closer to 4:1. This imbalance tips the prostaglandin pathway toward pro-inflammatory signaling, which shows up first in joints — especially under the load of daily sitting, prolonged screen time, and likely limited mobility work. The back pain (occasionally) and the occasional dizziness add further texture to this inflammatory picture.
Blood Sugar / Cognitive Energy
Skipping breakfast often with a BMR of 1,829 kcal means Hunter goes hours into his morning burning on empty, which forces the body to elevate cortisol as an emergency energy signal. Combined with work/life pressure already pushing cortisol higher, this creates a compounding cortisol load in the morning hours. The downstream effects match what Hunter reports: brain fog occasionally, hard to focus occasionally, easy to forget occasionally. These are not concentration problems — they are blood sugar stability problems that look like attention problems. The good news is that on days Hunter does eat breakfast with protein (which he reported doing sometimes), this entire pattern interrupts itself, which means the mechanism is highly responsive to the simplest intervention.
Summary
Hunter's body composition is genuinely strong for 26 — the muscle mass, visceral fat, and metabolic age all reflect someone who is physically capable and not in crisis. What the data and symptom pattern reveal is a nervous system that cannot fully shut off at night, a gut running hotter than it needs to on a diet that gives it almost no fiber or hydration, and an inflammatory load from the omega-3 deficit that is showing up in joints earlier than it should.
