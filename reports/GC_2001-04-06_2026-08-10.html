Grace's composition is within healthy bounds overall — BMI in range, visceral fat well controlled, bone density good. The two values worth attention are body water at 50.1% and metabolic age running one year older than actual. Both are consistent with insufficient water intake (1-1.5L/day for a 71.7kg woman is notably low), a mostly sedentary pattern, and a diet that leans toward refined carbs and low produce. The good news: these are among the most responsive metrics to lifestyle change. A meaningful recheck at 4 weeks is realistic.

Root Cause Analysis
Hormonal / Skin / Menstrual Pattern
Grace's most visible concern — frequent facial acne — and her most impactful monthly experience — heavy flow, period pain, PMS mood swings — share a common upstream driver: the prostaglandin pathway. Prostaglandins are fatty-acid-derived compounds that regulate inflammation, uterine contractions, and skin sebum activity. When omega-3 intake is low relative to omega-6, the balance tips pro-inflammatory: the uterus contracts harder (more pain, heavier flow), PMS mood instability worsens, and the skin produces more inflammatory sebum. Grace's diet is mostly vegetarian with refined carbs and low produce — a near-zero omega-3 food source — and her current supplement stack does not include omega-3. This is the clearest gap in an otherwise comprehensive stack. The occasional anemia is almost certainly tied to the heavy flow: heavy monthly blood loss steadily depletes iron stores, and vegetarian non-heme iron absorbs at a fraction of animal sources, making repletion slow without supplementation.
Blood Sugar / Breakfast
Skipping breakfast often, and eating no protein at the first meal even when she does eat, creates a predictable morning blood sugar pattern: cortisol rises sharply to compensate for the overnight fast with no food anchor, insulin eventually spikes when carbs arrive later, and androgen activity at the sebaceous glands responds to the insulin elevation — contributing directly to the acne cycle. This pattern is not a character flaw; it is a physiology sequence that repeats daily. The fix is simple and fast-acting: a protein-anchored first meal within 45 minutes of waking. Grace's supplement stack is robust — but supplements cannot intercept an insulin/androgen spike that a protein-forward breakfast could prevent upstream.
Hydration and Circulation
At 71.7kg, the minimum effective daily water intake is approximately 2L. Grace is at 1-1.5L consistently. Body water at 50.1% confirms this is a chronic pattern, not a single-day measurement. Low hydration concentrates the blood, which amplifies the subjective experience of anemia (fatigue, occasional dizziness), contributes to the occasional cold sweaty hands and heart palpitations (vasomotor instability in mild dehydration), and reduces the cellular environment in which her supplements are absorbed and transported. This is the highest-leverage lifestyle change she can make with zero financial cost.
Diet Quality vs. Supplement Stack
Grace is taking one of the most comprehensive supplement stacks seen in this assessment group — women's multi, B, C, magnesium, probiotic, fiber, GI primer, liver support, rhodiola, and lions mane, all daily. This is impressive consistency and reflects genuine commitment. The remaining symptoms exist not because the supplements are insufficient, but because the diet is presenting headwinds they cannot fully overcome: 0-1 servings of produce per day means the antioxidant and phytonutrient load from food is near zero; the mostly refined carb pattern creates repeated insulin spikes that override what the supplements are trying to do downstream. The opportunity is not to add more supplements — it is to reinforce the food foundation so the stack can work as intended.
Summary
Grace is already doing more than most — a comprehensive daily supplement stack taken consistently is a significant foundation. The signals that remain — persistent acne, heavy menstrual flow, occasional anemia, and low body water — point to two specific gaps the stack doesn't currently cover: omega-3 (the prostaglandin driver behind the skin and menstrual pattern) and the morning protein anchor that would interrupt the daily insulin/androgen cycle. Solving the breakfast skip and hydration habits, with Advanced Omega added to the stack, addresses the root causes behind the most visible and most felt symptoms simultaneously.
