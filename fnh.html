The four groups

How it works. Four categories, five squares each. On 100, 300 and 500 anyone can raise a hand, first hand answers, points go to your group. You get 20 seconds once you're called on, and get it wrong and your group loses the square — it goes to the floor and the other groups fight over it. So put your hand up when you know it, not when you hope. 800 and 1000 are for 3/6 and above, where somebody stands up and delivers rather than answers.

The rules, as the host says them

  1. Open the square. On 100 to 500 the clock starts by itself. Hands up — on Zoom the raise-hand list is already in order, in the room Johnny calls who was first.
  2. Twenty seconds. Wrong or out of time, hit Restart and it goes to the floor. That is why nobody switches off between turns.
  3. Reveal, then tap the group on the card. The score lands and the square goes dark. Tap the same group again to undo it, a different one to move it.
  4. Every card carries a line of context under the answer. Read it if it's worth reading and move on. On the open rows the point is pace, not teaching.
  5. 800 and 1000 are not a race. The group's own 3/6 takes their square and delivers it. Deliver it and the points are theirs. That is where the teaching is.
No buzzers. Buzz-in across Zoom and a room is decided by latency, not knowledge. Zoom's raise-hand list orders itself and cannot be argued with. The room is the half that needs a judge, which is why Johnny sits where he can see every hand.
